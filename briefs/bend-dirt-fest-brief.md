# Bend Dirt Fest - Synthesis Brief

## Core Identity

Bend Dirt Fest is a one-day gravel, mountain-bike, and trail-running festival based at the Athletic Club of Bend in Bend, Oregon. The organizer-confirmed 2027 gravel long course is 54.7 miles with 4,016 feet of climbing and 62% unpaved surface. The long gravel race is the Oregon Triple Crown Series final.

The race already had a source profile, page, and seven-plan TrainingPeaks fleet in Gravel God, but the fleet was built from the older Punchy fact base and retained incorrect or stale details. This brief documents the 2027 source refresh and clears the existing fleet for receipt-backed repair rather than claiming a brand-new source identity or a missing ladder.

## Source Resolution

- The 2027 technical guide is the controlling source for the course: 88 km / 54.7 miles and 1,224 m / 4,016 feet.
- The organizer-linked 2027 RideWithGPS route independently reports 54.68 miles, 4,014.7 feet, and 62% unpaved surface.
- The organizer page still contains an older 49-mile sentence and BikeReg rounds the long course to 55 miles. Those are not used over the guide and GPX-equivalent route record.
- The current organizer schedule and technical guide set the bike mass start at 8:15 AM. BikeReg still displays 8:00 AM; the event-specific guide controls.
- Volunteer-role pages in the guide retain 2026 weekday/date labels. They are not used for the 2027 race date, which the guide cover, organizer page, and registration all confirm as Saturday, July 10, 2027.

## Verified 2027 Facts

- **Date:** Saturday, July 10, 2027
- **Venue:** Athletic Club of Bend, 61615 Athletic Club Drive
- **Long course:** 54.7 miles / 4,016 feet / 62% unpaved
- **Short course:** 38.7 miles / 2,880 feet / 45% unpaved
- **Start:** 8:15 AM mass start with a neutral, flagger-controlled rollout
- **Course support:** Hydration-only stop near mile 21.5 and full aid near mile 27.7; Hammer Nutrition products and food
- **Cutoffs:** Mile 23 by 11:00 AM and mile 28 by 11:30 AM; published minimum pace 8.6 mph
- **Registration:** Starts at $109; solo-gravel field cap 300; total festival cap 500
- **Packet pickup:** Friday 2:00-6:00 PM at Pine Mountain Sports; Saturday from 6:00 AM until 30 minutes before the start
- **Parking:** OSU-Cascades; no participant parking at the Athletic Club of Bend

## Course Character

The route leaves Bend on pavement, turns onto Forest Road 41, and climbs from roughly 3,700 feet to a 5,608-foot high point near mile 24. The organizer-linked route flags a loose descent near mile 35, chucker holes and other loose hazards near mile 40, traffic-control interactions near mile 49, and a required slow/dismount crossing on the return. The result is a climbing-oriented mixed-surface course rather than a smooth-road fondo.

## Race History and Field

Source Weekly documented the 2025 race as the inaugural Bend Dirt Fest and described it as the successor to Mudslinger Events' Oregon 24 relay. Official 2026 OBRA results list 121 gravel entries across the long and short categories, including 83 on the long course and 38 on the short course. The 2027 race is the third edition and the Oregon Triple Crown Series final.

## Climate and Logistics

NOAA's 1991-2020 Bend normals put July at an 83.8°F average daily maximum and 48.6°F average daily minimum. The course guide emphasizes sun, dehydration, dust, and no outside support. Redmond Municipal Airport is 15 miles north of Bend, and the organizer lists Bend as roughly three hours from Portland, five hours from Boise, and 5.5 hours from Seattle.

## Scoring Rationale

- **Logistics (3):** Bend has a regional airport, plentiful lodging, a single venue, and designated parking, but destination travel and off-site parking require planning.
- **Length (2):** The 54.7-mile long course falls squarely in the 40-60-mile rubric.
- **Technicality (3):** Mostly unpaved forest roads, loose descents, chucker holes, surface transitions, and a dismount crossing create mixed-terrain handling demands.
- **Elevation (3):** 4,016 feet is just inside the 4,000-6,000-foot band.
- **Climate (3):** July heat, full-sun exposure, dust, and dehydration risk make conditions moderately challenging.
- **Altitude (3):** The course starts near 3,700 feet and tops out at 5,608 feet.
- **Adventure (3):** Central Oregon forest roads, scenic climbing, and the multisport format make the day memorable without making it a bucket-list expedition.
- **Prestige (3):** Oregon Triple Crown final status creates a real regional reputation; the event is too young for national significance.
- **Race Quality (3):** Detailed guide, flaggers, multiple timing points, medical support, stocked aid, live results, and structured awards indicate good organization.
- **Experience (3):** Live music, vendors, beer, age-group awards, and the team format create an enjoyable festival day.
- **Community (3):** Volunteer credits, local partners, COTA support, and the three-discipline team competition create a good local vibe.
- **Field Depth (2):** The 2026 gravel results show real competition but only 121 riders and no sustained national professional field.
- **Value (3):** $109 is fair for timing, aid, participant gear, awards, medical support, and the post-race festival.
- **Expenses (3):** A destination weekend in Bend generally fits the $500-$1,000 travel band.
- **Cultural Impact (1):** The event is emerging and regionally meaningful, with little media footprint outside Oregon cycling.

**Overall:** round((40 base + 1 cultural impact) / 70 × 100) = **59/100, Tier 3**.
