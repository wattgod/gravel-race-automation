# Proof, Reviews & Testimonials: Research Brief for Gravel God

> Research appendix to `receipts-social-proof-2026.md`. Compiled 2026-09-28 by a Claude research agent; web sources fetched that day. A reviewer (Fable 5.1) re-verified the FTC Q&A wording, 465.2, the $53,088 figure, the Dec 2025 warning letters and Canada s.74.02. **FR notice 2026-18853 and OMB M-26-11 are unverified by the reviewer** (bot wall); the penalty figure stands on the 2025 FTC release either way.


Prepared 2026-09-28. This is research, not legal advice. Anything that touches the removal of past testimonials or paid ads that used them is worth a one-hour call with an advertising lawyer.

Evidence labels used below: **[LAW]** statute, regulation or regulator guidance · **[PEER]** peer-reviewed research · **[PLATFORM]** the platform's own docs or code · **[MEASURED]** I fetched and counted it myself on 2026-09-28 · **[VENDOR]** a survey or report from a company that sells reviews, video or CRO services · **[PRACTITIONER]** expert opinion without a controlled study.

## 0. Headline findings

1. **The ~50 fabricated testimonials are the single biggest risk.** A business that writes testimonials attributed to people who don't exist, or who never had the experience, violates 16 CFR 465.2(a). Showing them on your own site counts as "disseminating" them, not "hosting" them. The civil penalty ceiling is **$53,088 per violation** ([FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers); [465.2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.2); [2025 penalty levels](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/02/ftc-publishes-inflation-adjusted-civil-penalty-amounts-2025), held flat for 2026 per [FR notice 2026-18853](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/15/2026-18853/civil-penalty-inflation-adjustments)).
2. **The prospect's complaint is backed by research.** NN/g's credibility research names "connected to the rest of the web" as one of four trust factors. It warns that a site that "cannot be found on third-party review sites… appears to either have something to hide" ([NN/g, Harley 2016](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)). Baymard found that users distrust curated on-site testimonials and try to click through to the source ([Baymard 2023](https://baymard.com/blog/tours-and-experiences-reviews)).
3. **Gravel God already has real third-party ratings, but TrainingPeaks hides nearly all of them [MEASURED].** The coach profile lists 1,957 plans. Only 9 plans have any rating: 15 ratings in total, weighted average 4.8/5. TP's plan-page script shows stars only when `ratingCount > 2`, so just 2 plans display a rating at all: Unbound 200 (4.5 from 4 ratings) and BWR California (5.0 from 3). The coach profile has no reviews section ([profile](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach/gravelgodcycling)).
4. **The first few real reviews do most of the work.** In the best-known study, most of the conversion gain came within the first 5–10 reviews. Higher-priced items needed at least five ([Spiegel/Northwestern 2017](https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/how-online-reviews-influence-sales/), retail data, see caveats). Five named, specific, linkable athlete stories would change the picture.
5. **The strongest competitors show proof you can check.** That means named results by race and placing, case studies with numbers, and a guarantee. None of the eight I checked links each result to an official results page, and none publishes what athletes generally achieve. That is the opening for a brand built on honesty.

## 1. Legal

### 1.1 FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule: 16 CFR Part 465 [LAW]

Finalized on 14 Aug 2024 by a 5-0 vote. Effective **21 Oct 2024** ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials); [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/08/22/2024-18519/trade-regulation-rule-on-the-use-of-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials)). Sections are listed at [LII Part 465](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/part-465).

| Section | Prohibits | Relevance to GG |
|---|---|---|
| 465.2(a) | A business must not **write or create** a review or testimonial that materially misrepresents that the reviewer **exists**, or that they **had actual experience** with the product, or what that experience was ([text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.2)). The FTC's announcement names AI-generated fake reviews ([press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)). | Applies directly to the ~50 fabricated testimonials. |
| 465.2(b) | A business must not **disseminate** a testimonial it **knew or should have known** was fake ([text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.2)). The FTC says: "A business that puts testimonials on its own website is disseminating them and is not merely 'hosting' them" ([Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)). | Each day they stay up is continued dissemination (my inference from the rule's wording). |
| 465.4 | No compensation or incentive "conditioned expressly or by implication" on a review expressing **a particular sentiment** ([text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.4)). Incentives that aren't tied to sentiment aren't banned by the rule, but must be disclosed under the Guides ([Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)). | "Free month for a 5-star review" is out. |
| 465.5 | Insider reviews (officers, managers, employees, agents, relatives) need a clear disclosure of the connection ([part list](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/part-465); [press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)). | Matt's partner, family or co-founders reviewing need a disclosure. |
| 465.6 | No review site that looks independent but is company-controlled ([press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)). | Don't build a "best gravel coaches" review page. |
| 465.7 | No groundless legal threats or intimidation to stop reviews. No misrepresenting that the reviews shown are "most or all" of those submitted when negatives were removed. Removal is allowed for defamatory, abusive, unrelated or fake content ([text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.7)). | Never say "all our reviews" while curating. |
| 465.8 | No buying or selling fake followers or views for commercial purposes ([press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)). | n/a |

- **Penalties.** Up to $51,744 per violation at adoption. That rose to **$53,088** from 17 Jan 2025 ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/02/ftc-publishes-inflation-adjusted-civil-penalty-amounts-2025)). The 2026 inflation adjustment was cancelled under OMB M-26-11, so the 2025 levels still apply ([FR 2026-18853](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/15/2026-18853/civil-penalty-inflation-adjustments); [OMB M-26-11](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/M-26-11-Cancellation-of-Penalty-Inflation-Adjustments-for-2026-Regarding-the-Federal-Civil-Penalties-Inflation-Adjustment-Act-Improvements-Act-of-2015.pdf)). Courts can also order consumer redress ([Goodwin summary](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/09/alerts-practices-cldr-ftc-finalizes-rule-on-consumer-reviews)).
- **Enforcement is live.** On 22 Dec 2025 the FTC sent the first warning letters under the rule to 10 companies it didn't name, demanding written confirmation of compliance ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/ftc-warns-10-companies-about-possible-violations-agencys-new-consumer-review-rule); [FTC blog](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/12/warning-letter-or-ten-businesses-comply-ftcs-consumer-review-rule)).
- **No small-business exemption** is mentioned anywhere in the rule or the FTC Q&A ([Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)).

### 1.2 FTC Endorsement Guides: 16 CFR Part 255, revised June 2023 [LAW]

The 2023 revision added a definition of "clear and conspicuous", covered incentivized and employee reviews, and set a new principle against procuring, suppressing, organizing or editing reviews in ways that distort what consumers think ([FTC 2023](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/06/federal-trade-commission-announces-updated-advertising-guides-combat-deceptive-reviews-endorsements)).

- **Real opinion from a real user.** Endorsements must reflect the endorser's honest opinion. Where the ad says the endorser uses the product, they must have been a bona fide user ([255.1(a),(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.1)). If you present people as "actual consumers", they must be actual consumers ([255.2(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.2)). A "composite" athlete is not an actual consumer.
- **Editing quotes.** A quote doesn't have to use the exact words "unless the advertisement represents that it is presenting the endorser's exact words". It "may not be presented out of context or reworded so as to distort" what the endorser said. The advertiser must also have "good reason to believe that the endorser continues to subscribe" to the views, so re-confirm old quotes ([255.1(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.1)). Practical rule: quotation marks mean the athlete's own words, trimmed only with the athlete's approval.
- **Performance results.** A testimonial about a key attribute implies the result is typical. If you can't show it's representative, you must "clearly and conspicuously disclose the generally expected performance" ([255.2(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.2)). The old "results not typical" safe harbor was removed in 2009 because consumers ignore that disclaimer ([FTC 2009](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2009/10/ftc-publishes-final-guides-governing-endorsements-testimonials); [FR notice](https://www.ftc.gov/sites/default/files/documents/federal_register_notices/guides-concerning-use-endorsements-and-testimonials-advertising-16-cfr-part-255/091015guidesconcerningtestimonials.pdf)). "+0.9 W/kg" next to a quote therefore needs either proof that it's typical or a statement of what athletes generally achieve.
- **Material connections.** Payments, **free or discounted products or services**, and family, business or personal relationships must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously if the audience wouldn't expect them ([255.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.5); [FTC FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides)). A comped or discounted coaching athlete, a sponsored rider, a friend or a family member all need a tag such as "coached at no cost".
- **Clear and conspicuous** means "difficult to miss… and easily understandable". In video the disclosure should appear on screen and be spoken. On social media it must be "unavoidable" ([255.0(f)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.0)).
- **Review procurement and suppression.** "Don't ask for reviews only from customers you think will leave positive ones" ([FTC soliciting guide, 2022](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/soliciting-paying-online-reviews-guide-marketers)). Advertisers "should not take actions that have the effect of distorting… what consumers think" ([255.2(d)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.2)). Choosing only positive quotes for marketing isn't banned by Part 465 itself. But "the use of non-representative consumer reviews in marketing could be deceptive" under Section 5 ([Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)).

### 1.3 The previously published fake testimonials: what to do

- **Take them down everywhere, now:** the site (the homepage and /products/training-plans/ both contain the string "testimonial" [MEASURED]), emails and autoresponders, PDFs and guides, social posts, ads, TP coach-profile or plan text, and any Stripe or checkout pages. I found no Review or AggregateRating schema on the three GG pages I checked [MEASURED], so there's nothing to remove from structured data there.
- **Don't replace them with composites, "representative" paraphrases, AI avatars, or real names attached to words those people never said.** Each of these fails 465.2 or 255.1–255.2 ([465.2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.2); [255.2(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.2)). The FTC does allow AI avatars in general, but not ones that imply a real person's testimony ([Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)).
- **An empty proof section is better than a fake one.** Keep a dated internal note of what was removed and when. If paid ads ran with the fake quotes, get counsel before posting any public statement about it.
- **The prospect.** Answer honestly without referring to the old quotes as real (see §7, recommendation 2).

### 1.4 Consent to use athlete names, photos, results and training data

- **Right of publicity (US).** Colorado recognizes the tort of appropriating someone's name or likeness for commercial benefit ([Joe Dickerson & Assocs. v. Dittmar, 34 P.3d 995 (Colo. 2001)](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2631844/joe-dickerson-associates-llc-v-dittmar/); [Rothman's Roadmap: Colorado](https://rightofpublicityroadmap.com/state_page/colorado/)). A written release is the norm for commercial use. Parents or guardians must sign for anyone under 18 ([Stanford Copyright & Fair Use](https://fairuse.stanford.edu/overview/releases/release-agreements/)).
- **Photos have two owners.** The athlete's release doesn't license the photographer's copyright. For example, MarathonFoto's terms bar any commercial use without written consent ([MarathonFoto ToU](https://www.marathonfoto.com/Home/TermsOfUse)). Use the athlete's own photos, or get a license from the photographer and credit them.
- **Health and performance data.** HR, HRV and body weight can be "health data":
  - *Washington My Health My Data Act:* covers "bodily functions, vital signs… measurements". It applies to businesses of any size that target Washington residents, and it requires opt-in consent to collect and **separate consent to share** ([WA AG](https://www.atg.wa.gov/protecting-washingtonians-personal-health-data-and-privacy); [IAPP](https://iapp.org/resources/article/washington-my-health-my-data-act-overview); [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/washington-my-health-my-data-act-guide/)).
  - *Colorado Privacy Act:* applies only at 100,000 Colorado consumers, or 25,000 plus revenue from selling data, so GG is almost certainly under it ([CO AG](https://coag.gov/resources/colorado-privacy-act/)).
  - *EU/UK athletes:* health data is GDPR Art. 9 special-category data and needs **explicit** consent, separate from general terms ([GDPR Local](https://gdprlocal.com/gdpr-for-wearable-technology/); [Legiscope](https://www.legiscope.com/blog/health-data-article-9-gdpr.html)).
  - *Canada (PIPEDA):* meaningful, preferably express, consent that states purposes, who receives the data, and risks. Consent can be withdrawn ([OPC guidelines](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/business-privacy/collecting-personal-information/consent/gl_omc_201805/)).
  - Practical line: publishing FTP or W/kg with a first name is low-sensitivity. HR, HRV, weight or medical context is high-sensitivity and needs its own tick-box.

### 1.5 International buyers (brief)

- **Canada.** Competition Act s.74.02: you may publish a testimonial only if the person previously published it, **or** you have their **written approval of the testimonial and permission to publish**. It must match what they said. Penalties reach C$10M for a corporation's first offence, or 3× the benefit ([Competition Bureau](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/use-tests-or-testimonials)). Written, approved consent satisfies this and the FTC at the same time.
- **EU.** UCPD Annex I points 23b and 23c (added by the Omnibus Directive 2019/2161) ban claiming reviews come from real users without "reasonable and proportionate steps" to check, and ban fake or misrepresented reviews in all circumstances. Art. 7(6) requires saying **whether and how** you verify reviews ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/2161/oj/eng); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/dodd-frank-consumer-protection-act/1189542/the-omnibus-directive-consumer-reviews)). One line such as "Every story here is from a coached athlete who approved it in writing" meets this.
- **UK.** The DMCC Act ban on fake and concealed-incentive reviews took effect **6 Apr 2025**. The CMA can fine up to 10% of global turnover ([CMA208 guidance](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67eeb64fe9c76fa33048c790/CMA208_-_Fake_reviews_guidance.pdf); [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-04-14-new-uk-consumer-law-regime-comes-into-force)).

## 2. What actually converts for high-consideration services

**Caveat first.** I found no controlled study of reviews for 1:1 coaching or consulting specifically. The evidence below is from e-commerce, travel and B2B. Treat it as directional.

### 2.1 Review volume, ratings and verification

- **Spiegel Research Center (Northwestern), 2017** [PEER-adjacent; data partner PowerReviews is a review vendor] ([summary](https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/how-online-reviews-influence-sales/); [PDF](https://irp-cdn.multiscreensite.com/00a8b24b/files/uploaded/Spiegel_Online%20Review_eBook_Jun2017_Pv2.pdf)).
  - **Findings:**
    - A product with 5 reviews had 270% higher purchase likelihood than one with none.
    - The lift was **380% for higher-priced items vs 190% for lower-priced** ones.
    - "Nearly all of the increase… occurs within the first 10 reviews, and the first five reviews drive the bulk."
    - "Higher-priced items need at least five reviews."
    - Purchase likelihood "typically peaks at ratings in the **4.0–4.7** range". Products rated 4.7–5.0 were less likely to be bought than those at 4.2–4.7, because perfect scores read as too good to be true.
    - A verified-buyer badge raised purchase likelihood 15%.
    - Reviews that were **prompted** (verified buyers asked by email) were *more* positive than unprompted anonymous ones. The report cites PowerReviews data that up to 80% of reviews come from follow-up emails.
  - **Caveats:** retail data only (two consumer-goods retailers plus a high-end gift retailer). "High-priced" means gifts, not $1,200 a month. The partner sells review software. The commonly quoted "4.2–4.5 sweet spot" is a secondary paraphrase; the primary source says 4.0–4.7. The underlying peer-reviewed paper is Maslowska, Malthouse & Viswanathan, *Decision Support Systems* 2017 ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167923617300593)).
- **Negative reviews add credibility.** 53% of Baymard test users sought out negative reviews. Negatives made ratings feel more authentic, and 37% counted a company's reply in its favour ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/respond-to-negative-user-reviews)) [PRACTITIONER-research, qualitative usability testing]. A 4.8 made of real, mixed reviews beats a wall of 5s.
- **Third-party and clickable beats on-site.** In Baymard's tours study, users said curated testimonials are "whatever nice things" the company picked. Third-party badges that weren't clickable backfired ("This is just like a screenshot"). Linking to the specific source page worked ([Baymard 2023](https://baymard.com/blog/tours-and-experiences-reviews)). NN/g: users "trust these external sources more than company-sponsored content" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)).

### 2.2 What makes a testimonial persuasive

- **Depth and specificity.** In 1,587 Amazon reviews, review depth predicted helpfulness, and for experience goods moderate reviews beat extreme ones ([Mudambi & Schuff, MISQ 2010](https://misq.umn.edu/misq/article/34/1/185/499/What-Makes-a-Helpful-Online-Review-A-Study-of)) [PEER]. Coaching is an experience good, so "Race, before, after, what changed" beats "Best coach ever!"
- **Identity cues matter, especially from similar or aspirational people.** Across three experiments, identity cues increased purchase intent for positive reviews. Cues from in-groups or aspirational groups helped; cues from dissociative groups hurt ([Frontiers in Psychology 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.784173/full)) [PEER]. For GG, a "45-year-old masters rider, 8 hrs a week" label is worth more than a pro's name.
- **Photos help recall.** CXL's eye-tracking study found testimonials with photos held attention longest and were the most memorable social-proof format ([CXL Institute](https://cxl.com/research-study/social-proof/)) [PRACTITIONER; small lab sample].
- **Two-sided messages.** A meta-analysis found the persuasive effect of admitting a downside depends on how much negative information there is and where it appears ([Eisend 2006, IJRM](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167811606000267)) [PEER]. A small downside after the positives can raise evaluations, but only when people aren't reading carefully ([Ein-Gar, Shiv & Tormala, JCR 2012](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/38/5/846/1796852)) [PEER]. Someone considering $1,200 a month *is* reading carefully, so use honesty for credibility, not as a trick.
- **Low numbers backfire.** Telling people a behaviour is common but undesirable ("many visitors take wood") doubled theft at Petrified Forest ([Cialdini 2003](https://www.academia.edu/25265171/Crafting_Normative_Messages_to_Protect_the_Environment_Social_Psychology)) [PEER]. Practitioners warn that small counts such as "3 athletes coached" signal unpopularity ([HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/negative-social-proof)) [PRACTITIONER]. Show quality with specifics, not headcounts.

### 2.3 Video, surveys, and "reference customers" (weak evidence, flagged)

- **Video.** Circulating claims like "viewers retain 95% of a video message vs 10% of text" have no traceable primary source; don't repeat them. The usable data is Wistia's platform data: videos under 1 minute averaged 52% engagement across 13M+ videos, and customer-testimonial videos 46% ([Wistia](https://wistia.com/learn/marketing/optimal-video-length)) [VENDOR].
- **Surveys.** BrightLocal 2025 (n=1,026 US adults, SurveyMonkey panel) [VENDOR] ([report](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2025/)):
  - 86% use Google to find reviews, and Trustpilot use is growing.
  - Detailed reviews with photos or video are increasingly valued.
  - 46% named AI-sounding text as the top sign of a fake review.
  - Email is the most effective way to ask (40%).
  TrustRadius 2024 found B2B buyers trust peers and user reviews over vendor sites, and 73% think they regularly or sometimes see fake reviews ([TrustRadius](https://solutions.trustradius.com/vendor-blog/2024-b2b-buying-disconnect-the-year-of-the-brand-crisis/)) [VENDOR]. Edelman 2024 found "someone like me" (74%) was trusted as much as scientists, but only for information about *innovation*. It's tangential here ([Edelman](https://www.edelman.com/news-awards/2024-edelman-trust-barometer)).
- **Reference calls.** I found no rigorous study. Buyer's guides now tell cyclists to ask coaches for "a case that includes the rider's starting ability, weekly hours, preparation period and result" and warn that "a podium photograph or a large FTP increase without that context tells you little" ([Roadman Cycling 2026](https://roadmancycling.com/blog/best-cycling-coach-usa)) [PRACTITIONER]. A reference call costs almost nothing and answers exactly the question the prospect asked.

## 3. Review platforms, so proof exists off-site

### 3.1 TrainingPeaks: where GG's buyers already are [MEASURED + PLATFORM]

- **What exists today.** The profile lists 1,957 plans: 1,009 at $99, 590 at $79 and 333 at $69. Only **9 plans have ratings: 15 ratings, weighted average 4.8** ([profile](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/coach/gravelgodcycling)).

  | Plan | Rating | Count |
  |---|---|---|
  | Unbound 200 | 4.5 | 4 |
  | BWR California | 5.0 | 3 |
  | Badass Build: Crit King | 5.0 | 2 |
  | Six other plans | 4.0–5.0 | 1 each |

- **Display threshold.** TP's plan-page bundle (`discover-details-bundle.js`) renders the stars component only `if (ratingCount > 2)`. Thirteen of the 15 ratings are therefore invisible. Plan data exposes only `averageRating` and `ratingCount`; I found no written-review text field. The coach profile has no reviews section, only a "Rating (Hi-Lo)" sort.
- **How ratings get collected.** Per a plan author's 2024 feature request, buyers get a review-request email **only on plan completion**, and coaches can't get a direct review link ([TP UserVoice](https://peaksware.uservoice.com/forums/106657-trainingpeaks-customer-feedback/suggestions/48694940-provide-coaches-authors-of-training-plans-with-a)). This isn't confirmed in official TP docs. I found no TP rule against coaches asking buyers to rate. FTC and Google-style rules still apply: ask every buyer, never only happy ones.
- **Limits.** Coaching clients aren't plan buyers, so TP holds no reviews for 1:1 coaching or custom plans. The profile headline reads "Road Cycling Coach" with location Longmont, CO; it should match the gravel brand and Boulder.

### 3.2 Google Business Profile [PLATFORM]

- **Eligibility.** "To qualify for a Business Profile, a business must make in-person contact with customers during its stated hours… online-only businesses" are **not eligible** ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036?hl=en)). Service-area businesses that travel to customers can list and hide a home address ([Google guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en)). The TP profile lists "Local Coaching" and "Group Coaching". **Only** if Matt runs real, recurring in-person sessions (rides, fits, consults) at stated hours is a GBP legitimate. Otherwise it risks suspension and is itself a misrepresentation.
- **Rules.** No incentives of any kind. Don't "selectively solicit positive reviews". No reviews from people with a conflict of interest ([Google Maps policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en)).
- **Stars in search.** Self-serving reviews can't produce review stars in search for LocalBusiness or Organization pages. That includes reviews shown through an embedded widget ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/review-snippet); [2019 change](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2019/09/making-review-rich-results-more-helpful)).

### 3.3 Trustpilot [PLATFORM]

- **Free plan.** 50 invitations a month, replies to reviews, 1 widget. Paid plans start at $99/month billed annually ([pricing](https://business.trustpilot.com/pricing)).
- **Rules.** "Invite consistently and fairly… inviting everyone in the same way", with no selective inviting and **no incentives** (discounts, prize entries, freebies). Owners can't review themselves ([guidelines](https://corporate.trustpilot.com/legal/for-businesses/guidelines-for-businesses)). Anyone can post, so negative reviews will appear, which by §2.1 is what makes the rating credible.
- **Fraud controls.** Trustpilot removed 4.5M fake reviews in 2024, 7.4% of submissions ([Trust Report 2025](https://corporate.trustpilot.com/press/news/trust-report-2025)). BrightLocal reports Trustpilot use rising ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2025/)).
- **Fit for GG.** It's location-agnostic, so it works for online coaching, custom plans and consults. The invite rules match the brand charter, and 50 invites a month is plenty.

### 3.4 Strava, Reddit and results databases

- **Strava** has no reviews feature. Public activities can be **embedded** on a web page, but only if both the profile and the activity are set to "Everyone" ([Strava help](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15402053-sharing-your-activities-and-routes-with-a-strava-embed)). A race-day activity with power data works as a receipt, with the athlete's consent.
- **Reddit.** Reputation there has to be earned through disclosed, useful participation. Reddit bans content and vote manipulation and coordinated inauthentic behaviour ([Reddit Help](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/360043066412-Disrupting-Communities)). Never seed threads.
- **Official results** are the cheapest third-party proof: [road-results.com](https://www.road-results.com/), [results.bikereg.com](https://results.bikereg.com/race/19224), and the USA Cycling member portal ([USAC help](https://usacyclinghelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/41839133020443-How-to-find-results-in-USA-Cycling-Member-Portal)). A link to the official result page is what Baymard's users kept trying to click.

### 3.5 Collection tools: Senja and Testimonial.to

| Tool | Free tier | Paid | Data portability | Page weight [MEASURED] |
|---|---|---|---|---|

### 3.6 Recommendation

1. **Trustpilot (free)** for coaching, custom-plan and consult clients. Invite everyone at fixed points and link the profile from the site.
2. **TrainingPeaks plan ratings** for plan buyers. Ask every buyer in the plan's final week, and push the 3–5 best-selling plans past TP's 3-rating display threshold.

GBP only if there is a real in-person offer. Skip Senja and Testimonial.to until video volume justifies them; collect with a plain form and store consent yourself.

## 5. Motion and data-viz as proof, not decoration

### 5.1 What research says about animation

- **Staged transitions help.** Carefully staged transitions between chart states "significantly improve graphical perception" ([Heer & Robertson, InfoVis 2007](https://idl.cs.washington.edu/files/2007-AnimatedTransitions-InfoVis.pdf)) [PEER].
- **Animating a trend is worst for analysis but enjoyable to watch.** Static small multiples were more accurate and static traces faster. Viewers found the animation "enjoyable and exciting" but made many errors ([Robertson et al., TVCG 2008](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/tvcg2008-trendvis.pdf)) [PEER].
- **The rule that follows:** animate to *reveal* in stages, then **end on a static, labelled chart** the reader can inspect. Never let the motion be the only way to read the data.
- **Truncated axes exaggerate differences, and warning cues don't fix it.** Y-axis truncation inflates perceived effect sizes, and the effect "is persistent… even for designs with explicit visual cues" ([Correll, Bertini & Franconeri, CHI 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.02035)) [PEER]. Start bar and W/kg axes at zero, or show the absolute change in numbers next to the chart.

### 5.2 Patterns that work as receipts

1. **Power-duration curve, before and after.** Two mean-maximal lines for the same athlete, drawn in sequence (before, then after), with the gap labelled in watts and %.
   - Label the date ranges and the power meter; flag any meter change.
   - Log-scale the duration axis and say so.
2. **Fitness build (CTL) drawing on scroll**, with race-day markers and the result at each. Label it as a **model**: CTL is an exponentially weighted average of daily TSS with a default 42-day time constant, derived from Banister's impulse-response model ([TrainingPeaks](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/learn/articles/the-science-of-the-performance-manager/)). Caption it "modelled fitness (CTL)", not "fitness".
3. **Race-result timeline.** Dots per race, placing on the y-axis, each dot linking to the official result page (§3.4).
4. **"Receipts" calendar scroll.** A screen recording of a real TP calendar week showing planned vs completed workouts and the coach's comments, used with the athlete's consent. Blur other athletes and private notes.
5. **Cohort distribution (the honesty chart).** One dot per coached athlete: all athletes coached ≥6 months, including anyone who got slower. Show n, median and range, and the dates. This is the "generally expected results" disclosure 255.2(b) asks for ([255.2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.2)), made into a chart.
6. **Vertical case-study videos, 15–45 s.** YouTube Shorts allow up to 3 min since 15 Oct 2024 ([YouTube](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/tall-updates-coming-to-shorts/)) and Instagram Reels up to 3 min since Jan 2025 ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/01/223-instagram-reels-3-minutes-us-tiktok-ban/)). Engagement data favours videos under a minute ([Wistia](https://wistia.com/learn/marketing/optimal-video-length)) [VENDOR]. Any material connection must be **on screen and spoken** ([255.0(f)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.0)).

**Honesty checklist for every chart:**
- data source (device, platform) and date range
- units on every axis
- n
- the smoothing window if smoothed (e.g., "7-day rolling")
- no easing curves that overshoot values
- the final frame matches the raw numbers
- one footnote: "Individual results; see the cohort chart for typical outcomes"

### 5.3 Technical practice

- **Reduced motion.** Honour `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` by showing the final static chart. MDN notes that scaling and panning large objects can trigger vestibular disorders, and the feature has been "widely available" since Jan 2020 ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion)). WCAG 2.2.2 (Level A) requires a way to pause auto-moving content that lasts more than 5 s, and that includes content triggered by scrolling it into view ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)). WCAG 2.3.3 (AAA) says motion triggered by interaction should be possible to disable ([W3C](https://w3c.github.io/wcag21/understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)).
- **Performance budgets.** Core Web Vitals "good" thresholds, measured at the 75th percentile: LCP ≤2.5 s, INP ≤200 ms, CLS ≤0.1 ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)).
  - Animate only `transform` and `opacity`. Composited animations don't cause layout shifts ([web.dev animations guide](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide); [Chrome Lighthouse](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/performance/non-composited-animations)).
  - Reserve chart and video dimensions so nothing shifts on load.
- **SVG/CSS vs Lottie vs video** [MEASURED sizes]:

  | Option | Library cost | What it is |
  |---|---|---|
  | Inline SVG + CSS/JS generated from real data | no library | The honest default: the chart *is* the data. |
  | `lottie-web` 5.13 | 306 KB min / **77 KB gzip** ([bundlephobia](https://bundlephobia.com/package/lottie-web)) | A drawing exported from After Effects |
  | `@lottiefiles/dotlottie-web` 0.80 | **33 KB gzip JS + a 1.24 MB WASM renderer (~498 KB gzip)** (npm tarball) | Same, in the dotLottie format |
  | Video | typically megabytes | Lazy-load it ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/lazy-loading-video)) |

  Lottie files are After Effects compositions exported to JSON via Bodymovin ([Lottie docs](https://airbnb.io/lottie/)), so a Lottie "chart" is a drawing, not data. Avoid Lottie for proof.
- **CSS scroll-driven animations** (`animation-timeline`) ship in Chrome and Edge 115+ (2023) and Safari/iOS 26 (Sept 2025). Firefox is not yet in stable, so the Baseline status is "limited availability… blocked since September 2025 by Firefox" ([web-features](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/scroll-driven-animations/); [caniuse](https://caniuse.com/mdn-css_properties_animation-timeline_scroll)). Use them as progressive enhancement inside `@supports (animation-timeline: scroll())`, with the static chart as the fallback.
- **Remotion (React to MP4).** Free, including commercial use, for individuals and companies with ≤3 employees ([LICENSE](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/LICENSE.md)). The same data file can produce the web SVG chart and a 1080×1920 Short, so both show identical numbers ([remotion.dev](https://www.remotion.dev/)).

## 6. Collection mechanics

### 6.1 Rules that keep it clean

- Ask **everyone**, at the **same trigger points**, whether or not they're happy ([FTC 2022](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/soliciting-paying-online-reviews-guide-marketers); [Trustpilot](https://corporate.trustpilot.com/legal/for-businesses/guidelines-for-businesses); [Google](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en)).
- **No incentives.** The FTC allows incentives that aren't tied to sentiment if disclosed ([465.4](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.4)), but Google and Trustpilot ban them outright. Offering none is the simplest policy that satisfies all three.
- Never ask employees, family or friends without a disclosure ([FTC 2022](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/soliciting-paying-online-reviews-guide-marketers)).

### 6.2 Timing (fixed triggers, applied to all)

1. **48–72 h after a target race**, whatever the result. That's when the details are fresh. (Practitioner judgment; I found no controlled study of timing.)
2. **End of each coaching block or renewal date.**
3. **Plan completion.** TP already emails buyers then ([UserVoice](https://peaksware.uservoice.com/forums/106657-trainingpeaks-customer-feedback/suggestions/48694940-provide-coaches-authors-of-training-plans-with-a)). Add a personal note in the final-week workout.
4. **On cancellation:** ask for a short exit note. It's valuable feedback, and asking only while people are happy would be selective solicitation.

### 6.3 Response-rate tactics that stay within FTC rules

- **Send it from Matt, personally, by email.** Email is the top-performing ask at 40% ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2025/)) [VENDOR]. Prompted buyers produce most reviews ([Spiegel PDF](https://irp-cdn.multiscreensite.com/00a8b24b/files/uploaded/Spiegel_Online%20Review_eBook_Jun2017_Pv2.pdf)). Buyers respond better to "a recognised address" ([UserVoice](https://peaksware.uservoice.com/forums/106657-trainingpeaks-customer-feedback/suggestions/48694940-provide-coaches-authors-of-training-plans-with-a)).
- **Pre-fill the facts.** Send the race, date, placing, FTP before and after, and hours per week, and ask the athlete to confirm or correct them. Their *opinion* stays theirs.
- **Offer a 10-minute call instead of writing.** Transcribe it and send the athlete the exact excerpt to approve. That satisfies FTC 255.1(b) ([255.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.1)) and Canada's written-approval rule ([s.74.02](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/types-deceptive-marketing-practices/use-tests-or-testimonials)).
- **One reminder after 7 days, then stop.** Make "no thanks" a one-click answer.

### 6.4 Question prompts

Adapted from D'Souza's six testimonial questions [PRACTITIONER] ([Psychotactics](https://www.psychotactics.com/six-questions-testimonials/)), plus prompts for before/after and context:

1. What almost stopped you from hiring a coach, or from choosing this one? (the objection)
2. Where were you when you started? (hours per week, FTP or W/kg, a race result, what kept going wrong)
3. What changed, in numbers, and at which race? (we'll link the official result)
4. What was one specific thing in the training or feedback that made the difference?
5. What was hard or didn't work? (optional, and we publish it if you want)
6. Who would you *not* recommend this to?
7. Anything else, in your own words?

Questions 5 and 6 produce the two-sided detail that §2 says builds credibility. They also filter out prospects who wouldn't fit.

### 6.5 Consent form essentials (tick-box tiers)

- **Identity level:** full name / first name + last initial / first name + age group / anonymous.
- **Each asset gets its own tick:** quote · photo (who owns the copyright, with credit) · race results with links · power and FTP numbers · **health data (HR, HRV, weight): a separate explicit tick** (MHMDA, GDPR Art. 9) · video/voice · Strava embed.
- **Where it can be used:** website · TP profile or plans · email · social · paid ads. **Duration:** e.g., 3 years, renewable.
- **The athlete approves the final text and edits** (255.1(b), s.74.02).
- **Material connection:** declare any comped or discounted coaching, sponsorship, or friend/family relationship. It will be shown with the quote (255.5).
- **Withdrawal:** by email at any time; content comes down within 7 days (PIPEDA/GDPR).
- **Minors** need a parent or guardian signature ([Stanford](https://fairuse.stanford.edu/overview/releases/release-agreements/)).
- **Keep records:** the original words, the approved version, the date, and the consent record. They also substantiate the claims (255.1(d): the advertiser is liable).
