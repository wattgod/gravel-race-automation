# BEAR 100 — COMMUNITY RESEARCH

## Scope Correction

This file covers the Bear 100 gravel bike race in Laona, Wisconsin. It does not cover the Bear 100 ultramarathon in Utah or the separate Hungry Bear 100 gravel event in Cable, Wisconsin.

## Rider Quotes & Race Reports

**Logan [COMPETITIVE]:** Called the hard-packed forest roads a "very cool atmosphere and cool setting for a gravel race here in northern Wisconsin." He raced the 31-mile Honey Bear in 2025, reported roughly 140 riders in that field, finished second overall and first in his division, and said the wet roads felt like riding on Velcro. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nQczFit1tGU)

**Logan [COMPETITIVE]:** Described rain-softened roads, potholes, puddles, pine and birch forest, and a wet Rat River Trail. He praised the organization, food, door prizes, and race director Brent, and recommended both the Honey Bear and long Bear routes. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nQczFit1tGU)

**Adam [RECREATIONAL]:** Called the long event "a tough race" and a useful early-season test after riding it three times in five years. His 2025 account describes sustained mud, soft two-track, back pain, and the value of riding with other racers when the day became difficult. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7x7v5U5sWYE)

**Adam [RECREATIONAL]:** Reported that organizers removed a deeply muddy ATV section after heavy rain because it was unsafe. He approved of the decision and described the remaining course as physically and mentally demanding. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7x7v5U5sWYE)

**Tyson Schwiesow [COMPETITIVE]:** Published race footage from the 73-mile version through Nicolet National Forest and identified the event as a fundraiser for the Laona Fire Department. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LzLgSIIe7qk)

## Course Specifications & Logistics

- The official 2026 event offered 31-, 61-, and 103-mile timed races from Laona, with long-route starts at 7:00 AM and shorter fields starting later. (https://www.thebear100.com/routes)
- The 61- and 103-mile routes included about three miles of easy Strong Falls singletrack in addition to gravel, forest roads, and rougher two-track. (https://www.thebear100.com/routes)
- BikeReg listed a 550-rider event limit. The 2026 fee was $59 for 61 or 103 miles, $49 for 31 miles, and free for riders 16 and under. (https://www.bikereg.com/the-bear-100)
- Performance Timing published 2026 results, confirming the race used chip timing and competitive categories. (https://www.performancetiming.com/2026/05/the-bear-100-gravel-bike-race/)
- Green Bay is the closest practical commercial-airport approach. Laona has limited lodging and dining, so racers should arrange both early.

## Terrain Details (Rider Perspective)

- Logan described hard-packed dirt roads under thick forest cover, with short paved connectors and a flat opening trail through town. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nQczFit1tGU)
- After rain, he encountered sticky surfaces, potholes, puddles, downed sticks, and soft mud on the Rat River Trail. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nQczFit1tGU)
- Adam's long-route account describes extended muddy climbing and soft two-track that increased muscular fatigue well before the finish. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7x7v5U5sWYE)
- The official route materials place the longer races deep in Nicolet National Forest and include Strong Falls singletrack. (https://www.thebear100.com/routes)

## Weather Experienced

- The 2025 Honey Bear followed substantial rain. Logan described wet, sticky roads and standing water; Adam described cold at the start and extensive mud on the longer course. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nQczFit1tGU) (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7x7v5U5sWYE)
- Early May in northern Wisconsin can change quickly. Racers should prepare for a cold start, rain, and soft forest roads rather than assume dry summer conditions.

## Race Strategy & Pacing

- Logan's 31-mile field rolled out around 15–16 mph before the front riders increased the pace. He and eventual winner David worked together before the Rat River Trail decided the race. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nQczFit1tGU)
- Adam deliberately avoided the fastest group and managed the long route by focusing on the next navigation cue instead of total distance. Riding with Reed, Dave, Tanya, Ferez, and Dylan helped him continue through mud and pain. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7x7v5U5sWYE)
- For the 103-mile race, self-sufficient pacing matters because official support is limited and the Laona Rescue Squad ended course support at 7:00 PM in 2026. (https://www.thebear100.com/faqs-rules)

## Equipment & Gear Recommendations

- The official rules tell riders to carry their own food, repair supplies, and other essentials; water is the primary on-course provision. (https://www.thebear100.com/faqs-rules)
- Spring mud and rougher two-track favor durable gravel tires with real tread and enough width for wet forest roads.
- Riders should carry layers and rain protection for a cold or wet Northwoods start, plus navigation and repair capacity for remote sections.

## Community Feel & Atmosphere

- The event has run since 2013 and directs all proceeds to the Laona Rescue Squad for equipment, supplies, and training. (https://www.thebear100.com/)
- Logan highlighted good food, door prizes, organization, and the race's small-town setting. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nQczFit1tGU)
- Multiple distances, free youth entry, and a 550-rider cap make the event accessible while keeping timed category racing intact. (https://www.bikereg.com/the-bear-100)
- Nicolet National Forest, the Laona Rescue Squad mission, and the Strong Falls section give the race a specific regional identity.
