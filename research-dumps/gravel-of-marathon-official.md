# The Gravel of Marathon — official sources check

Reviewed: 2026-11-17

Built from pages fetched on the review date. Each fact below is stated on at least one listed source.

## Publishable facts

- The Gravel of Marathon, on Sunday 15 November 2026 in Marathon, Attica, is the first UCI Gravel World Series race in Greece, organized by the MBike team.
- The organizer site says it is the first race of the series in Greece and qualifies riders for the 2027 World Championships.
- The long route qualifies elite men and women, men 19-59 and women 19-49; the short route qualifies men 60+ and women 50+. Both start at the Marathon Stadium.
- The UCI race preview gives the main course as 96 km with 1,320 m, approximately 75 percent off-road, and about 250 riders from 23 countries registered; the race report says the race covered 95 km, won by Lukas Malezsewski and Lejla Njemcevic.
- The organizer's route page gives the long route as 95.9 km with 1,520 m and the short route as 65.6 km with 1,130 m, both 75 percent on dirt. Both routes start at Marathon Stadium, cross town, climb 2 km on rocky ground at 6 percent average (9 percent max), pass Grammatiko, run 5 km of asphalt, cross a shallow stream and climb a hard 500 m to the start of a 31 km loop.
- The 31 km loop: 16 km of farm road alternating dirt and asphalt to the Lake Marathon entrance (second feed station), 4 km of soft dirt beside the lake, a farm road past the greenhouses, a second shallow stream, a 200 m soft-dirt climb reaching 12 percent, and 6 km of dirt back to the loop start. The long route rides the loop twice and the short route once; both then retrace the approach through Grammatiko and Ano Souli to finish in central Marathon.
- The organizer's 95 km GPX (Komoot export, 2026-10-12) measures 95.8 km and passes each point on the loop twice: the loop runs from about km 17 to km 48 and again to km 79, the return climbs to about 380 m near km 84 before descending to Marathon. The 65 km GPX passes the loop once.

## Conflicts and unknowns

- The current UCI page gives 96 km / 1,320 m (long) and 65 km / 1,150 m (short), both roughly 75 percent off-road; the same page also mentions registration packages for 121 km and 86 km routes, and the organizer site shows 95 km and 65 km. An earlier 121 km / 2,020 m / 90 percent course description was superseded. The organizer's route page gives 95.9 km / 1,520 m and 65.6 km / 1,130 m, matching its own GPX files; the profile uses the organizer's figures. Both climbing figures sit in the same 4,000-6,000 ft scoring band.
- The UCI page's course text says the 31 km circuit is repeated "twice more" on the long route; the organizer's short-route text and both GPX files show two laps (long) and one lap (short). Three laps plus the approach and return would be about 127 km.
