# HIBERNATOR 100 — COMMUNITY RESEARCH AND OFFICIAL SOURCE ADJUDICATION

## Identity

Hibernator 100 is the fall gravel event in Laona, Wisconsin, organized with the Laona Rescue Squad. It is distinct from the spring Bear 100, the Vermont Hibernator gravel weekend, and the Hathersage Hibernator running event.

## 2026 event

- Date: October 3, 2026.
- Location: 4863 Elm St, Laona, Wisconsin.
- Routes: 31, 61, or 103 miles.
- Start times: 8:00 AM CT for 61 and 103 miles; 9:00 AM CT for 31 miles.
- Entry: $39 for every route; registration cap 250.
- The 61- and 103-mile routes each include three miles of easy singletrack on the Strong Falls Trail system.
- The organizer-linked 103-mile RideWithGPS route records 165.379 km and 1,035.75 m of climbing, or approximately 102.8 miles and 3,398 feet.

## Competition eligibility

The organizer's rules are explicit: "This is not a race." The event is not chip timed, finish times are recorded manually, e-bikes are welcome, and there are no prizes. Hibernator is therefore an active gravel event but is not eligible for a race-specific TrainingPeaks plan fleet.

This official classification controls even though riders may informally compare finish times or describe riding hard at the front. An individual rider's competitive intent does not convert an untimed, prize-free, e-bike-inclusive event into a race. That distinction is the reason the source profile remains useful while the commercial race-plan record must be removed.

## Support model

This is a limited-support, self-sufficient event. Laona Rescue provides emergency route support until 5:00 PM. The organizer supplies route arrows, maps, cue sheets, GPX files, and neutral water stops, but riders must bring their own food and a backup navigation and rescue plan.

## History and community

The fall Hibernator began in 2014 after the inaugural Bear 100 introduced riders to the area's gravel. One hundred percent of event proceeds support Laona Rescue Squad equipment, supplies, and training.

The organizer describes Laona as Wisconsin's unofficial capital of gravel road cycling and frames the weekend around gathering, riding, and celebrating. The community proposition is unusually direct: registration revenue funds the rescue squad that supports the routes and serves the surrounding communities year-round.
