The Ratings

Course Profile
Logistics 4/5

Adelaide Airport (ADL) handles direct flights from all Australian state capitals, plus connections through Sydney and Melbourne to international hubs. The airport is 8 km from the city centre — a 15-minute taxi ride. Adelaide's compact grid layout and efficient public transport make getting around straightforward. The 136 km start at Henley Beach is accessible by tram and car from the CBD. For the 116 km and 63 km distances, shuttle bus options are available from the finish at Nairne back to Flagstaff Hill and Meadows respectively. The event operates under the Santos Tour Down Under umbrella — Events South Australia and NX Sports have been running TDU-associated rides for over two decades. Road closures are coordinated with SA Police, rest stops are professionally stocked, and the entire infrastructure of a UCI WorldTour race stage supports the community ride.

Riding the actual WorldTour stage route on the same day as the pros is a privilege that very few community rides worldwide can offer. The event has operated continuously for over 20 years under various names — the Bupa Challenge Tour (2003-2017), and now the Adelaide Epic Ride (from 2025). André Greipel serves as a 2026 ambassador. Eddy Merckx has ridden the community ride. The prestige comes through association with the professional race rather than from the community ride itself, but that association is genuine and deeply embedded.

Facts & History

The community ride component of the Santos Tour Down Under began in 2003 as the Bupa Challenge Tour, sponsored by private healthcare provider Bupa. The concept was simple and compelling: give amateur cyclists the chance to ride the same stage route as the UCI WorldTour professionals, on the same day, just hours before the peloton rolled through. Bupa's sponsorship ran until 2017, during which time the ride became a fixture of the Tour Down Under festival, regularly attracting 5,000-7,000+ riders across multiple distances. The ride covered various stage routes over the years — through the Barossa Valley to Tanunda, from Norwood through the Adelaide Hills, and across South Australia's diverse cycling terrain. Following the cancellation of TDU during 2020-2022 due to COVID-19 and subsequent restructuring, the community ride was relaunched in 2025 as the Adelaide Epic Ride for the 25th anniversary edition of the Tour Down Under. NX Sports now manages the event as a third-party associated event. The rebrand brought a fresh identity while preserving the core proposition: ride a WorldTour stage, earn your bragging rights, and be part of Australia's biggest professional cycling event. Founded in 2003. By Events South Australia (as part of Santos Tour Down Under).

Reputation: The community ride of Australia's biggest professional cycling race. Over 20 years of operation under two names — the Bupa Challenge Tour and the Adelaide Epic Ride — have embedded this event in South Australian cycling culture. The proposition is unique and enduring: ride a WorldTour stage route on race day, in the January heat, and earn your place in the Tour Down Under story.

2003: Inaugural community ride as part of the Santos Tour Down Under, sponsored by Bupa as the 'Bupa Challenge Tour'
2012: 10th edition of the Bupa Challenge Tour attracts 7,036 registered riders — 5,000 on the full 138 km distance from Norwood to Tanunda in the Barossa Valley
2013: Route from Modbury to Tanunda with four distance options (127 km, 92 km, 46.5 km, 20 km). Cancer Council SA partnership established for 'Ride for a reason' fundraising.
2014: Bupa renews Tour Down Under sponsorship for three additional years, extending Bupa Challenge Tour naming rights through 2017
Eddy Merckx and other cycling legends have participated in the community ride, adding to its prestige and cultural significance
2020-2022: Tour Down Under cancelled or significantly modified due to COVID-19 — community ride suspended
2025: Relaunched as the Adelaide Epic Ride for the 25th anniversary edition of TDU. Three distances: 120 km, 85 km, 20 km from Norwood to Uraidla.
2026: Route follows Ziptrak Stage 3 from Henley Beach to Nairne — 136 km, 116 km, and 63 km options. André Greipel serves as event ambassador.

The Course

A sealed-road journey from the Adelaide coast through the southern suburbs and into the Adelaide Hills, following the exact route of the UCI WorldTour Men's Stage 3. The 136 km route begins at Henley Beach at sea level and heads south and east through Adelaide's suburban fringe before the terrain tilts decisively upward into the Adelaide Hills. The character shifts dramatically across the ride: flat coastal kilometres give way to rolling suburban hills, which in turn yield to the punchy categorised climbs that define the stage. Chandlers Hill arrives first — a short, sharp shock to legs that have been spinning on flat road. Wickham Hill follows, a more sustained effort through Adelaide Hills bushland. The final act is Mount Barker Summit, arriving with less than 5 km to the finish in Nairne, when accumulated fatigue and January heat conspire to make every gradient feel twice as steep. The descents between climbs are fast and rewarding — wide, well-surfaced roads swept to professional racing standards.

Signature challenge: Mount Barker Summit with less than 5 km to the finish — the final categorised climb after 130+ km of riding and 1,500+ metres of accumulated climbing in January heat. Every pedal stroke on this climb is earned. The gradient is honest, the heat is real, and the finish at Nairne is close enough to be cruel.

MILE
The Coastal Flat
Rolling out of Henley Beach at 5:45 AM in pre-dawn summer light, five thousand riders filling the road as the Adelaide skyline wakes up to the east. The first 30 km are flat and fast along the coast and through Adelaide's southern suburbs — a chance to settle into rhythm, manage your effort, and absorb the scale of the event. The ocean breeze at your back is a gift you will remember fondly when the hills arrive. Use this section to eat, drink, and find a group. The climbing ahead will be easier in company.
20
MILE
Chandlers Hill
The first real test. Chandlers Hill rises through Adelaide's southern suburbs — a categorised climb with gradients that remind you this is a WorldTour stage, not a coastal cruise. The climb is short but punchy, and it arrives just as the terrain begins its decisive shift from flat suburban roads to rolling Adelaide Hills landscape. Pace yourself here. The temptation to attack the first climb is strong, but you have 100 km and two bigger climbs still ahead. Chandlers Hill is a gateway, not a destination.
45
MILE
The Adelaide Hills Rollers
Between the categorised climbs, the Adelaide Hills deliver relentless rolling terrain — short rises and fast descents through eucalyptus bushland, vineyards, and small towns. The accumulated vertical is deceptive. No single roller is hard, but the constant up-and-down drains your legs and your patience. The scenery compensates — the Adelaide Hills are genuinely beautiful, with long views across vineyards and bushland that make you forget you are 40 km from a capital city. The heat is building now. The morning cool is a memory. Drink consistently.
60
MILE
Wickham Hill
The second categorised climb and the longest sustained effort on the route. Wickham Hill demands honest climbing legs — the gradient is consistent rather than steep, but the length of the effort, combined with accumulated fatigue from the preceding 90+ km, makes this the ride's attritional centrepiece. By the time you reach the top, you have climbed over 1,200 metres and the January sun is directly overhead. The descent off the back is fast and welcome — tuck in, recover, and prepare mentally for the final test.
75
MILE
Mount Barker Summit
The final climb, arriving with less than 5 km to the finish in Nairne. Mount Barker Summit is where the Adelaide Epic earns its name. Your legs carry 120+ km of fatigue. The heat is at its peak. The gradient does not care about your excuses. This climb breaks the riders who have overcooked the earlier sections — pacing across the entire ride is vindicated or punished on Mount Barker Summit. The finish at Nairne is close enough to smell but far enough to hurt. Grind over the top, descend into Nairne, and cross the line knowing you have ridden the same roads the WorldTour pros will race in a few hours.

Race Logistics

Airport
Adelaide Airport (ADL) — direct flights from all Australian state capitals (Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth, Darwin, Hobart, Canberra). International connections primarily through Sydney and Melbourne. Airport is 8 km from the CBD, 15 minutes by taxi. Henley Beach start (136 km) is approximately 5 km from the airport.
Lodging
Adelaide CBD offers the best base — compact, walkable, and within easy reach of Henley Beach by car or tram. Hotels range from $120-250 AUD per night during TDU week. Glenelg and Henley Beach offer beachside accommodation close to the 136 km start. The Adelaide Hills (Hahndorf, Stirling, Mount Lofty) provide character stays but require a car. Book early — Tour Down Under week is Adelaide's peak tourism period and quality accommodation fills fast. Consider staying the full TDU week to watch professional racing stages and attend festival events.
Food
Adelaide's food and wine culture punches well above its weight. The Central Market is a pre-ride carb-loading destination. Rundle Street and Gouger Street offer everything from Asian to Italian to modern Australian. The Adelaide Hills are wine country — Hahndorf's German heritage restaurants and the Barossa Valley are within easy reach for rest days. Post-ride, Nairne's options are limited — most riders shuttle back to Adelaide for celebration meals. The Tour Village in Victoria Square is the social hub during TDU week.
Packet Pickup
Event information and jersey collection details published on adelaideepic.org. Check for pre-event pickup locations and times. Jerseys, timing tags, and rider information packs collected prior to event day. AusCycling membership verification required.
Parking
Henley Beach (136 km start) has limited street parking — arrive early or arrange drop-off. Flagstaff Hill (116 km start) and Meadows (63 km start) have designated parking areas. Shuttle bus services run from Nairne finish back to start locations — book separately through the event website. Public transport options available from Adelaide CBD to Henley Beach via tram.
RIDERS REPORT
Allow around seven hours for the full route. Stop at McLaren Vale for lunch - the Cottage Bakery is right next to the bike path and a popular refueling spot.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Adelaide Epic Ride (Santos Tour Down Under)?

+

Late January in Adelaide means summer heat, full stop. Average maximums sit around 29-32C, but heatwave days exceeding 40C are not uncommon — Adelaide recorded 46.6C in January 2019. Even on a typical day, riders finishing the 136 km route after midday will face 30-35C temperatures on exposed climb faces with limited shade. The dry heat is deceptive — low humidity means sweat evaporates quickly, masking the rate of fluid loss. The organisers mitigate this with the pre-dawn 5:45 AM start, but physics and geography are unforgiving. Carry more water than you think you need. Electrolyte supplementation is not optional. The climate score of 3 reflects that while Adelaide heat is a genuine challenge, it is a predictable one — you know what you are riding into, and you can prepare for it.

How adventurous is Adelaide Epic Ride (Santos Tour Down Under)?

+

The adventure here is experiential rather than geographical. You are riding the same roads that WorldTour professionals will race on later that same day. You will climb the same hills, descend the same roads, and finish in the same town. The Adelaide Hills — rolling vineyards, eucalyptus-lined roads, small towns like Hahndorf and Nairne — deliver genuine scenic appeal that surprises riders expecting flat farmland. The Ziptrak Stage 3 route from the coast through the hills to the finish is a well-designed journey with narrative arc: flat coastal start, transitional suburbs, then the hills reveal themselves. It is not wilderness riding, but it is a meaningful journey through distinctive South Australian landscape.

Should I race Adelaide Epic Ride (Santos Tour Down Under)?

+

Yes if you want the unique experience of riding a genuine WorldTour stage route on race day, embedded in the Santos Tour Down Under festival atmosphere. Yes if you can handle South Australian summer heat and want a well-supported 136 km ride with real climbing. Skip if you need competitive racing, if you cannot tolerate extreme heat, or if you find $225 AUD steep for a community ride.
