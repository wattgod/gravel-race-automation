Facts & History

Created in memory of Amy Gillett, an Australian Olympic rower who transitioned to competitive cycling and was killed at age 29 by a motorist while training with the Australian national cycling team on a road in Zeulenroda, Germany, on July 18, 2005. Six of her teammates were also hit — all survived with serious injuries. Her death galvanized Australian cycling safety advocacy. The Amy Gillett Foundation was established to reduce the incidence of cycling fatalities, and the gran fondo became its flagship fundraising event, using the Great Ocean Road as a stage for both remembrance and the message that every cyclist deserves safe passage. Founded in 2011. By Amy Gillett Foundation.

Reputation: Australia's memorial ride. Where the Great Ocean Road meets a cause worth riding for.

2005: Amy Gillett killed by a motorist while training in Germany, catalyzing Australian cycling safety movement
Amy Gillett Foundation established, driving 'a metre matters' minimum passing distance legislation
~2011: First Amy's Gran Fondo on the Great Ocean Road
Event has grown to 3,000-4,000 riders, becoming Australia's premier charity cycling event

The Course

Coastal beauty and forested climbing along one of the world's great roads. The route starts in Lorne and rides the Great Ocean Road — Southern Ocean on one side, Otway Ranges forest on the other. The coastal sections are rolling with curves that hug the cliffs. Inland diversions climb through temperate rainforest — tall eucalyptus, tree ferns, dappled light. The route finishes in Aireys Inlet. The terrain is moderate — challenging enough to feel earned, accessible enough that the ride's meaning reaches everyone.

Signature challenge: The Great Ocean Road itself — not a single climb or feature, but 103 km of coastal beauty and Otway forest riding that serves as a stage for remembrance.

MILE
Lorne Start
Rolling out from Lorne, one of the Great Ocean Road's most charming coastal towns. The Southern Ocean stretches to the horizon. Morning light on the water. The emotional weight of riding in Amy's memory is palpable at the start line.
20
MILE
The Otway Climbs
The route turns inland into the Otway Ranges. Temperate rainforest climbing — tall eucalyptus, tree ferns, damp air. The climbs are sustained but moderate in gradient. The canopy closes overhead. This is where the Great Ocean Road's tourism gives way to something quieter and greener.
40
MILE
The Coastal Return
Back on the Great Ocean Road. The Southern Ocean reappears. Rolling coastal terrain with curves that reward smooth bike handling. Wind can be a factor on exposed headland sections. The scenery — coastal cliffs, crashing waves, distant headlands — is the Great Ocean Road at its best.
55
MILE
Aireys Inlet Finish
The final approach to Aireys Inlet. The iconic Split Point Lighthouse marks the finish. The last kilometers are rolling and scenic. The finish line atmosphere is emotional — riders who lost friends to road incidents, families honoring loved ones, and thousands who rode for the cause.

Race Logistics

Airport
Melbourne Tullamarine (MEL) — international and domestic connections. Lorne is approximately 2.5 hours southwest. Avalon Airport (AVV) — budget domestic flights, closer to Geelong and the Great Ocean Road.
Lodging
Lorne is a small coastal town — accommodation is limited and books out fast for event weekend. Book months in advance. Apollo Bay and Aireys Inlet are nearby alternatives. Melbourne accommodation is abundant but means a 2.5-hour drive. Budget AUD $150-250/night in Lorne. Many riders stay in Geelong (1 hour from Lorne) for more options.
Food
Great Ocean Road towns have solid cafe and restaurant scenes — Australian coastal cuisine with good coffee. Lorne's main strip has multiple options. Carb loading: find a pasta or fish-and-chips spot. Post-ride: coffee and a pie from a local bakery. Australian cycling culture runs on flat whites.
Packet Pickup
Saturday before the event in Lorne. Registration and event information from the Amy Gillett Foundation.
Parking
Lorne has limited parking — arrive early or use event shuttle options from Geelong or Melbourne. Follow event transport directions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Amy's Gran Fondo?

+

September in Victoria is spring — variable but not extreme. The weather can change rapidly along the Great Ocean Road, and coastal wind is a factor. But temperatures are comfortable for cycling (55-70F), and genuine extremes are rare. The climate adds character rather than danger. Bring a vest, be prepared for rain, ride through it.

How are the logistics for Amy's Gran Fondo?

+

Melbourne (Tullamarine) is Australia's second-largest airport with domestic and international connections. Lorne is about 2.5 hours southwest via the Great Ocean Road or the faster inland route. Event logistics are solid — well-organized by the Amy Gillett Foundation with clear information, bus transfers, and parking. The Great Ocean Road infrastructure supports the event well.

How adventurous is Amy's Gran Fondo?

+

The Great Ocean Road is one of the world's iconic coastal drives, and riding it on a bike is special. The Otway Ranges temperate rainforest is lush and atmospheric. Southern Ocean views, koala sightings, towering eucalyptus. The scenery is genuinely beautiful. But this is well-traveled tourist infrastructure, not remote adventure.

How prestigious is Amy's Gran Fondo?

+

Amy Gillett's name carries deep significance in Australian cycling. She was an Olympic rower turned cyclist killed by a motorist while training in Germany in 2005. Her death catalyzed cycling safety advocacy across Australia. The foundation bearing her name has driven 'a metre matters' legislation. Riding in her memory has genuine emotional weight that goes beyond typical event prestige.

Should I race Amy's Gran Fondo?

+

Yes if you want to ride the Great Ocean Road for a cause that matters to every cyclist who shares the road with cars. Yes if you want a beautiful, accessible ride with a passionate community. Skip if you need serious physical challenge or competitive racing.
