The Ratings

Course Profile
Logistics 4/5

St. Anton am Arlberg is a top-tier ski resort that knows how to host large events. Innsbruck airport is 100 km east, Zurich is 200 km west — both offer solid international connections. The town sits directly on the main rail line between Zurich and Innsbruck, making car-free travel straightforward. Accommodation ranges from budget pensions to luxury hotels, all accustomed to handling thousands of visitors. Registration opens October of the prior year and sells out — the 2025 edition was completely full. Packet pickup, start, and finish all happen in St. Anton's pedestrian zone, which is compact and walkable. The tourism association runs the event, and it shows.

Editorial
Prestige 3/5

The Arlberg Giro has built a solid reputation over 13 completed editions as one of Austria's premier road cycling gran fondos. CycloWorld calls it an 'Austrian tier 1 gran fondo.' The professional criterium on Saturday attracts pro teams from across Europe, lending legitimacy to the weekend. The event sells out and draws international riders from 20+ countries. But it is not a UCI event, it does not have the history or name recognition of the Maratona dles Dolomites or the Otztaler Radmarathon, and it remains primarily a European affair. It is prestigious within the Austrian and central European gran fondo scene, not globally.

Facts & History

The Arlberg Giro was created by the St. Anton tourism association as part of a strategy to establish the ski resort as a summer cycling destination. The route leverages the same dramatic Alpine passes that make the Arlberg region legendary for skiing — the Arlbergpass and Silvretta Hochalpenstrasse — and transforms them into a cycling challenge. Since its inaugural edition in 2013, the event has grown steadily from a regional event to an international gran fondo that sells out annually at 1,500-2,000 riders. Founded in 2013. By Tourism Association St. Anton am Arlberg.

Reputation: Widely regarded as one of Austria's best-organized alpine gran fondos. CycloWorld rates it as an 'Austrian tier 1 gran fondo' and describes it as having 'organization of the highest level.' The event has earned a loyal following of repeat participants drawn by the combination of challenging climbing, stunning scenery, and the unique village atmosphere of St. Anton am Arlberg.

2013: Inaugural edition establishes the event in the Austrian gran fondo calendar
Addition of the International Professional Criterium creates a full weekend cycling festival
2023: 1,200 riders start; Swiss rider Fadri Barandun wins, German Julia Schallau defends her women's title
2024: Event skipped due to Arlberg tunnel renovation — one of only two missed editions
2025: 13th edition returns with 1,500 participants — described as a triumphant comeback
2026: 14th edition announces route change due to Silvretta Hochalpenstrasse closure — Zeinisjoch/Ganifertal alternative introduced

The Course

A circular alpine gran fondo starting and finishing in St. Anton am Arlberg, crossing two major mountain passes through the Austrian states of Tyrol and Vorarlberg. The route demands sustained climbing ability and confident descending on well-maintained alpine roads with top-tier mountain scenery throughout.

Signature challenge: The Silvretta Hochalpenstrasse climb: 22.5 km at 5.8% average to the Bielerhöhe at 2,032m — the defining ascent of the Arlberg Giro, arriving after 90+ km of riding and the Arlbergpass already in the legs.

Pre-Dawn Start
5:00 AM mass start from St. Anton's pedestrian zone in darkness. The town sits at 1,304m — you begin at altitude. Cold morning air and adrenaline as 1,500 riders roll out together.
?
MILE
Arlbergpass Summit
10.5 km climb averaging 6.0% from St. Anton to the Arlbergpass at 1,793m. The opening salvo — sorts the field before the descent into Vorarlberg. The first cut-off at ~08:15.
?
MILE
Klostertal Descent to Bludenz
Fast, sweeping descent from the Arlbergpass through the Klostertal valley into Bludenz. Speed builds through tunnels and curves — the first real descending test of the day.
?
MILE
Montafon Valley
Rolling valley road through the Montafon toward Partenen. Transitional riding before the main climbing event. The scenic valley provides a brief mental respite.
?
MILE
The Main Climb
The centerpiece: Silvretta Hochalpenstrasse (22.5 km at 5.8% to 2,032m) in normal years, or the Ganifertal/Zeinisjoch alternative in 2026. This is where the race is won or lost. Sections reach 11-14% gradient. Cut-off at Zeinisjoch: 13:00.
?
MILE
Paznauntal Return
Descent and valley riding through the Paznauntal and Stanzertal back toward St. Anton. Tired legs meet fast valley roads. The finish line is in sight but the final kilometers feel longer than they should.

Race Logistics

Airport
Innsbruck (INN) 100 km east (~1.5 hr drive), Zurich (ZRH) 200 km west (~2.5 hr drive or train). Munich (MUC) 250 km northeast (~3 hr). St. Anton is on the main Zurich-Innsbruck rail line — direct trains available.
Lodging
St. Anton is a major ski resort with extensive accommodation ranging from budget pensions (EUR 60-80/night) to luxury hotels (EUR 200+/night). Summer rates are significantly lower than winter. Book early — the event sells out and accommodation fills. The compact village means everything is walkable from most lodging. Nearby villages (St. Christoph, Pettneu, Flirsch) offer additional options within 10 km.
Food
St. Anton has excellent dining from traditional Austrian mountain huts (Hutten) to international restaurants. The pasta party is included with registration. Post-race, the pedestrian zone has numerous cafes and restaurants. Alpine Austrian cuisine — Tyrolean dumplings, schnitzel, strudel — is the reward after 2,500m of climbing.
Packet Pickup
Available in the days before the event in St. Anton. Check arlberg-giro.com for exact dates and locations.
Parking
Available in St. Anton — the town has public parking facilities for the ski season that serve event participants in summer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Arlberg Giro?

+

August in the Austrian Alps is beautiful when it cooperates and genuinely miserable when it does not. St. Anton sits at 1,304m and averages 18C in August — comfortable for riding. But the passes above 1,800m can be 10-15 degrees cooler, and August dumps roughly 200mm of rainfall with about 20 rain days. The 5:00 AM start means riding in darkness and cold before the valleys warm. Alpine thunderstorms can materialize in the afternoon precisely when slower riders are still on exposed mountain roads. The CycloWorld review describes conditions as manageable but notes the need for layering strategy and rain preparation.

+

How adventurous is Arlberg Giro?

+

The Arlberg region is one of the most scenic corridors in the Austrian Alps. The route traverses two Austrian states — Tyrol and Vorarlberg — crossing the continental divide at the Arlbergpass and descending into the Rhine watershed before climbing back over the Silvretta massif. You ride through the Klostertal, into the Montafon, and past the Silvretta reservoir (Bielerhöhe) with views of glaciers and 3,000m peaks. St. Anton itself is a charming Alpine village that reinvents itself every summer from ski resort to cycling and hiking destination. The scenery is top-tier. What keeps it at 4 rather than 5 is that this is well-traveled Austrian tourism territory — stunning, but not remote or wild.

How prestigious is Arlberg Giro?

+

The Arlberg Giro has built a solid reputation over 13 completed editions as one of Austria's premier road cycling gran fondos. CycloWorld calls it an 'Austrian tier 1 gran fondo.' The professional criterium on Saturday attracts pro teams from across Europe, lending legitimacy to the weekend. The event sells out and draws international riders from 20+ countries. But it is not a UCI event, it does not have the history or name recognition of the Maratona dles Dolomites or the Otztaler Radmarathon, and it remains primarily a European affair. It is prestigious within the Austrian and central European gran fondo scene, not globally.

Should I race Arlberg Giro?

+

If you want a well-organized Alpine gran fondo with stunning scenery and a genuine festival atmosphere — absolutely. This is European cycling at its most enjoyable. If you are chasing UCI points, maximum vertical, or global prestige — the Maratona dles Dolomites or Otztaler Radmarathon may be better targets. If you are coming from outside Europe, combine it with a broader Alps cycling trip to justify the travel.
