The Ratings

Course Profile
Logistics 3/5

Spartanburg sits on I-85 in upstate South Carolina — a real city with real infrastructure, not some mountain hamlet. Greenville-Spartanburg International Airport (GSP) is 20 minutes away with decent domestic connections. Charlotte (CLT) is 80 minutes and a major hub. Hotels in Spartanburg are plentiful and affordable. The start at Spartanburg Memorial Auditorium is well-organized with packet pickup the day before. The catch: the finish is 102 miles away on top of a mountain. You need to arrange shuttle transport back to Spartanburg after the ride, which the organizers provide but which adds hours to an already 11-hour day. The point-to-point logistics are the main complexity — this is not a loop course.

Editorial
Prestige 4/5

The Beast of the East. One of the oldest continuously running cycling events in the United States, founded in 1975 when John Bryan and a group of Freewheelers rode from Spartanburg to the summit of Mt. Mitchell. Nearly 50 years later, the ride is an institution. Completing the full Mitchell course carries weight in Southeast cycling circles — it is the region's defining long-distance climbing challenge. The event is not internationally famous like La Marmotte or the Maratona, and it does not have the brand recognition of events like RAGBRAI, but within American road cycling — especially east of the Rockies — saying you finished the Assault means something. The course record of 4:54 (Scottie Weiss, 2012) stands as a benchmark that even strong riders cannot approach.

Facts & History

In 1975, John Bryan — an early member of the Freewheelers, a Spartanburg-based cycling club founded in 1973 — set out to ride from his hometown to the summit of Mt. Mitchell with a group of hardy clubmates. That 103-mile ride through the Carolina Piedmont and up the Blue Ridge Parkway became the template for what would evolve into one of America's most iconic cycling events. The Freewheelers formalized the ride, and it grew from a club challenge into a nationally recognized endurance event. In the late 1990s, demand led the Freewheelers to add a second course ending in Marion, NC at mile 74 — a stepping stone for riders building toward the full Mitchell summit. In 2015, the Freewheelers created a 501(c)(3) sister organization, the Freewheelers Cycling Association, to formalize their charitable mission. The event now gives back more than $8,000-10,000 annually to organizations including Mt. Mitchell State Park, the Blue Ridge Parkway, Partners for Active Living, and other Upstate SC and Western NC nonprofits. The 47th edition in 2024 drew 533 cyclists. The 2025 edition was canceled due to infrastructure work at Mt. Mitchell State Park, with the 2026 return scheduled for May 18. Founded in 1975. By John Bryan and the Freewheelers Cycling Club of Spartanburg, SC.

Reputation: The Beast of the East. The longest-running cycling challenge to the highest point east of the Mississippi. An institution of Southeast cycling culture.

The Course

The Assault on Mt. Mitchell is a study in patience and delayed devastation. The first 80 miles lull you into thinking this is a pleasant century ride — rolling Piedmont roads through South Carolina and the North Carolina foothills, gaining barely 500 feet of net elevation as you pass through rural towns between Spartanburg and Marion. The road is flat enough to make you forget what you signed up for. Then mile 85 arrives, and the road tilts upward, and it does not stop tilting for the next 18 miles. The climb to the Blue Ridge Parkway gains over 1,000 feet in under 2 miles. The Parkway itself is a sustained grind at 3,000-5,000 feet with no flat recovery. At mile 98, you turn off the Parkway toward Mt. Mitchell State Park, and those final 5 miles to the summit are the cruelest on the course — steep, relentless grades through ancient spruce-fir forest to the summit at 6,684 feet. The air is cooler. The trees are different. You are standing at the highest point east of the Mississippi River, and your legs stopped working two miles ago.

Signature challenge: The final 18 miles from Marion to the summit. After 80+ miles of rolling Piedmont riding, the course demands 5,000+ feet of climbing in the final stretch — a backloaded elevation profile that punishes anyone who did not respect the first 80 miles. The last 5 miles up the Mt. Mitchell summit road, on legs that have been working for 7-10 hours, through gradients that would be hard on fresh legs, is where the Beast of the East earns every letter of its name.

MILE
Spartanburg — The Warm-Up Lie
6:30 AM start from Spartanburg Memorial Auditorium at 800 feet elevation. The morning is warm, the group energy is high, and the road rolls north through South Carolina. You are riding at 15-18 mph in a peloton and the legs feel great. This will not last. The first 40 miles through the Piedmont are flat-to-rolling, gaining almost nothing in elevation. The danger is going too hard too early because the road lets you.
40
MILE
The Foothills — Quiet Attrition
Crossing into North Carolina, the terrain begins to undulate. Nothing savage — just enough grade to remind your legs they have been working for 2-3 hours. Rest stops are welcome oases. The road passes through small Carolina towns. You are gaining elevation slowly, almost imperceptibly. The mountains ahead are visible now.
74
MILE
Marion — The Decision Point
Rest stop 5 at Marion, NC. This is where the Marion course ends and where Mitchell riders say goodbye to the flatland. You have ridden 74 miles and gained only about 500 feet of net elevation. You feel good. You should not feel good — you should be saving everything you have. From here forward, every mile goes up.
85
MILE
The Wall Begins
At mile 85, the road begins to climb with intent. You are at 2,300 feet. By mile 87, turning onto the Blue Ridge Parkway, you are suddenly at 3,365 feet — over 1,000 feet of gain in less than 2 miles. The gears you thought you would not need are now your only friends. Your legs, which have been rolling at tempo for 5-6 hours, are being asked to climb. The adjustment is violent.
87
MILE
Blue Ridge Parkway — The Long Grind
America's Favorite Drive becomes your personal punishment. The Parkway is a sustained climb through 3,000-5,000 feet with sweeping mountain vistas that would be gorgeous if you could look up from your stem. The average grade is 4% but with pitches to 8%. There is no flat. There is no descent. The views are spectacular and you cannot appreciate a single one of them because every pedal stroke costs more than the last.
98
MILE
Mt. Mitchell Summit Road — The Beast
Turn off the Parkway at mile 98. The final 5 miles to the summit are the hardest you will ride all day, on the most exhausted legs you have ever had. The road climbs from roughly 5,000 feet to 6,684 feet through dense spruce-fir forest — trees that look like they belong in Canada, not North Carolina. The grades are relentless. Your heart rate is maxed. Your glycogen is gone. The summit marker at 6,684 feet — the highest point east of the Mississippi River — appears through the fog. You are done. You rode here from South Carolina.
RIDERS REPORT
The last 20 miles
Riders describe this as brutal climbing that feels endless. One rider called it the slowest 20 miles of their life, saying you think you're done when you enter the park but you're not.
First 80 miles
The start is described as a sprint with riders going faster than they've ever ridden before, making pacing critical for the climbs ahead.
The last section climbing for 20 miles is described as worse than the Rockies, just keeps going and going. Riders say it's more challenging than cross-country riding.
Beautiful countryside from Spartanburg with lovely rolling terrain, but conditions can turn rough once you get up on the Parkway.

Race Logistics

Airport
Greenville-Spartanburg International (GSP) — 20 minutes. Charlotte Douglas (CLT) — 80 minutes, major hub with cheap flights. Atlanta (ATL) — 3.5 hours, largest US hub.
Lodging
Stay in Spartanburg — affordable hotels along I-85 and in downtown ($80-150/night). Book well ahead. Some riders stay in nearby Greenville (30 min) for more dining and nightlife options. The start is at Spartanburg Memorial Auditorium, 385 N Church St — easy access from I-85 hotels.
Food
Spartanburg has a solid restaurant scene for a mid-size Southern city. Carb-loading options abound. Pre-race morning: eat at your hotel and arrive early. On-course nutrition is provided at 9 rest stops. Post-ride: the Freewheelers organize food at the finish and at packet pickup. Bring summit layers and cold-weather nutrition for the final climb.
Packet Pickup
Sunday before the Monday ride at Spartanburg Memorial Auditorium, 1:00-5:00 PM. In-person registration available at packet pickup.
Parking
Spartanburg Memorial Auditorium parking. Arrive by 5:30 AM for a 6:30 AM start. Shuttle buses return riders from Mt. Mitchell summit to Spartanburg after the ride.
RIDERS REPORT
Don't get caught up in the sprint start - riders go faster in the first 80 miles than they've ever ridden, which can hurt you on the climbs.
Be prepared for weather changes - conditions can turn nasty with rain and hail, especially in the final climbing sections.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at The Assault on Mt. Mitchell — Beast of the East?

+

Mid-May in the Carolina Piedmont is warm and humid — expect 70-80F at the 6:30 AM start in Spartanburg and climbing into the low 80s through the foothills. The heat and humidity in the first 50 miles will drain your hydration budget before the real climbing starts. Once you hit the Blue Ridge Parkway above 3,000 feet, the temperature drops meaningfully. At the Mt. Mitchell summit (6,684 ft), conditions can be 25-35 degrees colder than Spartanburg — fog, 45F temperatures, and gusty wind are normal for May. You need to ride in summer kit for the first half and have a vest and arm warmers for the summit. The weather is not extreme, but the spread from start to finish demands preparation.

How adventurous is The Assault on Mt. Mitchell — Beast of the East?

+

You start in a South Carolina city and end on the highest mountain east of the Mississippi River. You cross state lines. You ride through rural Piedmont farmland, Carolina foothills, the Blue Ridge Parkway's sweeping mountain vistas, and finish in a high-elevation spruce-fir forest that feels transported from New England or the Pacific Northwest. The transformation from urban start to alpine summit is dramatic. The Blue Ridge Parkway section is one of America's most scenic roads. Standing at 6,684 feet with your bicycle, looking out over the Black Mountains, knowing you rode there from South Carolina — that is an adventure. It loses a point only because the first 80 miles of rolling Piedmont highway, while pleasant, are not what anyone would call wild.

Should I race The Assault on Mt. Mitchell — Beast of the East?

+

Yes if you want the defining climbing challenge east of the Rockies. Yes if a century ride with 11,000 feet of backloaded climbing and a geographic superlative finish appeals to you. Yes if you value tradition and community over timing chips and podiums. Skip if you need competitive structure, technical terrain, or cannot handle 80 miles of rolling warm-up before the real climbing begins.
