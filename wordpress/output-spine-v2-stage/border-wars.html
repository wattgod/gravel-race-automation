Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Border Wars?

+

The 2024 race started at 38°F and warmed to 60°F with "20-30 mph gusts with mostly headwind," making the Alabama section particularly challenging. Carolyn Misick appreciated the "sunny and temps in the 60s" forecast, though the temperature range "made it a bit tough to dress for." Late February timing avoids summer heat while staying generally rideable. Similar climate challenges to SBT GRVL.

How are the logistics for Border Wars?

+

Packet pickup at S&S Fire Pits the day before shows local business integration and prevents race morning chaos. Franklin, Georgia provides a small-town race hub without big-city complications, though specific parking and lodging details remain vague. The missing airport and accommodation strategy information keeps this from being higher.

How adventurous is Border Wars?

+

Creek crossings provide the main adventure elements, with Carolyn Misick noting "sinking your shoe into deep muddy water" before a "stream crossing at the bottom" that served as a "shoe wash." The route crosses state lines mid-race, and Carolyn enjoyed seeing "my time greatly improve as we rode through Alabama which is on central standard time."

How prestigious is Border Wars?

+

Three years old and still finding its identity—started as Border Wars, morphed into Homegrown Gravel Adventure, but everyone still calls it Border Wars. No pro field, no prize purse, no mention of notable winners in any coverage. It's a solid regional event that locals respect for the terrain punishment, but it's not moving any needles beyond the GA/AL border.

Should I race Border Wars?

+

If you're in GA/AL/Southeast and want a local event—yes, it's solid. If you're from out of region—there are better options.
