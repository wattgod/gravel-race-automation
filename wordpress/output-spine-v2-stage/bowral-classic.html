Facts & History

The Bowral Classic was born in 2016 when Sydney-based Yaffa Media, publisher of Australia's leading cycling magazine Bicycling Australia, decided to create a cycling gran fondo worthy of the Southern Highlands region. James Yaffa devised a media and marketing campaign that generated $3 million in exposure from a $50,000 investment. An initial target of 1,000 riders was obliterated when more than 3,000 cyclists from across Australia and eight overseas countries descended on Bowral on 23 October 2016 for the inaugural event. Participants raised $109,000 for charity in that first edition alone. The event was centred on Bowral -- the largest town in the Southern Highlands, famous as the birthplace of cricket legend Sir Donald Bradman -- and the surrounding countryside that had long been a favourite of Sydney cyclists for its rolling terrain and picturesque villages. The success of the Bowral Classic spawned the Cycling Classics series, including the Clare Classic (South Australia) and Noosa Classic (Queensland). By 2024, the events had collectively raised over $500,000 for Australian charities. Founded in 2016. By Yaffa Media (publisher of Bicycling Australia magazine). Event conceived by James Yaffa..

Reputation: Australia's most consistently delivered cycling gran fondo. While flashier events (L'Etape Australia, Cadel Evans) carry bigger names, the Bowral Classic has built its reputation on reliability, community, and a course that genuinely showcases one of Australia's most beautiful regions. Within the Australian cycling community, it is the accessible bucket-list event -- challenging enough to require training, scenic enough to justify the effort, and social enough to bring friends.

2016: Inaugural event. 3,000+ riders from across Australia and 8 countries. $109,000 raised for charity. Immediate success.
2017: Wins 'Best New Event' and 'Best Community Event' at the Australian Event Awards. Establishes credibility beyond cycling.
2018: Cycling Classics brand expands with Clare Classic (SA) and Noosa Classic (QLD) sister events.
2019-2021: Navigates COVID disruptions. Event adapts and continues while other Australian cycling events fold.
2024: Ninth edition. Close to 3,000 riders. Maxi Classic distance nearly sold out. Over 2,500 riders on course.
2025: Tenth anniversary edition on 19 October. Hosted in Extreme Fire Danger conditions (event proceeded). Continues to attract international entries.
2026: Eleventh edition announced for 18 October.

The Course

A rolling pastoral odyssey through the Southern Highlands of New South Wales. The course is characterised by relentless undulation rather than sustained alpine climbing -- the road is almost never flat, constantly rising and falling through green hills, dairy country, and heritage villages. The 150km Maxi Classic passes through Bowral, Berrima, Moss Vale, Sutton Forest, Bundanoon, Robertson, Kangaloon, and Glenquarry before the final assault on Mt Gibraltar and the finish at Loseby Park. The terrain rewards steady tempo riding and punishes early heroics. Three K/QOM segments provide competitive structure within the rolling landscape.

Signature challenge: The relentless undulation. There is no single monster climb -- the Southern Highlands course kills you with a thousand cuts. Constant rolling terrain across 150km means your legs never fully recover between efforts. Mt Gibraltar at the finish is the exclamation point, but the real challenge is managing fatigue through 140km of terrain that never lets you rest.

Race Logistics

Packet Pickup
Event Village at Loseby Park, Bowral on Saturday (day before). Combined with cycling exhibitors, food stalls, and entertainment.
Parking
Designated event parking in Bowral township. Arrive early -- the 6:30 AM Maxi Classic start means parking fills quickly.
RIDERS REPORT
Try not to stop - stopping is absolute dead time. Carry as much food and water as you can, maybe take extra plastic water bottles to throw in feed zones to keep moving.
Strategy is to hit it hard and fast for the first 36 km as advised by coach - if you can stay with front pack for first 36k, the next 60k is pretty much flat rolling hills where you can sit in wheels.
Plan to wait for teammates at the top of KOM sections but minimize stopping time at rest stops except for the one at 100 km. Goal is to keep momentum going.
Make every move predictable when riding in the big group early on since you don't know the abilities or awareness of other riders. Feel free to go to the front and ride hard on early lumps and bumps.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Bowral Classic?

+

October in the Southern Highlands is genuinely pleasant cycling weather. At 690m elevation, Bowral avoids the coastal humidity of Sydney and the summer heat that plagues Australian events held in January-March. Spring temperatures of 15-22C are close to ideal for endurance cycling. The morning chill at 6:30 AM start (8-12C) means layering, but you warm up fast. Rain risk exists but is moderate. Compared to events like Cadel Evans (extreme January heat) or L'Etape Australia (cancellation-level storms), the Bowral Classic climate is predictable and favourable.

+

On-course, the execution is excellent. Getting there requires planning.

How adventurous is Bowral Classic?

+

The Southern Highlands is genuinely beautiful -- rolling green hills, historic sandstone towns, cool-climate vineyards, and views that stretch to the escarpment edge. Riding through Berrima (Australia's best-preserved Georgian village), past the turn-off to Fitzroy Falls, and through the rainforest edge of Robertson is scenic and characterful. The towns have personality: antique shops, pubs, galleries, and the unmistakable feel of a region that has been settled for 200 years. But this is pastoral countryside 90 minutes from a major city, not remote wilderness. The adventure is cultural and aesthetic rather than geographical.

How prestigious is Bowral Classic?

+

Within Australian cycling, the Bowral Classic has established itself as a fixture -- it won Best New Event and Best Community Event at the 2017 Australian Event Awards, consistently attracts 2,500-3,000 riders, and has spawned sister events (Clare Classic, Noosa Classic). Bicycling Australia magazine's backing gives it media visibility domestically. But it carries no international prestige: no UCI affiliation, no pro-am category, no notable professional cyclists participating, and no recognition outside the Australian cycling bubble. It is a well-regarded domestic gran fondo, not a destination event on the world stage.

Should I race Bowral Classic?

+

If you are based in Sydney or eastern Australia and want a well-organized, scenic gran fondo without the logistical complexity of traveling to the Victorian Alps or the cancellation risk of L'Etape Australia, the Bowral Classic delivers. The 150km Maxi Classic with 2,393m of climbing is a genuine fitness challenge, the Southern Highlands scenery rewards you for every pedal stroke, and the Event Village at the finish is a proper celebration. Spring weather in October makes the riding enjoyable rather than a battle against heat or cold. Skip if you need international prestige, competitive depth, or mountain-scale climbing. Skip if you are flying internationally specifically for this event -- there are better-value destinations for that level of travel investment.
