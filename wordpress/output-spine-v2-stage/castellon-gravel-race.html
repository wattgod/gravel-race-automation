Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Castellon Gravel Race?

+

Historical weather data shows average highs of 60°F and lows of 41°F, with a 23% chance of rain on any given day, and gusts reaching 32 mph. Padrones reports "temperatures around the 15 degrees Celsius along the race itself" with morning chill giving way to comfortable conditions. The February timing attracts international riders "encouraged... attracted by the mild weather we are used to having in the Mediterranean area at this time of the year." Similar climate challenges to SBT GRVL.

How are the logistics for Castellon Gravel Race?

+

Jorge Padrones notes you arrive in Llucena to discover "big mountains around you as the only exit from the village" - rural isolation that limits infrastructure. The race sold out at 800 riders with 250 traveling from abroad, but basic travel details remain scarce for what's become "the most internationally popular gravel event so far in Spain."

How adventurous is Castellon Gravel Race?

+

Racing through every corner of Llucena's municipal territory on 72% gravel roads delivers proper exploration of Spanish mountain terrain most cyclists never see. The course design visiting the entire town area means discovering rural Spain, not just hammering a predetermined loop. February timing adds off-season adventure appeal when most European gravel racing shuts down.

How prestigious is Castellon Gravel Race?

+

Opening the 2026 UCI Gravel World Series globally and offering Worlds qualification elevates this second-year race significantly. Valverde's repeat victory and the international field of 800 riders builds credibility, but the event is still establishing its reputation against established European gravel races.

Should I race Castellon Gravel Race?

+

If you're in Europe—yes, it's solid. If you're international—there are better options.
