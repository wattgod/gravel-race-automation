Facts & History

Created in 2017 by retired cycling legend Fabian Cancellara through his company Sette Sports AG (Schenkon, Switzerland). The original concept was simple: 333 amateur cyclists on a 10-15km course with steep hills, trying to cross the finish line before Cancellara. The format evolved into ultra-distance point-to-point routes through the Swiss Alps, with the Bern-Zermatt (275km, 5,500m) and Zurich-Andermatt (195km, 4,200m) becoming the flagship events. Cancellara — nicknamed 'Spartacus' during his pro career — expanded the series to include the Granfondo Vaduz (92km, Liechtenstein), virtual Zwift events (from 2019), and international editions in Mallorca, Abu Dhabi, Denmark, and Italy. The event also launched 'Fast and Female' (women's participation initiative) and the 'Gummelerin' format. Around 400 cycling enthusiasts participate per event. Founded in 2017. By Fabian Cancellara / Sette Sports AG.

Reputation: Switzerland's toughest one-day cycling event — where amateurs try to beat an Olympic champion through the Alps.

2017: First edition with original short-course format
2018: Expanded to nine destinations globally
2019: Zwift virtual events launched
2024: Bern-Zermatt route updated — 275km, 5,500m climbing
2025: Three events in different formats across the season

The Course

An ultra-distance point-to-point road race through the Swiss Alps. The Bern-Zermatt flagship begins at 2:00 AM in Ittigen near Bern, threading through the Gantrisch Natural Park in darkness before crossing the Mittelberg and Col du Pillon passes. The route winds through the lower Valais valley before the brutal final climb from Visp to Zermatt (1,620m) — a relentless drag after 250km in the saddle. The Zurich-Andermatt variant follows Lake Zurich through Glarnerland before tackling the Klausen Pass (1,948m) and descending to Andermatt. Both routes are fully paved on excellent Swiss roads.

Signature challenge: The final climb from Visp to Zermatt — 35km of sustained climbing to 1,620m after 250km already in the legs, with the Matterhorn watching your suffering.

Race Logistics

Airport
ZRH (Zurich) for Zurich-Andermatt; BRN (Bern) or GVA (Geneva) for Bern-Zermatt
Lodging
Book accommodation at the finish city (Andermatt or Zermatt) — that's where you'll collapse. Use Swiss rail to get to the start. Zermatt is car-free; arrive by train from Visp/Tasch. Book early — Alpine resort towns fill up in summer.
Food
Swiss Alpine cuisine. Pasta party included with registration. Six feed zones on course. Post-race meals in Zermatt/Andermatt.
Packet Pickup
Starter package mailed to home address before event. Race day check-in at start location.
Parking
Zurich: Saalsporthalle area. Bern: Ittigen area. Zermatt is car-free — park in Tasch and take the shuttle train.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Chasing Cancellara?

+

Alpine summer weather is generally favorable but unpredictable. June-September timing avoids the worst, but mountain passes don't care about the calendar. The Bern-Zermatt starts in darkness at 2:00 AM, meaning the first half is ridden in cool night air. By afternoon, valley floors can be hot. Rain arrived on the Cyclingnews reviewer's final climb to Zermatt. Pack for everything.

How are the logistics for Chasing Cancellara?

+

Swiss precision meets cycling. Starter package mailed to your home. Baggage transport from start to finish. Six feed zones across 275km. Online race briefing and roadbook. Transport of personal items between checkpoints. The point-to-point format means you need to sort your own transport between start and finish cities, but Switzerland's train system makes that trivial. Zurich and Bern are major international airports.

How adventurous is Chasing Cancellara?

+

This is pure adventure cycling wrapped in a competitive format. Starting from the Swiss capital at 2:00 AM, riding through Alpine darkness, crossing mountain passes as dawn breaks, descending into the Valais valley, and finishing in Zermatt beneath the Matterhorn after 275km — that's the stuff of cycling dreams. The point-to-point format through some of Europe's most spectacular scenery is a genuine journey, not a loop.

How prestigious is Chasing Cancellara?

+

Fabian Cancellara is a genuine cycling legend — two Olympic gold medals, four Paris-Roubaix wins, three Tour of Flanders, multiple world TT championships. The man they called 'Spartacus' now invites amateurs to literally chase him through the Swiss Alps. That's not a marketing gimmick with a B-list celebrity — that's a Hall of Famer who still rides. The concept has spawned imitators but nobody else has a Cancellara.

Should I race Chasing Cancellara?

+

Yes if you want an ultra-endurance Alpine road challenge with genuine star power. Yes if 275km and 5,500m of climbing in one day excites rather than terrifies you. Skip if you want gravel or mixed-surface riding. Skip if Swiss pricing is a dealbreaker.
