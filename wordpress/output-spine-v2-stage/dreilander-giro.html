The Ratings

Course Profile
Logistics 3/5

Nauders is a mountain village of 1,500 people at 1,394m on the Austrian side of the Reschenpass. Innsbruck airport is the closest at roughly 1.5 hours, but has limited international flights. Munich (3.5h) and Zurich (3h) are the realistic gateway airports. A free Postbus from Landeck-Zams station with bike transport helps for train arrivals. The event sells out within hours — not a lottery, but you need to be ready the instant registration opens on October 1. Two route options (168 km and 120 km) give flexibility. Start and finish in the same village. No complaints about packet pickup or pre-event organization — the GiroDays festival week leading into race day is a genuine bonus.

Editorial
Prestige 4/5

Since 1994, the Dreiländergiro has grown into one of Europe's largest and most respected cycling marathons. The Stelvio alone confers legendary status — it is arguably the most famous climb in professional road cycling. The event sells out within hours and draws riders from 30 nations. In the DACH cycling world (Germany, Austria, Switzerland), completing the Dreiländergiro is a badge of honor. But it doesn't yet carry the global household-name recognition of La Marmotte, L'Etape du Tour, or the Maratona dles Dolomites. Hence p4, not p5.

Facts & History

The Dreiländergiro was born in 1994 when the Bikeclub Nauders — a small, non-profit cycling club in a Tyrolean village of 1,500 people — created a road cycling marathon that would exploit Nauders' unique geographic position at the tri-border point of Austria, Italy, and Switzerland. The concept was simple and irresistible: start in Austria, cross into Italy, climb the legendary Stelvio Pass, descend into Switzerland, and return to Nauders — three countries, one ride. The Stelvio, already hallowed by decades of Giro d'Italia history, gave the event instant credibility. What began as a local cycling adventure grew steadily through the 2000s and 2010s, propelled by word of mouth in the German-speaking cycling world. By the 2020s, the event had become one of Europe's largest cycling marathons — 3,000 riders from 30 nations, selling out within hours. The 30th edition in 2023 was a milestone celebration. The 33rd edition in 2026 continues the tradition. Founded in 1994. By Bikeclub Nauders.

Reputation: The definitive tri-border Alpine cycling experience. In DACH cycling culture, the Dreiländergiro is the event that combines the Stelvio's legendary status with the unique adventure of riding through three countries in a single day. Organized by a non-profit cycling club that treats the event as a passion project, not a business.

The Course

A geographic masterpiece disguised as a bike ride. Route A (Stelvio Engadin) is the complete experience: 168 km through Austria, Italy, and Switzerland, with the Stelvio as the centerpiece and the Engadin Valley as the reward. Route B (Stelvio Vinschgau) delivers the Stelvio and the Umbrail in a more compact 120 km format. Both routes start and finish in Nauders at 1,394m. The signature challenge is obvious — the Stelvio's 48 hairpins from Prad, 24.9 km at 7.4% average, ascending to 2,757m. But the route's genius is everything around the Stelvio: the approach through the Italian Vinschgau, the Umbrail descent into Switzerland, and (on Route A) the long Engadin traverse before the final push over the Norbertshöhe.

Signature challenge: The Stelvio from Prad. 48 hairpins. 24.9 km at 7.4% average. 2,757 meters above sea level. This is the climb that has decided Giro d'Italia stages, broken professional cyclists, and inspired a million cycling photographs. On the Dreiländergiro, you ride it in a mass-start event with 1,500 others, counting down the numbered hairpins from 48 to 1. The upper switchbacks above treeline — exposed to weather, short of oxygen, spectacular in every direction — are the most dramatic kilometers in European sportive cycling.

MILE
Nauders to Reschenpass
The opening kilometers climb gently from Nauders (1,394m) to the Reschenpass at the Austrian-Italian border. The drowned church tower of Graun rises from Lago di Resia — your first sign that this ride deals in drama. The peloton of 1,500 riders is still bunched. Enjoy the company. It thins out on the Stelvio.
25
MILE
Prad to Stelvio Summit
The main event. From Prad am Stilfserjoch (915m), the road begins its ascent to 2,757m — 24.9 km at 7.4% average with gradients reaching 15%. The 48 numbered hairpins are cycling's most iconic switchbacks, each one a marker of progress and pain. Below treeline, the road winds through forest. Above 2,000m, the landscape opens to bare rock, snow patches, and panoramic views of the Ortler massif. The final hairpins are exposed, cold, and relentless. Reaching the summit is the defining moment of the ride.
50
MILE
Stelvio to Umbrail Pass and Val Mustair
A brief climb from near the Stelvio summit reaches the Umbrail Pass (2,501m) — the highest paved pass in Switzerland and the border crossing from Italy. The descent into the Val Mustair is fast, exposed, and often cold. You are now in Switzerland. The contrast with the Italian side is immediate: Swiss precision, orderly villages, a different pace. Regroup and refuel in Santa Maria Val Mustair.
65
MILE
Ofenpass (Route A only)
From Santa Maria, Route A tackles the Ofenpass (Pass dal Fuorn) — 13.3 km at 5.8% through the Swiss National Park. The climbing is steady but not savage. The landscape is pristine: Alpine meadows, ancient forests, and the quiet that comes with riding through a protected wilderness. The summit at 2,149m opens into the Engadin. Route B riders skip this pass and return via the Vinschgau.
90
MILE
Engadin Valley to Norbertshöhe (Route A)
The Engadin is a reward. Roughly 70 km of valley riding through one of Switzerland's most beautiful landscapes — Zernez, Susch, Lavin, Guarda, Scuol, Martina. The road trends gently downhill but the distance accumulates on tired legs. At the Swiss-Austrian border in Martina, the Norbertshöhe awaits: 5.8 km at 6.4%, the final climb before the descent to Nauders. Your legs know what they have done. The Norbertshöhe does not care.

Race Logistics

Airport
Innsbruck (INN) — 1.5 hours (closest, limited international flights). Munich (MUC) — 3.5 hours (best connections). Zurich (ZRH) — 3 hours. Train to Landeck-Zams station, then free Postbus with bike transport to Nauders.
Lodging
Stay in Nauders — the start/finish village with 209 accommodations and 4,200 beds. Book immediately upon securing your entry. Alpine guesthouses (Pensionen) offer the best value at EUR60-90/night. Hotels run EUR100-200/night. The GiroDays festival week starts Tuesday before the Saturday race — arrive early for the training rides and atmosphere. Nearby alternatives: Reschen/Resia (10 min, Italian side) or Pfunds (20 min down-valley).
Food
Tyrolean Alpine cuisine — Kaspressknödel (cheese dumplings), Tiroler Gröstl (pan-fried potato and meat), Kaiserschmarrn (shredded pancake), strudel. Italian influence from across the Reschenpass — pasta, pizza, South Tyrolean Speck. The GiroDays program includes food-focused events. Feed stations on course provide a mix of Austrian and Italian provisions.
Packet Pickup
Bib collection and event expo in Nauders during GiroDays week. The multi-day festival format means pickup is relaxed and combined with training rides, workshops, and community events.
Parking
Follow event parking instructions in Nauders. Limited parking in the village — the event strongly encourages public transport (free Postbus) and carsharing with a 15% entry fee discount for green travel.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Dreiländergiro?

+

Late June in the Alps is prime season but far from predictable. The Stelvio at 2,757m can deliver snow, sleet, rain, fog, or blazing sun — sometimes all in the same day. The 48 hairpins are fully exposed above treeline with no shelter. Valley sections in the Italian Vinschgau can hit 28C while the summit hovers near freezing. The Umbrail descent into Switzerland is notorious for cold and wind. Pack for four seasons. The one mercy: late June days are long, with usable daylight past 9 PM.

Start and finish in the same village. No complaints about packet pickup or pre-event organization — the GiroDays festival week leading into race day is a genuine bonus.

How adventurous is Dreiländergiro?

+

Three countries in a single ride. You leave Austria, cross into Italy at the Reschenpass, ascend the legendary Stelvio, descend the Umbrail into Switzerland, ride through the Swiss National Park over the Ofenpass, traverse the Engadin Valley, and re-enter Austria over the Norbertshöhe. Three languages, three currencies (though all use euros except Swiss francs), three distinct Alpine landscapes. The Stelvio's 48 numbered hairpins are cycling's most photographed switchbacks. The drowned church tower of Lago di Resia appears as you cross into Italy. This route is a geographic masterpiece.

But it doesn't yet carry the global household-name recognition of La Marmotte, L'Etape du Tour, or the Maratona dles Dolomites. Hence p4, not p5.

Should I race Dreiländergiro?

+

Yes if the Stelvio is on your bucket list and you want to ride it in a fully supported, mass-start event through three countries. Yes if you thrive on major Alpine climbing and want 3,600m of vertical gain in a single day. Skip if you cannot commit to the October 1 registration scramble. Skip if remote Austrian Alpine logistics are a dealbreaker.
