The Ratings

Course Profile
Logistics 3/5

Zernez is a small village at the gateway to the Swiss National Park — charming but remote. The Rhaetian Railway provides hourly connections from Chur, Landquart, the Upper Engadin, and Scuol, which makes car-free travel entirely feasible. Zurich airport is 3-3.5 hours by train. The organization is textbook Swiss: everything runs on time, the signage is impeccable, and the finish village in Zernez is compact and well-run. But the village is small — accommodation books out early, and there is no major airport nearby. The multi-day format means you need 3-4 nights of lodging, which complicates the logistics.

Editorial
Prestige 3/5

The Engadin Radmarathon is well-respected in Swiss and German-speaking cycling circles. It is part of the Swiss Cycling Top Tour — a quality label for Switzerland's top sportives. But it lacks the global name recognition of the Maratona dles Dolomites, L'Etape du Tour, or even the Otztaler Radmarathon. Founded in 2007, it is relatively young by European sportive standards. Within Switzerland, it is a bucket-list event. Outside the Alpine cycling world, it is largely unknown.

Facts & History

The Engadin Radmarathon was first organized in 2007 by the municipality of Zernez, leveraging the Lower Engadin's extraordinary position at the crossroads of some of the finest Alpine pass roads in Switzerland. The event was designed to showcase the Engadin Valley — a high-altitude corridor in the canton of Graubunden where Romansh is still spoken and the Swiss National Park provides a wilderness backdrop unique in the Alps. From its inception, the event was built around Swiss Cycling Top Tour standards: top organizational quality, panoramic routes, and a focus on the rider experience rather than pure competition. The three-day format — prologue, two stages — gave the event a mini-stage-race character that distinguished it from single-day sportives. By its 20th edition in 2026, the Engadin Radmarathon had established itself as one of Switzerland's premier cycling events. Founded in 2007. By Municipality of Zernez.

Reputation: Switzerland's best-kept cycling secret. One of the most beautiful multi-day sportives in the Alps, organized with quiet Swiss precision in a valley where the scenery does the talking.

2007: Inaugural edition from Zernez — establishing the three-day, five-pass format through the Engadin
Accepted into the Swiss Cycling Top Tour — Switzerland's quality label for top-tier sportives
Growth to 1,500-rider capacity — deliberately kept intimate to preserve the Alpine village experience
La Svizra route established as the queen stage: Fluela Pass and Albula Pass in a single day with 2,815m of climbing
2026: 20th anniversary edition — July 3-5, three days of Alpine cycling through the Engadin

The Course

A three-act Alpine drama staged across the Engadin Valley. The Friday prologue is a sharp appetizer: 6.7 km straight up toward the Ofenpass through the Swiss National Park, legs burning from the first pedal stroke. Saturday's La Cuorta is the international chapter: through a closed tunnel into the duty-free Italian enclave of Livigno, over the Forcola di Livigno and the Bernina Pass in rapid succession — with the glaciers of Piz Bernina and Piz Palu gleaming above the descent to Pontresina. Sunday's La Svizra is the masterpiece: the Fluela Pass at 2,383m rising from Susch in 13 km of sustained climbing, a fast descent into Davos, the long Landwasser Valley approach to the Albula Pass alongside the UNESCO World Heritage Rhaetian Railway, and the final descent back into the Engadin to Zernez. The cumulative effect of three days, five passes, and 221 km at altitude is a slow-building immersion in some of the finest cycling terrain in the Alps.

Signature challenge: The Albula Pass on Sunday. After two days of Alpine climbing — five passes already in the legs — the 26 km approach from the Landwasser Valley alongside the UNESCO World Heritage Rhaetian Railway is the event's emotional and physical crescendo. The climb is not the steepest, not the highest, not the most dramatic. It is the most relentless — and it comes when you have the least to give.

MILE
Il Prolog — Ofenpass Time Trial
Friday evening. 6.7 km from Zernez straight up toward the Ofenpass, gaining 400 meters. Through the Swiss National Park, the road climbing steadily through Alpine meadow and forest. The gradient is honest — 5-6% sustained — but the format is a time trial. Full effort from the gun. Your legs will remember this on Saturday morning.
MILE
La Cuorta — Ofenpass to Livigno
Saturday's opening act: the climb to the Ofenpass at 2,149m, then through the closed Munt la Schera tunnel into Italy. The descent into Livigno — a duty-free Italian enclave nestled in a high valley — provides a brief respite before the day's real climbing begins. The tunnel crossing feels like entering another world: Swiss discipline on one side, Italian flair on the other.
26
MILE
La Cuorta — Forcola and Bernina Double
The crux of Saturday: Forcola di Livigno (2,315m) and Bernina Pass (2,328m) with only a short, fast descent between them. Two passes above 2,300m in quick succession — the kind of climbing that makes you question your stage-race ambitions. The reward is the descent from the Bernina toward Pontresina: the glaciers of Piz Bernina (4,049m) and Piz Palu gleam to the left in one of cycling's great panoramas. The rails of the UNESCO World Heritage Bernina Express run alongside as you descend back into the Engadin.
MILE
La Svizra — Fluela Pass
Sunday's opening salvo and the highest point of the entire event. From Susch — just 4 km from Zernez — the road tilts upward for 13 km at 6-7% average to the Fluela Pass at 2,383m. The upper reaches are barren and windswept, the air noticeably thin on legs that already carry two days of Alpine climbing. The 13 km descent to Davos is fast and technically demanding — cold hands, tired legs, switchbacks that demand attention.
50
MILE
La Svizra — Landwasser Valley to Albula Pass
The defining section of the entire event. From Davos, the route descends through the Landwasser Valley to Alvaneu Bad at 957m — the lowest point of the day. Then the real climbing begins: 26 km alongside the UNESCO World Heritage Rhaetian Railway, through Bergun and up to the Albula Pass at 2,312m. The approach is relentless — steady gradient, no false flats, no reprieve. The railway viaducts and spiral tunnels that made this line a World Heritage Site provide distraction for the eyes while the legs burn. The summit at 2,312m, on the third day of racing, is where the Engadin Radmarathon reveals its true character.

Race Logistics

Airport
Zurich (ZRH) — 3-3.5 hours by train via Chur and the Rhaetian Railway. Innsbruck (INN) — 2.5 hours by car via the Arlberg or Reschen Pass. Milan Malpensa (MXP) — 4 hours via the Bernina or Julier Pass. Engadin Airport Samedan (SMV) — 45 minutes (limited private aviation).
Lodging
Stay in Zernez — the start/finish village. Hotels and holiday apartments book out well in advance for the event weekend. The civil defense facility in central Zernez offers bunk beds at CHF 40/night including breakfast (bring your own sleeping bag) — practical and central. Partner hotel packages with 2+ night stays offer special services via the Fan Package. Nearby villages (Susch, Lavin, Guarda) are 10-20 minutes away and offer alternatives if Zernez is full. The Upper Engadin (St. Moritz, Pontresina) is 30-45 minutes away with more options.
Food
Engadin cuisine is hearty Alpine fare: Capuns (chard rolls with spaetzle), Pizzoccheri (buckwheat pasta), Bundner Barley Soup, and Engadiner Nusstorte (the region's famous walnut tart). Zernez has a handful of restaurants serving traditional Graubunden and Italian-influenced dishes. Swiss prices apply — budget CHF 25-50 for a main course. Feed stations provide standard cycling nutrition.
Packet Pickup
Event registration and number pickup in the Zernez finish village area, typically available the day before the prologue. The compact village setting keeps everything walkable.
Parking
Zernez is small — follow event parking instructions. Designated parking areas with walkable distance to start/finish. The Rhaetian Railway is an excellent car-free alternative — hourly connections from Chur, Landquart, the Upper Engadin, and Scuol.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Engadin Radmarathon?

+

The Engadin is blessed with some of Switzerland's most reliable sunshine, and early July is prime Alpine cycling weather. But the high starting elevation of 1,473m means even the valleys are cool — morning starts at 7:00 AM in the Engadin are jacket weather. Pass summits above 2,300m bring the temperature down another 10-15 degrees. The descents — particularly the 13 km drop from the Fluela Pass to Davos — require warm layers at speed. This is not hostile weather, but it demands respect and layering strategy.

How are the logistics for Engadin Radmarathon?

+

Zernez is a small village at the gateway to the Swiss National Park — charming but remote. The Rhaetian Railway provides hourly connections from Chur, Landquart, the Upper Engadin, and Scuol, which makes car-free travel entirely feasible. Zurich airport is 3-3.5 hours by train. The organization is textbook Swiss: everything runs on time, the signage is impeccable, and the finish village in Zernez is compact and well-run. But the village is small — accommodation books out early, and there is no major airport nearby. The multi-day format means you need 3-4 nights of lodging, which complicates the logistics.

How adventurous is Engadin Radmarathon?

+

The Engadin delivers. The Friday prologue climbs through the Swiss National Park — Switzerland's only national park, established in 1914. Saturday crosses into Italy via a closed tunnel to Livigno, a duty-free Italian enclave, before climbing the Forcola and Bernina passes with views of the Bernina glacier massif. Sunday sends you over the Fluela Pass to Davos, through the Landwasser Valley alongside the UNESCO World Heritage Rhaetian Railway, and up the Albula Pass. Glaciers, national parks, UNESCO heritage, cross-border adventure, five high passes — the variety of landscapes in 221 km is extraordinary.

How prestigious is Engadin Radmarathon?

+

The Engadin Radmarathon is well-respected in Swiss and German-speaking cycling circles. It is part of the Swiss Cycling Top Tour — a quality label for Switzerland's top sportives. But it lacks the global name recognition of the Maratona dles Dolomites, L'Etape du Tour, or even the Otztaler Radmarathon. Founded in 2007, it is relatively young by European sportive standards. Within Switzerland, it is a bucket-list event. Outside the Alpine cycling world, it is largely unknown.

Should I race Engadin Radmarathon?

+

Yes if you want a multi-day Alpine stage experience with top-tier scenery and Swiss-level organization. Yes if five passes above 2,000m through the Engadin, with cross-border adventure into Italy, is your definition of a good weekend. Skip if Swiss prices are a dealbreaker — accommodation and dining add up fast across a multi-day format. Skip if you want a competitive race environment or a field bigger than 1,500 riders.
