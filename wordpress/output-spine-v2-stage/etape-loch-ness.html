The Ratings

Course Profile
Logistics 4/5

Twelve editions of operational refinement show in every detail. The Event Hub at Eden Court in Inverness centralizes registration, merchandise, Q&A sessions, mechanical support, and socializing into a polished weekend experience. Saturday registration (10:00-19:00) with mandatory photo ID is strictly enforced — no Sunday morning registration, no event-day entries, no exceptions. Four start waves (A-D) based on estimated finish time release riders in groups at 3-minute intervals between 06:00 and 06:50, preventing the chaos of a mass start with 6,000 riders. Inverness Airport (INV) is 15 minutes from the city center with connections to London, Manchester, and European hubs. The start area is 0.6 miles from Eden Court. Multiple support stations with HIGH5 nutrition, on-course mechanical support, and chip timing are standard. The only logistical friction: accommodation in Inverness fills quickly on event weekend, and parking requires early arrival.

Editorial
Prestige 3/5

Scotland's largest closed-road cycle sportive and one of the UK's most recognized cycling events. The 2025 edition drew riders from over 20 countries, with a third of participants traveling from outside Scotland. General entry sells out within days of opening. The event has hosted celebrities like Stephen Roche (1987 Triple Crown winner) as official starter for the 10th edition. It is part of the London Classics qualification pathway. However, this is a mass-participation sportive, not a UCI race or globally elite event — prestige is national and regional rather than international racing significance.

Facts & History

The inaugural Etape Loch Ness took place in 2014 as the first-ever closed-road cycling event around Loch Ness. Over 1,000 cyclists took part in that first edition, which was won by Alan Dean from Edinburgh in 2 hours 55 minutes, with Lynne Fraser from Deeside Thistle the fastest woman at 3 hours 26 minutes. Organiser Malcolm Sutherland said 'the route is absolutely incredible' and expressed confidence it would become an annual fixture. It grew rapidly — by 2022, the field had expanded to 6,100 riders and general entry was selling out in 36 hours. The partnership with Macmillan Cancer Support has been central since the beginning, with riders raising over £2 million for the cancer charity across the event's history. The 2024 10th edition was officially started by 1987 Triple Crown winner Stephen Roche, with 5,650 riders from 24 countries. The 2026 edition — the 12th — sold out its general entry, with remaining places available only through Macmillan charity entry. Founded in 2014. By Caledonian Concepts (Scotland) Ltd — the same team behind the Baxters Loch Ness Marathon and Festival of Running.

Reputation: Scotland's largest and most iconic closed-road cycle sportive. The combination of Loch Ness mythology, Highland scenery, closed-road access, and Macmillan Cancer Support partnership creates an event identity that transcends cycling — it is a Highland experience that happens to involve bicycles.

The Course

A 360-degree loop around one of the world's most famous bodies of water on fully closed roads. The route departs Inverness heading west along the north shore of Loch Ness on the A82, following the loch through the Great Glen — a geological fault line that splits Scotland diagonally from Inverness to Fort William. At Fort Augustus, the route reaches the southern tip of the loch where the Caledonian Canal connects Loch Ness to Loch Oich. Here, riders face the King of the Mountain: a 4.8-mile timed climb above the village gaining 380 metres with gradients reaching 12%. The summit delivers Highland panoramas before the descent to the south shore. The return leg follows narrower, quieter B-roads along the south side of Loch Ness — more atmospheric, more exposed to wind, and with rolling terrain that keeps the legs honest. The final miles approach Inverness from the south, with the last section through the city operating on left-lane closure rather than full road closure. The finish returns riders to the event area near Eden Court, completing the circumnavigation.

Signature challenge: The King of the Mountain at Glen Doe above Fort Augustus. 4.8 miles gaining 380 metres with gradients reaching 12%. The only timed competitive element in a non-competitive sportive, it is where the Etape Loch Ness transforms from a scenic ride into a proper cycling challenge.

MILE
Inverness to North Shore
The dawn start sends 6,000 riders west from Inverness on closed roads. The first miles are flat to gently rolling along the north shore of Loch Ness. Groups form quickly in the wave starts as riders settle into pace. The A82 is wide enough to accommodate the field without dangerous bunching. Morning mist over the loch and the first views of the Highland mountains create the atmosphere that defines the event.
34
MILE
King of the Mountain
At Fort Augustus, the route turns uphill for the signature challenge: a 4.8-mile (7.72km) timed climb gaining 380 metres with gradients reaching 12%. This is where the sportive earns its right to be called a challenge. The KOM/QOM split time is the only competitive element, and it separates riders definitively. Steep ramps force riders out of the saddle, and the sustained effort at near-threshold for 20-40 minutes exposes any fitness gaps. The summit rewards with Highland views before a descent that demands concentration.
45
MILE
South Shore Return
After the KOM descent, the route follows the south side of Loch Ness on narrower B-roads. This section is more exposed to wind — the Great Glen funnels air along the loch, and headwinds can sap energy from already-tired legs. The terrain is rolling rather than flat, with constant small climbs and descents that prevent recovery. The scenery is spectacular — closer to the water, more intimate than the north side — but by this point, riders are focused on the remaining 20+ miles rather than sightseeing.
60
MILE
Final Approach to Inverness
The last six miles bring riders back into Inverness. The final section uses left-lane closure rather than full road closure, meaning awareness of the road environment increases. Fatigue, 900m of accumulated climbing, and potentially 4-6 hours in the saddle make these final miles feel longer than the map suggests. The finish line and the medal are within reach, but the legs know they have been around a loch.

Race Logistics

Airport
Inverness Airport (INV) — 15 minutes from city center. Connections to London (Heathrow, Gatwick, Luton), Manchester, Bristol, and seasonal European routes via easyJet and Loganair. Edinburgh (EDI) or Glasgow (GLA) are 3-4 hours by road or rail.
Lodging
Book immediately upon securing entry. Inverness accommodation fills rapidly on event weekend — hotels, B&Bs, and Airbnb properties all get snapped up months in advance. The city center puts you within walking distance of the Event Hub at Eden Court and the start area. Budget travelers can look at hostels or accommodation in Beauly, Dingwall, or Nairn (20-30 minute drive). Many riders and families extend the weekend to explore the Highlands.
Food
Inverness has a solid food and drink scene for a Highland city. The Victorian Market and surrounding streets offer restaurants, pubs, and cafes. The Event Hub at Eden Court features local food vendors, coffee, and pop-up bars. Fort Augustus at the halfway point is a small Highland village — do not expect extensive options. Bring your own on-bike nutrition and hydration strategy; do not rely solely on support stations.
Packet Pickup
Saturday 25 April 2026, 10:00-19:00 at the Event Hub, Eden Court, Bishops Road, Inverness, IV3 5SA. Mandatory — no Sunday morning registration. Bring photo ID. Combined with event expo, merchandise sales, Q&A sessions, and mechanical support.
Parking
Designated event parking in Inverness — details communicated in rider instructions. Arrive early. The start area is 0.6 miles from the Event Hub. Many riders walk or ride from accommodation.
RIDERS REPORT
When you sign up, put in an accurate predicted time as the starting pens are organized based on how quick you think you'll get around, so you end up in groups with riders of similar ability.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Etape Loch Ness?

+

Scottish Highlands in late April is a weather gamble that every rider must take seriously. Temperatures at the 06:00 start hover around 4°C (39°F), climbing to maybe 10-12°C by midday — this is not warm. Rain probability is significant, and the event mandates waterproof layers in the recommended kit list. Wind channeled through the Great Glen along Loch Ness can create biting headwinds on exposed sections. Riders have reported everything from glorious spring sunshine to driving rain and near-freezing wind chill. The event posts pre-ride weather advisories on social media. Climate is a genuine variable that separates prepared riders from miserable ones, but it rarely reaches the extreme conditions of winter Highland cycling.

How adventurous is Etape Loch Ness?

+

Loch Ness is one of the most recognizable natural landmarks on Earth, and cycling a complete 360-degree loop around it on closed roads is genuinely special. The route passes through the Great Glen — a geological fault line that bisects Scotland — with views of Urquhart Castle, the Caledonian Canal, and the Highland mountains rising from the loch shores. Fort Augustus at the halfway point offers a quintessential Highland village scene. The dawn start adds atmosphere as riders roll out into the early morning Highland light. But this is a well-supported sportive on closed public roads with support stations, mechanical assistance, and thousands of fellow riders — the adventure is scenic and atmospheric rather than wild or self-reliant.

Should I race Etape Loch Ness?

+

Yes, if riding closed roads around Loch Ness at dawn with 6,000 cyclists appeals to you as an experience. Yes, if you want a well-organized sportive that welcomes all abilities while still offering a legitimate climbing challenge. Skip if you need serious competitive racing depth — this is participatory. Skip if sub-100km on paved roads doesn't challenge you. And check the weather forecast before packing — Scottish April weather demands respect and proper kit.
