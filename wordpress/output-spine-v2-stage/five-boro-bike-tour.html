The Ratings

Course Profile
Logistics 4/5

48 years of organizational refinement for 32,000 riders. Staggered wave starts from 7:30 to 10:30 AM prevent bottlenecks at the Bowling Green start. Rest areas with snacks, entertainment, and free bike repair line the route. The Rider Identification Kit system (helmet cover, bib, bike plate) manages access cleanly. Wave assignments are color-coded and communicated via email. The only friction: navigating to Lower Manhattan on a Sunday morning, the sheer scale of 32,000 riders creating congestion at rest stops and bridge approaches, and the post-ride Staten Island Ferry wait that can stretch to 2+ hours in the early afternoon.

Editorial
Prestige 3/5

The largest cycling event in the United States and one of the largest in the world. Since 1977. Riders from 65 countries and all 50 states. The prestige is cultural and participatory, not athletic. Completing the Five Boro Bike Tour earns no competitive credentials — it earns bragging rights for experiencing New York City from the saddle. TD Bank title sponsorship and Bike New York's nonprofit mission add institutional weight.

Facts & History

On June 12, 1977, approximately 250 participants rode an 80-mile course through New York City as the Five Boro Challenge — originally conceived to provide training in bicycle safety for high school students. Then-Mayor Ed Koch championed the idea of a citywide bike tour, the distance was shortened, and the event grew into the TD Five Boro Bike Tour: the largest annual cycling event in the United States, organized by Bike New York to fund free cycling education programs across all five boroughs. Founded in 1977. By Bike New York (originally as the Five Boro Challenge).

Reputation: America's largest bike ride. 32,000 riders. Five boroughs. One day. The event that proves New York City can belong to cyclists.

The Course

Flat and festive. This is a city tour, not an athletic course. The 40 miles wind through all five boroughs on car-free streets, crossing five major bridges. The route begins in Lower Manhattan at Bowling Green, heads north via Sixth Avenue through Central Park, continues into Harlem and the Bronx via the Madison Avenue Bridge, re-enters Manhattan along the FDR Drive, crosses the Queensboro Bridge into Queens, traverses the Pulaski Bridge into Brooklyn, rides the Brooklyn-Queens Expressway and Gowanus Parkway, and culminates with the crossing of the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge into Staten Island. The Finish Festival at Fort Wadsworth and the free Staten Island Ferry ride back to Manhattan complete the experience.

Signature challenge: There is no physical challenge. The signature experience is the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge crossing — the only day of the year this bridge opens to cyclists. The longest suspension bridge in the US, with New York Harbor spread beneath you and the Manhattan skyline behind you.

Race Logistics

Airport
JFK, LaGuardia (LGA), or Newark (EWR) — all within 30-60 minutes of Lower Manhattan by transit.
Lodging
Manhattan hotels are convenient but expensive ($250-400/night). Brooklyn or Queens offer better value with easy subway access to Lower Manhattan. Book early — NYC hotels fill for any major event. Hostels and Airbnb are viable. Local riders: just take the subway.
Food
It's New York City. You have 27,000 restaurants. Pre-ride: grab a bagel. Rest areas along the route provide snacks. Post-ride: the Finish Festival on Staten Island has food trucks and vendors. Or take the ferry back to Manhattan and eat anywhere.
Packet Pickup
Pre-event Expo, typically at a Manhattan venue. Rider ID kits (helmet cover, bib, bike plate) can be shipped in advance for an additional fee — shipping deadline is usually mid-January.
Parking
Do not drive to this event. Take the subway. Seriously. Lower Manhattan on a Sunday morning with 32,000 cyclists converging is not a driving situation. MTA runs enhanced service for the event.
RIDERS REPORT
Start as early as possible and get close to the front because there are a lot of people.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at TD Five Boro Bike Tour?

+

Early May in New York is generally pleasant — 55-70F, spring conditions, occasional rain. The 2024 edition saw steady rain and 50s all day; 2025 was clear and warm. Neither scenario approaches extreme. The wind on the Verrazzano can bite, but this is a mild-weather urban ride. Climate is not a factor in preparation or performance.

How adventurous is TD Five Boro Bike Tour?

+

Riding a bicycle through car-free New York City streets is genuinely extraordinary. The FDR Drive — normally a six-lane expressway — becomes a cycling path along the East River. You ride through the interior of Central Park, pedal through Harlem and the Bronx, cross the Queensboro Bridge with the Manhattan skyline behind you, traverse Brooklyn on the BQE, and finish by crossing the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge with views of New York Harbor, the Statue of Liberty, and the towers of Lower Manhattan. For anyone who has ever been stuck in NYC traffic, cycling these roads in silence is borderline spiritual.

How prestigious is TD Five Boro Bike Tour?

+

Should I race TD Five Boro Bike Tour?

+

You don't 'race' the Five Boro Bike Tour — you experience it. Yes if you want the once-in-a-lifetime thrill of cycling car-free through all five NYC boroughs and crossing the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge. Yes if you want to ride alongside 32,000 people from 65 countries. Skip if you need athletic challenge, competitive racing, or technical terrain.
