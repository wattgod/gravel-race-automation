The Ratings

Course Profile
Logistics 3/5

Ngurah Rai International Airport (DPS) in Denpasar is Bali's gateway — a major hub for Southeast Asian carriers with direct flights from Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, Bangkok, Hong Kong, Tokyo, Sydney, and several European cities via Middle Eastern connections. From the US, you're looking at 20-30 hours of travel with one or two connections. Once on the island, ground transport to the Lebih/Klungkung area is 60-90 minutes depending on Bali's notorious traffic. The GFNY VIP package includes airport shuttles and hotel, which simplifies things significantly. Bike boxes are manageable through DPS. The logistics are not difficult, but they are long for Western riders.

Editorial
Prestige 3/5

GFNY Bali served as the GFNY Asia Championship from 2022-2025, giving it a legitimate title within the GFNY ecosystem. Six editions establish it as a proven, recurring event. The GFNY brand carries weight in the gran fondo world — professional timing, anti-doping, competitive classification, and World Championship qualification. But this is still a franchise satellite, not the flagship. The Asian Championship designation has moved to GFNY Belitung for 2026, which may reduce Bali's standing within the series. Prestige is earned through the GFNY infrastructure, not through decades of independent history.

Facts & History

GFNY Bali launched in 2019 as the franchise's Southeast Asian expansion play, leveraging Bali's global reputation as a tropical paradise and Indonesia's growing road cycling culture. The Island of the Gods offered what no other GFNY location could: a volcanic mountain climb through rice terraces and Hindu temples, combined with top-tier tourism infrastructure. The inaugural edition proved the concept — competitive racing on fully closed roads in one of the most photogenic settings in world cycling. COVID cancelled or disrupted 2020-2021, but the event returned strong in 2022 with the GFNY Asia Championship designation and a record 1,500 riders. The course has evolved across editions, with the start/finish shifting between Sayan Village (Ubud) and Lebih (Klungkung coast), but the Kintamani volcano ascent has remained the consistent centerpiece. In 2025, the 6th edition served as the opening event of the GFNY global season, cementing Bali's role as a flagship Asian location. For 2026, the GFNY Asia Championship title moves to a new GFNY Belitung event, but GFNY Bali appears set to continue as its own edition. Founded in 2019. By GFNY / Uli Fluhme (GFNY series founder), co-organized with local Indonesian partners.

Reputation: The most beautiful course in the GFNY calendar. A genuine climbing gran fondo in an exotic tropical setting that delivers adventure, culture, and physiological challenge in equal measure. Regionally prestigious as the GFNY Asia Championship (through 2025), but limited international field depth keeps it below the series' European and NYC events in competitive terms.

The Course

A point-to-summit-and-back volcanic mountain gran fondo that starts at Bali's coast, climbs relentlessly through the island's cultural and agricultural heartland to the rim of the Kintamani volcanic caldera, then descends back to the sea. The course is defined by one sustained ascent and one sustained descent, bookended by rolling coastal terrain. The climbing is not Alpine-steep but it is Alpine-long, and the tropical heat transforms moderate gradients into savage efforts. The Kintamani crater rim provides the emotional and physical summit — a view of Lake Danau Batur and Mount Batur that rewards every watt spent getting there.

Signature challenge: The Kintamani volcano ascent. A 25-30 km sustained climb from the lowland rice terraces to the volcanic crater rim at 1,500 meters, performed in equatorial tropical heat. The gradient is steady (4-6% average, 8-12% pitches) but the length and conditions make it brutal. This is where the race breaks apart every year — the lead group of 15-20 fragments to 5-7 by the summit. The crater rim view of Lake Danau Batur is the payoff: one of the most dramatic summit finishes (mid-course) in world gran fondo cycling.

MILE
Coastal Start — Lebih to Lowland Villages
The peloton rolls out from the Lebih coastal area at 6:30 AM, racing south of Semarapura in the Klungkung regency. The first 20 km are rolling terrain through lowland villages and rice paddies at near sea level. Temperatures are already 26-28C at dawn with high humidity. Groups form quickly as the road is relatively flat and wide. This is the calm before the climb — use this section to settle into a paceline and manage your core temperature before the gradient kicks in.
12
MILE
The Rice Terrace Climb — Ascending Through the Paddies
The road tilts upward as the course leaves the coastal plain and enters Bali's terraced agricultural zone. For the next 20 km, the gradient averages 3-5% through some of the most visually stunning landscape in world cycling: tiered emerald rice paddies cascading down volcanic hillsides, punctuated by Hindu temple compounds with stone-carved gates and ceremonial offerings. The climbing is steady rather than steep, but the tropical heat at this elevation (200-700m) is at its worst — hot, humid, and airless between the paddies. Pacing here determines whether you survive the Kintamani wall above.
31
MILE
Kintamani — The Volcano Wall
The main event. The road steepens to 6-12% as the course attacks the final 15-20 km to the Kintamani crater rim at approximately 1,500 meters elevation. The landscape shifts from rice terraces to denser jungle and volcanic terrain. The temperature drops noticeably — from 32C at the coast to 20-24C at the rim — but the humidity persists and the gradient is unrelenting. The climb to Penelokan-Kintamani splits the peloton every year: in 2024, only seven riders from a lead group of 17 survived to the summit together. When you crest the rim and the caldera opens up — Lake Danau Batur shimmering below the cone of Mount Batur — you have earned one of cycling's great views.
47
MILE
The Descent — Crater Rim to Ubud
The descent from Kintamani back toward the coast is fast, technical, and potentially dangerous. Steep switchbacks through mountain villages demand confident bike handling, especially if wet-season rain has arrived. The road passes through the Ubud area — Bali's cultural capital — where narrow village streets, temple walls, and the famous Monkey Forest create a sensory overload at speed. Traffic management is strong (GFNY police support is extensive), but the descent requires full attention. The temperature climbs rapidly as you lose altitude, and the heat hits like a wall when you reach the lowlands.
62
MILE
The Coastal Return — Rolling Heat to Finish
The final 30+ km back to the Lebih coast are rolling terrain in the worst heat of the day. After 100 km including a volcano climb and technical descent, the flat-to-undulating coastal roads feel far harder than they look. The equatorial sun is directly overhead, shade is minimal, and dehydration accumulated during the climb now manifests as power loss and cramping. The front of the field races this section; everyone else survives it. The finish line at the coast closes the elevation loop — volcano summit to sea level — and delivers you to the post-race buffet.
RIDERS REPORT
Gage Hill
The second climb is 3.5 kilometers long with 5.2% average gradient and maximum of 8.9%. Has three ramps at beginning, middle, and end that all reach nearly 9%. You come into it from a sharp right-hand turn with almost no speed, so you need gears ready. This is where attacks start going everywhere and where last year's winners made their move.
Cheese Cup
Immediately follows Gage Hill after a left-hand turn and small fast descent. It's essentially two climbs in one hard section of the race.
Gage Hill is described as a power climb with three challenging ramps that repeatedly hit you when you think the worst is over.

Race Logistics

Airport
Ngurah Rai International Airport (DPS) in Denpasar — Bali's sole airport and a major Southeast Asian hub. Direct flights from Singapore (~2.5 hrs), Kuala Lumpur (~3 hrs), Sydney (~6 hrs), Tokyo (~7 hrs), and several Middle Eastern hubs connecting Europe. From the US, connections via Tokyo, Singapore, or Doha are typical, with total travel times of 20-30 hours.
Lodging
The start/finish area near Lebih in Klungkung regency is the practical base. Budget guesthouses from $30-50/night are plentiful. Mid-range hotels $50-120/night. The GFNY VIP package includes accommodation at a partner hotel (historically The Mansion in Ubud). Ubud — Bali's cultural capital — is 30-40 minutes from the start and offers top-tier hotels, restaurants, and cultural attractions. Beachside resorts in Sanur or Seminyak are 60-90 minutes away and may appeal for post-race vacation days. Book 2-4 weeks in advance for race weekend.
Food
Bali's food scene is exceptional and affordable. Warungs (local restaurants) serve nasi goreng, satay, gado-gado, and fresh seafood for $2-5 per meal. Ubud offers international cuisine, vegan restaurants, and high-end dining. Pre-race carb-loading is easy with abundant rice and noodle dishes. Hydration strategy is critical — start loading fluids 48 hours before the race and supplement with electrolytes in the tropical heat. Coconut water is everywhere and makes an excellent natural hydration option.
Packet Pickup
Race expo and packet pickup typically held Friday-Saturday before the race at the start area or host hotel. Check the official GFNY Bali site for exact hours and location each year. Riders must pick up packets before race day.
Parking
Not a major concern — most riders take taxi, Grab (ride-hailing), or GFNY shuttle to the start. Bali traffic can be severe, especially around Denpasar. Plan extra time for ground transport on race morning. The GFNY VIP package includes organized transport to the start.

Drink and eat before Gage Hill climb. Be aware of what's happening around you as attacks start going everywhere from every rider left in the group.
Save energy for after Gage Hill because there's another climb (Cheese Cup) coming immediately, plus lots of rollers after this section.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at GFNY Bali?

+

Racing at 8 degrees south of the equator is a physiological event unto itself. Sea-level temperatures of 30-32C with 80%+ humidity at the start give way to a moderate 20-24C at the Kintamani crater rim, then plummet back to furnace conditions on the coastal return. February is Bali's wet season — monsoon rain is a real possibility, particularly on the afternoon descent. The combination of extreme heat at low elevation, rapid temperature changes with altitude, and the omnipresent humidity creates conditions that European and North American riders are profoundly unprepared for. Your cooling system fails before your muscles do.

How are the logistics for GFNY Bali?

+

Ngurah Rai International Airport (DPS) in Denpasar is Bali's gateway — a major hub for Southeast Asian carriers with direct flights from Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, Bangkok, Hong Kong, Tokyo, Sydney, and several European cities via Middle Eastern connections. From the US, you're looking at 20-30 hours of travel with one or two connections. The GFNY VIP package includes airport shuttles and hotel, which simplifies things significantly. Bike boxes are manageable through DPS. The logistics are not difficult, but they are long for Western riders.

How adventurous is GFNY Bali?

+

This is where GFNY Bali earns its distinction. The course climbs from a black-sand volcanic beach through emerald rice terraces carved into hillsides over centuries, past Hindu temples draped in offerings and incense, through jungle corridors alive with monkeys, to the rim of an active volcanic crater overlooking Lake Danau Batur. Then you descend back through it all. The Island of the Gods is not marketing hyperbole — Bali's landscape is genuinely one of the most visually stunning places on earth to ride a bicycle. No European sportive, no American gran fondo, no Australian ride can replicate this combination of volcanic geology, tropical ecology, and living spiritual culture experienced from the saddle.

+

GFNY Bali served as the GFNY Asia Championship from 2022-2025, giving it a legitimate title within the GFNY ecosystem. Six editions establish it as a proven, recurring event. The GFNY brand carries weight in the gran fondo world — professional timing, anti-doping, competitive classification, and World Championship qualification. But this is still a franchise satellite, not the flagship. The Asian Championship designation has moved to GFNY Belitung for 2026, which may reduce Bali's standing within the series. Prestige is earned through the GFNY infrastructure, not through decades of independent history.

Should I race GFNY Bali?

+

Yes if you want a top-tier adventure cycling experience wrapped in a GFNY competitive framework. Yes if volcanic terrain, tropical conditions, and Balinese culture sound like the ride of a lifetime. Yes if you're based in the Asia-Pacific region and want a serious climbing gran fondo with professional organization. Skip if field depth matters to you — the competitive front is thin. Skip if you can't handle sustained climbing in equatorial heat. Skip if 20-30 hours of travel from the Americas is a dealbreaker.
