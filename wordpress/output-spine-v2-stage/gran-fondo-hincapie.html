Facts & History

Gran Fondo Hincapie was born in 2012, the year George Hincapie retired from professional cycling after one of the most decorated careers in American road cycling history. George had started 17 Tours de France — more than any American ever — and was the only rider to support Lance Armstrong through all seven of his Tour de France victories as a member of US Postal and Discovery Channel. He also domestiqued for Tour winners Alberto Contador (2007) and Cadel Evans (2011). George won a Tour de France stage in 2005, claimed Gent-Wevelgem in 2001, and earned three US Professional Road Race titles. When he retired, he and brother Rich created the Gran Fondo as a way to share the Blue Ridge roads where George had trained for France — routes radiating from Hotel Domestique, the luxury boutique hotel the Hincapie family built in Travelers Rest. What started as a single Greenville event has grown into a five-event national series spanning Greenville, Chattanooga, Lehigh Valley, Bentonville, and Merced. Founded in 2012. By George and Rich Hincapie.

Reputation: The most legitimate celebrity gran fondo in North America. George Hincapie's name carries genuine weight in the cycling world, and the Blue Ridge terrain delivers authentic suffering that justifies the prestige. The Hurricane Helene relief response cemented the event's reputation as more than branded cycling tourism.

2012: Inaugural edition from Hotel Domestique — George Hincapie's retirement celebration ride
2015: Event establishes itself as the Southeast's premier gran fondo with growing national draw
2020: Held during the pandemic as one of the first cycling events permitted — modified COVID-safe format
2024: Hurricane Helene devastates Polk County three weeks before the event; organizers pivot from cancellation to a modified 19-mile loop format, raising $750,000+ for relief. Cavendish, Wiggins, and Ullrich ride to support the cause
2025: 14th edition draws 2,000+ riders and earns 'Best Charity Fondo in North America' recognition
2026: 15th anniversary edition scheduled for October 17 — courses may be subject to change due to lingering Hurricane Helene road damage

The Course

George Hincapie trained on these roads for 17 Tours de France, and the Gran route makes you understand why. The 83-mile course from Hotel Domestique punches north into the Blue Ridge foothills, crossing into North Carolina's Polk County for the signature climbs before looping back to the Travelers Rest finish. Three timed segments — Skyuka Mountain, Green River Cove, and the final climb to the finish — structure the suffering around sustained, gradient-heavy climbing. Between the major climbs, rolling Blue Ridge countryside and Lake Road's technical turns keep the legs honest. The autumn foliage through dense forest canopy creates postcard scenery that's easy to miss when you're redlined on Skyuka's 8.7% gradient. This is a road event on paved surfaces throughout, but the mountainous character, the altitude variation from 1,100 ft to nearly 3,000 ft, and the relentless vertical accumulation make it one of the hardest gran fondos in the Eastern United States.

Signature challenge: Skyuka Mountain. Four miles at 8.7% average gradient, 1,814 feet of vertical gain, a summit at 2,967 feet, and a technical descent so steep that carbon clincher rim brakes are banned from the course. This is where the Gran Fondo Hincapie earns the right to carry a Tour de France veteran's name.

MILE
Hotel Domestique to Lake Road
The Gran rolls out from Hotel Domestique through the Blue Ridge foothills. The early miles are rolling, allowing groups to form and settle into rhythm. Lake Road arrives with its twisting turns through the forest — Cat4Garage noted 'Lake Road twisties' as a key early feature. The pack is dense here, and the turns demand attention. Crashes can happen in the early chaos of 2,000+ riders finding their pace.
35
MILE
Skyuka Mountain
The first timed climb and the signature suffering of the Gran Fondo Hincapie. Skyuka Mountain is 4 miles at 8.7% average gradient with 1,814 feet of elevation gain. The climb features waterfalls, a lake, and mountain scenery that you'll only appreciate if you have oxygen to spare. The summit at 2,967 feet marks the high point of the ride. The descent is where things get genuinely dangerous — steep switchbacks with poor road surface in places. Carbon clincher rim brake wheels are banned for this reason. fljones3 describes the 'switchbacks coming down the first climb are technical.' This descent has separated the cautious from the reckless for 15 years.
55
MILE
Green River Cove
The second timed segment takes riders through the switchbacks of Green River Cove in Polk County, North Carolina. These are the same switchback roads that George Hincapie used to build the climbing engine that drove nine Tour de France champions to Paris. The cove walls close in, the gradient kicks up, and the accumulated fatigue from Skyuka starts extracting its toll. The communities of Saluda, Columbus, and Tryon have hosted riders through these roads for 13 years — the same communities the event raised $750,000 for after Hurricane Helene.
75
MILE
The Final Climb
The third timed segment — the climb back to Hotel Domestique. By mile 75, you've accumulated 7,000+ feet of climbing, and the legs that felt strong on Lake Road now feel like concrete. The final climb is shorter than Skyuka but comes at the worst possible moment. fljones3 has 'attempted the gran 3-4x' but 'only completed it once' — this is where the cutoff claims those who went too deep on the earlier climbs. Adoliner called the event 'still one of the hardest I've ever done' and this final push back to the finish is why.

Race Logistics

Airport
Greenville-Spartanburg International (GSP) — 30 minutes to Travelers Rest. Strong domestic connections, reasonable fares. Charlotte Douglas International (CLT) is 90 minutes and offers more flight options.
Lodging
Hotel Domestique (10 Road of Vines, Travelers Rest) is race HQ — luxury boutique hotel founded by George Hincapie in the Blue Ridge foothills. Premium pricing but unbeatable proximity. Downtown Greenville (10 miles south) offers a full range of hotels at various price points. Embassy Suites downtown connects directly to Travelers Rest via the Swamp Rabbit Trail. Book 2-3 months ahead — the event weekend fills local lodging. Airbnb options in Travelers Rest and northern Greenville County provide alternatives.
Food
Greenville's food scene punches well above its weight for a mid-sized Southern city. Phil Gaimon highlighted Tandem Crepery ('Get there early — long line'), Methodical Coffee, and the Commons along the Swamp Rabbit Trail. Downtown Greenville's Main Street offers extensive dining. The event weekend includes a Michelin-starred celebrity chef dinner (Chef Matthew Accarrino) and a post-ride festival with complimentary food and beverages for riders and families.
Packet Pickup
Day before at Hotel Domestique. Combined with event expo and social activities. On-site registration available at a premium tier with reduced swag.
Parking
Hotel Domestique and designated event parking areas in Travelers Rest. The Swamp Rabbit Trail provides car-free bike access from downtown Greenville to the start.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Gran Fondo Hincapie?

+

October in Greenville averages highs of 74 degrees F and lows of 51 degrees F — theoretically ideal cycling weather. But the Southeast doesn't play by averages. The 2025 event saw 'sunny and warm' conditions that forced 'many people having to take extra hydration and salts on board to cope with the heat.' South Carolina humidity persists well into autumn, and sustained climbing in direct sun on exposed mountain roads amplifies thermal stress. Morning starts in the cool 50s lull you into complacency before afternoon heat on the third timed climb punishes the unprepared.

How adventurous is Gran Fondo Hincapie?

+

CaptainThunderpants rode 'some of the prettiest roads I've ever ridden' on George Hincapie's actual Tour de France training grounds through Blue Ridge countryside. The route traverses the border between South Carolina and North Carolina, passing through the mountain towns of Saluda, Columbus, and Tryon in Polk County. Waterfalls, lakes, dense forest canopy, and autumn foliage create genuine scenic beauty. But these are established roads through populated areas — the adventure is scenic rather than wild, and you're never far from civilization.

Should I race Gran Fondo Hincapie?

+

Yes if riding a Tour de France domestique's personal training roads alongside cycling legends appeals to you. Yes if you want a legitimate 83-mile, 8,100-foot climbing challenge wrapped in a luxury cycling weekend. Skip if you need competitive racing depth — this is participatory, not a race. Skip if premium pricing for a branded experience isn't your thing.
