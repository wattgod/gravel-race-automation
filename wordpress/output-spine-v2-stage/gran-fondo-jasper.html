The Ratings

Course Profile
Logistics 3/5

Jasper is remote — stunningly beautiful, but remote. The nearest major airport is Edmonton International (YEG), a 3.5-4 hour drive west through the Rockies on Highway 16. There is no commercial airport in Jasper. VIA Rail's Canadian service connects Jasper to Edmonton, Vancouver, and Toronto, but schedules are infrequent and unreliable. Once in Jasper, the townsite is compact and navigable. The event start and finish are in town, and the routes radiate outward on national park highways. Packet pickup and event logistics are well-organized after 10 years of operation. Lodging in a national park town is limited and books fast — Jasper has fewer than 5,000 permanent residents and lost 30% of its structures in the 2024 wildfire. The 2026 event warns of potential course adjustments due to ongoing fire mitigation and reconstruction work.

Editorial
Prestige 2/5

Gran Fondo Jasper is well-known in Canadian cycling circles and carries the unique distinction of being Canada's only fondo inside a national park. The Marmot Basin climb gained WorldTour credibility when it appeared as Stage 4 of the 2015 Tour of Alberta, won by Tom-Jelte Slagter (Cannondale-Garmin). MNP, a Canadian accounting and advisory firm, is the title sponsor. But internationally, this event has limited name recognition. The field of 300+ riders is modest. It lacks the brand-name athlete associations of events like Hincapie or the competitive draw of Gran Fondo Stelvio. The prestige is scenic and locational rather than competitive or institutional.

Facts & History

Gran Fondo Jasper launched in 2014 as Canada's first and only gran fondo held entirely within a national park. The event was born from a simple recognition: Jasper National Park contains some of the most spectacular road cycling terrain in North America — the Icefields Parkway, Marmot Basin Road, and the mountain highways of the Canadian Rockies — yet no organized event existed to showcase it. The Marmot Basin climb had already earned recognition as Alberta's top categorized road climb from Canadian Cycling Magazine. When the 2015 Tour of Alberta brought WorldTour professionals to race up Marmot Basin as Stage 4 (won by Tom-Jelte Slagter of Cannondale-Garmin), the climb's pedigree was confirmed at the highest level. The event grew steadily through the 2010s, establishing partnerships with MNP as title sponsor and the Jasper Yellowhead Historical Society as charity partner. The July 2024 wildfire that destroyed 30% of Jasper's structures — 358 properties and an estimated $1.2 billion in insured damage — tested the event's resilience. The 2025 edition proceeded with potential course modifications, and the 2026 10th anniversary edition represents a statement of community recovery. Founded in 2014. By Gran Fondo Jasper organizing committee.

Reputation: The definitive national park cycling experience in Canada. Known for top-tier scenery rather than competitive depth. The Marmot Basin climb carries WorldTour credibility from the 2015 Tour of Alberta. The post-wildfire editions carry emotional weight as part of Jasper's community recovery.

The Course

Every route option starts and finishes in Jasper townsite, radiating outward through the Canadian Rockies like spokes from a hub. The signature Gran Fondo (165 km) heads west on Connaught Drive, picks up the Icefields Parkway, then turns onto Highway 93A before attacking Marmot Basin — a 13-14 km climb averaging 5.4-5.8% to the ski resort base lodge, ranked Alberta's #1 categorized climb. After descending Marmot, the route continues south on the Icefields Parkway toward Poboktan Creek, where glaciers and 3,000 m peaks frame the turnaround before riders retrace north to Jasper. The Forte Fondo (190 km) adds Edith Cavell Road — 19 km of narrow, winding switchbacks climbing to the Mount Edith Cavell Wilderness Hostel with views of Angel Glacier — between Marmot and the Icefields Parkway push. The Leg Burner (126 km) reaches Athabasca Falls before routing south to Goatlick on the Parkway. The Medio (100 km) includes Marmot Basin and rolling 93A terrain. The Piccolo (65 km) loops through 93A without major climbs. Throughout every distance, you are inside a UNESCO World Heritage national park surrounded by the Canadian Rockies — elk on the roadside, glaciers on the horizon, and mountain air that tastes different from anywhere else.

Signature challenge: Marmot Basin Climb. Alberta's #1 categorized road climb per Canadian Cycling Magazine. 13-14 km at 5.4-5.8% average gradient with 770-828 m of elevation gain. Maximum gradient 9.3%. Featured as Stage 4 of the 2015 Tour of Alberta, won by WorldTour rider Tom-Jelte Slagter. The climb winds through subalpine forest to the Marmot Basin ski resort base lodge, with panoramic views of the Athabasca Valley from the upper reaches. Steady, scenic, and relentless — the climb that defines the event.

MILE
Jasper Townsite to Highway 93A
The neutral rollout from Jasper townsite onto Connaught Drive and the Icefields Parkway before turning south onto Highway 93A. The legs are fresh, the mountain air is crisp at 1,060 m elevation, and the Rockies are waking up around you. Groups form naturally. The road is wide and well-paved. This is the calm before Marmot.
20
MILE
Marmot Basin Climb
The turn onto Marmot Basin Road marks the beginning of the event's signature effort. Alberta's #1 categorized climb: 13-14 km averaging 5.4-5.8% with maximum gradients reaching 9.3%. The road climbs through dense subalpine forest toward the Marmot Basin ski resort base lodge. The majority of the climbing sits in the 5-7% range with a relatively flat 2 km section early on. In 2015, WorldTour riders from the Tour of Alberta raced this same climb. The final 1.5 km may include a short dirt section before the turnaround. The views from the upper reaches — looking back across the Athabasca Valley — are the reward for the suffering.
50
MILE
Edith Cavell Road (Forte Only)
Only for the 75 Forte Fondo riders: the turn onto Edith Cavell Road after descending Marmot. This is 19 km of narrow, winding road climbing at 3.4% average to the Mount Edith Cavell Wilderness Hostel. Tight switchbacks, minimal shoulders, and heavy tourist traffic make this the most technically demanding road on the course. The payoff is approaching one of the Rockies' most iconic peaks, with Angel Glacier visible at the turnaround. The 696 m of elevation gain here is what separates the Forte from the Gran.
75
MILE
Icefields Parkway South to Poboktan Creek
The long push south on the Icefields Parkway toward Poboktan Creek. This section is rolling rather than climbing — the gradient is gentle but relentless. The scenery is top-tier: glaciers cling to peaks above the tree line, turquoise rivers cut through valley floors, and mountain goats pick their way across distant slopes. Athabasca Falls thunders to the west at the 30 km mark south of Jasper. By Poboktan Creek (~1,540 m), the accumulated fatigue from Marmot (and Edith Cavell for Forte riders) starts compounding. The turnaround reveals the challenge ahead: 80+ km back to Jasper, likely into a headwind.
95
MILE
The Return North — Headwind Home
The return leg on the Icefields Parkway and Highway 93A back to Jasper. Prevailing winds in the Athabasca Valley often blow from the north, meaning the return can feature sustained headwinds that turn a gentle rolling road into a grinding slog. By this point, Gran Fondo riders have 120+ km in the legs. Forte riders have 150+ km and two major climbs behind them. The scenery is still magnificent — you are still inside Jasper National Park — but the focus narrows from awe to survival. The final approach into Jasper townsite, with the familiar peaks of the Colin Range welcoming you home, is the emotional climax.

Race Logistics

Airport
Edmonton International Airport (YEG) — 3.5-4 hour drive west via Highway 16. The only realistic major airport. Calgary (YYC) is 4.5+ hours via the Icefields Parkway or Highway 11. VIA Rail connects Edmonton and Vancouver to Jasper but schedules are infrequent.
Lodging
Book early — Jasper is a small national park town (~4,800 residents) with limited lodging that books months ahead for June. The 2024 wildfire destroyed 358 properties, further constraining supply. Options include Fairmont Jasper Park Lodge (luxury, $400+ CAD/night), mid-range hotels on Connaught Drive ($200-350 CAD), hostels (HI Jasper, $40-80 CAD), and Parks Canada campgrounds ($25-40 CAD, reserve at reservation.pc.gc.ca). Hinton, Alberta (45 min east on Hwy 16) offers significantly cheaper lodging as a budget alternative. Airbnb supply in Jasper is minimal post-wildfire.
Food
Jasper townsite has a range of restaurants from casual to upscale — Fiddle River (seafood), Evil Dave's Grill, and The Raven Bistro are local favorites. Bear's Paw Bakery is the essential pre-ride breakfast stop. Grocery options include Robinson's IGA and a small Safeway — national park pricing applies. Post-ride options cluster on Patricia Street and Connaught Drive. For the ride itself, pack nutrition aggressively — the Icefields Parkway has no services between aid stations.
Packet Pickup
Day before race in Jasper townsite. Combined with event check-in and pre-race activities.
Parking
Designated event parking in Jasper townsite. The compact town is walkable from most lodging to the start/finish area.
RIDERS REPORT
Prioritize reliability over performance when traveling - bring your most reliable bike setup rather than the highest-end components to avoid mechanical issues far from home.
Hot wax your chain before racing in dry, sandy conditions for better durability - can last up to 1000km without reapplication.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Gran Fondo Jasper?

+

June in the Canadian Rockies is a double-edged sword. The 17 hours of daylight and 18-21°C afternoon temperatures create near-perfect cycling conditions when the sun cooperates. But June is Jasper's wettest month — 117 mm of rainfall spread across 14-17 rainy days. Mountain weather changes in minutes: sunshine at the Jasper start can turn to cold rain at Poboktan Creek 80 km south. Morning temperatures of 4-9°C at the start line demand layering decisions. The Icefields Parkway corridor at higher elevation is colder and windier than town. Riders returning north from Poboktan Creek commonly face headwinds. Not hostile, but unpredictable — you must prepare for all four seasons in a single ride.

How adventurous is Gran Fondo Jasper?

+

This is the maximum adventure score for a road event. You are riding through a UNESCO World Heritage Site national park in the Canadian Rockies. The Icefields Parkway is consistently ranked among the world's most beautiful roads — glaciers, turquoise lakes, waterfalls, and 3,000 m peaks visible throughout. Marmot Basin climbs toward the treeline with panoramic mountain views. Edith Cavell Road approaches one of the park's most iconic peaks with its Angel Glacier. Wildlife encounters are not hypothetical — bears, elk, mountain goats, and bighorn sheep are routinely spotted on the course. Athabasca Falls is a thundering landmark waypoint. The 2024 wildfire has added a raw, post-apocalyptic dimension to parts of the landscape. No other road fondo in North America offers this combination of wilderness, scenery, and genuine wildness.

Should I race Gran Fondo Jasper?

+

Yes if riding through a UNESCO World Heritage national park in the Canadian Rockies is your idea of a perfect day on a bike. Yes if the Icefields Parkway, glaciers, and mountain wildlife matter more to you than competitive depth or field size. Skip if the 4-hour drive from Edmonton and limited Jasper lodging is a dealbreaker. Skip if you need a large, competitive field — this is 300 riders enjoying mountains, not 3,000 racing each other.
