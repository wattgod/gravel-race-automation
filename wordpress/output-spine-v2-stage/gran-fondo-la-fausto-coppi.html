The Ratings

Course Profile
Logistics 3/5

Cuneo is the capital of the Granda Province in southern Piedmont, positioned on a plateau at 550m between the Maritime Alps and the Piedmont plain. It is a handsome, walkable city with Piazza Galimberti as its grand centerpiece — and the start/finish line. Cuneo-Levaldigi Airport (CUF) is 23 km away but has limited connections. Turin-Caselle Airport (TRN) is 1.5 hours by car with better European coverage. Nice (NCE) in France is 2 hours through the Tenda tunnel. Milan-Malpensa (MXP) is 3 hours. Accommodation in Cuneo is adequate but not abundant for event weekend — book 2-4 months ahead. The single-location loop from Piazza Galimberti eliminates transfer logistics. A well-run event for 37 editions, but the limited airport access and smaller city infrastructure keep logistics at 3.

Facts & History

Fausto Coppi was born in Castellania, near Alessandria, on September 15, 1919. He became the greatest Italian cyclist of the twentieth century — Il Campionissimo, the Champion of Champions. Five Giro d'Italia victories (1940, 1947, 1949, 1952, 1953), two Tour de France triumphs (1949, 1952), the World Championship (1953), Milan-San Remo three times, the Giro di Lombardia five times, and the hour record. He was the first man to win the Giro and the Tour in the same year, and he did it twice. He died of undiagnosed malaria on January 2, 1960, at age 40. In 1987, the cycling community of Cuneo — at the foot of the Maritime Alps where the road to Colle Fauniera begins — created a granfondo in his name. The route was built around the Fauniera, a 22.3 km monster of a climb that rises to 2,481 meters through wild, remote mountain terrain. The event grew from a regional tribute into one of Italy's premier international granfondos, attracting riders from over 40 countries. In 1999, Marco Pantani placed a legendary attack on the Fauniera during the Giro d'Italia; after Pantani's death in 2004, a memorial was erected at the summit, creating an extraordinary intersection of cycling history — Coppi's name on the event, Pantani's spirit at the summit. Founded in 1987. By ASD Fausto Coppi on the Road (Cuneo cycling association).

Reputation: One of the five greatest granfondos in Italy. Named after the foundational legend of Italian cycling, built around one of the country's most demanding mountain climbs, and crowned with the memorial to cycling's most tragic modern hero. The combination of Coppi's name, the Fauniera's brutality, and Pantani's ghost at the summit gives this event a historical weight that few cycling events in the world can match.

The Course

A mountain marathon that begins in the elegant city of Cuneo and ventures deep into the Maritime Alps before returning. The route is defined by three major climbs — Colle di Sampeyre, Colle Fauniera, and Madonna del Colletto — connected by rolling valley roads and demanding descents. The first 20 km are flat, rolling through the Piedmont countryside. Then the road tilts upward and does not genuinely flatten again until the return approach to Cuneo. The Granfondo traverses three distinct alpine valleys — the Varaita, the Maira, and the Grana — each with its own character, before the final sting of Madonna del Colletto delivers the last 7.3 km of climbing. The terrain transitions from refined Piedmont civilization to raw Alpine wilderness and back again over 172 km.

Signature challenge: Colle Fauniera: 22.3 km of climbing to 2,481 meters, with the upper half averaging 9.2% on deteriorating road surfaces through increasingly remote and wild Alpine terrain. The combination of sustained gradient, altitude, rough roads, and the psychological weight of the Pantani memorial at the summit makes the Fauniera the defining challenge of the event — and one of the most demanding single climbs accessible by gran fondo anywhere in Europe.

25
MILE
Colle di Sampeyre — The First Wall
After 20 km of flat approach through the Piedmont countryside and the transitional Colletta di Rossana, the route enters Valle Varaita and begins the 16 km sustained ascent of Colle di Sampeyre. Rising to 2,284m, this is a long, steady climb with regular gradients that test pacing discipline early in the day. The terrain shifts from cultivated valley floor to alpine meadows as the altitude builds. Sampeyre is the gateway — a serious climb in its own right, but a prelude to what follows.
48
MILE
Sampeyre Descent + Valle Maira Transfer
The descent from Colle di Sampeyre into Valle Maira is 17.7 km of fast, technical mountain road with tight switchbacks and variable surfaces. Speed builds quickly on the descent, and concentration is critical. The valley transfer through Maira is brief respite — rolling terrain that allows legs to recover before the main event begins. The approach to the Fauniera from the Grana Valley marks the psychological turning point of the day.
58
MILE
Colle Fauniera — The King of the Mountains
The defining challenge. 22.3 km of climbing from the Grana Valley to the summit at 2,481 meters. The first 8 km average a deceptive 5-6% through the lower valley. Then the road steepens dramatically: the upper 14 km average 9.2% with pitches reaching 14-15%. The road narrows, the surface deteriorates, and the landscape becomes increasingly wild and remote. Above 2,000m, the vegetation thins to alpine scrub and rock. The final kilometers are the steepest — grinding ramps on rough asphalt at altitude, where reduced oxygen compounds the physical demand. At the summit: the Marco Pantani memorial, erected in 2004, stands at 2,481 meters overlooking the Maritime Alps. This is where Pantani attacked during the 1999 Giro. This is where Coppi's name meets Pantani's legend.
87
MILE
Madonna del Colletto — The Final Sting
After the descent from the Fauniera and the transition through the lower valley, Madonna del Colletto arrives at approximately km 143 — a 7.3 km climb with gradients reaching 13%. On fresh legs, this would be a moderate effort. After 140+ km and 3,500+ meters of accumulated climbing, it is a test of everything that remains. The steeper pitches above 10% on hollowed-out legs separate those who paced correctly from those who spent too much on the Fauniera. The summit at 1,304m is the last high point before the descent to Cuneo.
100
MILE
The Return to Piazza Galimberti
The descent from Madonna del Colletto merges into the Cuneo approach — rolling to flat terrain as the Maritime Alps recede behind. The final kilometers into the city are fast and flat, returning to Piazza Galimberti where the day began. The grand square, the arcaded buildings, the cheering crowd — the civilization at the finish provides a sharp contrast to the wild isolation of the Fauniera summit. You started in a piazza; you climbed to 2,481 meters; you return to the piazza.

Race Logistics

Airport
Cuneo-Levaldigi (CUF) — 23 km (closest, but very limited flight connections). Turin-Caselle (TRN) — 1.5 hours (best practical option for European flights). Nice (NCE) — 2 hours via Tenda tunnel (alternative with wider international connections). Milan-Malpensa (MXP) — 3 hours (widest international connections).
Lodging
Stay in Cuneo — the start and finish are at Piazza Galimberti in the city center. The Cuneo Hotel is 600m from the station and meters from the start line. Hotels and B&Bs in the city center run EUR80-150/night for event weekend. Book 2-4 months in advance. Cuneo is a smaller city than Turin or Milan, so accommodation options are limited but the city itself is elegant and walkable. The Piedmont Bike Hotel near Alba (20 km south) caters specifically to cyclists with bike storage, guides, and Pinarello rentals. For a more rural experience, agriturismi in the Cuneo countryside offer excellent value and Piedmont ambiance.
Food
Piedmont is one of Italy's greatest culinary regions. Agnolotti del plin (hand-pinched stuffed pasta), tajarin al ragu, vitello tonnato, bagna cauda, and the renowned cheeses of the Langhe. Cuneo is famous for its chocolate — the cuneesi al rhum are a local icon. The region produces Barolo, Barbaresco, and Nebbiolo wines. Pre-race carb loading in Piedmont is an experience unto itself. Post-race, the trattorias around Piazza Galimberti serve honest Piemontese cooking that rewards every calorie burned on the Fauniera.
Packet Pickup
Event village at Piazza Galimberti or nearby expo area, 1-2 days before the race. The Cuneo Bike Festival typically runs alongside event weekend with ancillary programming and cycling expo. Registration desk opens at 5:45 AM on race day; grid opens at 6:15 AM.
Parking
Cuneo manages event parking in designated areas around the city center. Piazza Galimberti and surrounding streets are closed for the start. Walking to the start from central accommodation is the simplest approach. The city's position on a plateau between the Stura and Gesso rivers means approach roads are limited — follow event signage.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Granfondo La Fausto Coppi?

+

Late June in Piedmont delivers warm but not oppressive conditions at valley level — 25-27C highs in Cuneo with comfortable morning start temperatures around 17-19C. The challenge is the altitude differential. The Colle Fauniera summit at 2,481m can be 15C cooler than the valley, meaning riders transition from warm approach roads to near-Alpine conditions at the top. Afternoon convective storms over the Maritime Alps are a real risk — lightning on the exposed upper reaches of the Fauniera is dangerous. The weather is not extreme enough to define the event (unlike a desert or Arctic race), but the temperature range from valley to summit requires layering strategy, and the storm risk adds unpredictability.

How adventurous is Granfondo La Fausto Coppi?

+

The Colle Fauniera is one of the most remote and dramatic road climbs in the Alps. The upper reaches are narrow, isolated, and wind through a landscape that feels genuinely wild — sparse vegetation, rocky terrain, and sweeping views across the Maritime Alps toward the French border. At the summit, the Marco Pantani memorial stands as a testament to one of cycling's most beloved and tragic figures — erected in 2004, months after his death, on the pass where he placed his devastating attack during the 1999 Giro d'Italia. The route traverses three distinct mountain valleys — the Varaita, the Maira, and the Grana — each with its own character. Stone villages, alpine meadows, chestnut forests, and the dramatic contrast between the refined city of Cuneo and the raw Alpine wilderness above 2,000m create an adventure that is both civilized and wild.

Should I race Granfondo La Fausto Coppi?

+

Yes if you want genuine Alpine climbing — 4,300m of vertical including a summit at 2,481m — in a historically significant Italian granfondo. Yes if the names Fausto Coppi and Marco Pantani mean something to you and you want to ride the mountain where their legacies intersect. Yes if you are a strong climber looking for a demanding one-day event that rivals the Dolomite granfondos in vertical gain. Skip if you need polished road surfaces — the upper Fauniera is rough and remote. Skip if the limited airport access to Cuneo is a dealbreaker. Skip if 4,300m of climbing in 172 km exceeds your current fitness.
