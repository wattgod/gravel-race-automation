Course Profile
Logistics 4/5

Bergamo is one of the best-connected mid-size cities in northern Italy. Bergamo Orio al Serio airport (BGY) — the event's title sponsor — is 5 km from the start line, and it serves budget carriers across Europe (Ryanair's Italian hub). Milan Malpensa (MXP) is 1 hour, Milan Linate (LIN) 50 minutes, both with global connections. The start and finish are in the same location in central Bergamo, near the Gewiss Stadium. Bergamo itself is a manageable city with good accommodation options at Italian (not Milanese) prices. Thirty years of organizational experience, FCI calendar status, and professional event management from the same organizing committee make race-day logistics polished and predictable.

Editorial
Prestige 4/5

Named after Felice Gimondi — born in Sedrina, 10 km from Bergamo, one of only seven cyclists in history to win all three Grand Tours (Tour 1965, Giro 1967/69/76, Vuelta 1968). The Bianchi co-branding (Bianchi's factory is in Treviglio, just outside Bergamo) adds heritage weight. The event has run continuously since 1996, is a permanent fixture on the FCI Serie A granfondo calendar, and has attracted 70,000+ cumulative riders. Since Gimondi's death in 2019, the event also honors all seven Triple Crown winners. In Italian cycling culture, this is deeply respected. Globally, it lacks the Monument brand power of Paris-Roubaix or the Tour de France association of L'Etape, but among those who know their cycling history, Gimondi's name carries enormous weight.

Facts & History

In 1996, the city of Bergamo launched a gran fondo in honor of its most famous cycling son: Felice Gimondi, born in nearby Sedrina in 1942. Gimondi personally designed the routes — threading through the six valleys of his youth, over the climbs where he first developed the legs that would carry him to victories in all three Grand Tours. The partnership with Bianchi — the legendary Italian bicycle manufacturer headquartered in Treviglio, just outside Bergamo — was natural. Gimondi had ridden Bianchi throughout his career. The event grew steadily, accumulating over 70,000 total participants across its first three decades. When Gimondi died of a heart attack while swimming on holiday in Sicily on August 16, 2019, the event became more than a ride — it became a memorial. Since 2019, the event honors all seven cyclists who have won all three Grand Tours, celebrating the exclusive club that Gimondi joined in 1968. The 2026 edition marks the 30th anniversary, with a new Sprint route making the event more accessible than ever. Founded in 1996. By Giuseppe Manenti and organizing committee, in collaboration with Felice Gimondi and Bianchi.

Reputation: The cycling heartland's tribute to its greatest champion. Thirty years of riding through Gimondi's valleys on routes he personally drew, in a city where cycling is not a hobby but an identity.

The Course

Six Bergamasque valleys connected by sustained climbs, starting and finishing in the cultural city of Bergamo. The climbs are not short punchy walls — they are genuine valley ascents of 7-11 km at 5-7% average gradients, with steeper pitches up to 12%. Selvino's 19 hairpins are the signature feature: nearly 11 km of climbing through switchbacks named after local cycling legends, reaching 946m above sea level. Between climbs, the valleys offer rolling terrain through stone villages, river gorges, and alpine meadows. The rhythm is valley-floor recovery, gradual approach, sustained climb, hairpin descent, repeat. The long route does this six times across 148 km.

Signature challenge: Selvino's 19 hairpins — 10.9 km of sustained climbing through switchbacks named after Bergamasque cycling legends. It is not the steepest climb on the route (Colle Gallo has steeper pitches), but it is the most sustained and the most iconic. The 19 bends create a hypnotic rhythm that either carries you upward or slowly grinds you down. On the long route, Selvino comes early enough to be manageable but late enough that the remaining four climbs are ridden on Selvino-depleted legs.

15
MILE
Colle dei Pasta — The Deceptive Opener
3.4 km at 4.2% average with a sting in the tail: 12% maximum gradient sections that surprise riders expecting a gentle warm-up. Only 143m of elevation gain, but the steep pitches test your gearing choices early. This is where you discover whether your cassette is large enough for the day ahead.
25
MILE
Colle Gallo — The Cyclists' Shrine
7.5 km of sustained climbing with a 6% average gradient and 12% maximum pitches, gaining 445 meters. The upper sections steepen through forest switchbacks. At the summit sits the Santuario della Madonna del Ghisallo dei Ciclisti — the Sanctuary of Our Lady of Cyclists, a genuine pilgrimage site with cycling memorabilia and an annual candlelit vigil. Even non-religious riders feel something at the top. The descent off the back is fast and technical.
38
MILE
Selvino — The 19 Hairpins
The defining climb of the Granfondo Gimondi. 10.9 km of sustained ascending, gaining 605 vertical meters at 5.5% average with a 10.6% maximum gradient. Nineteen hairpin bends, each dedicated to a famous cyclist from Bergamo province. The rhythm is mesmerizing and relentless: climb, hairpin, climb, hairpin. The gradient never relents but never brutalizes — this is not a wall, it is a war of attrition. At 946m, the summit is the roof of the route. The descent back down the 19 hairpins requires full concentration.
70
MILE
Costa d'Olda and Forcella di Bura — The Valley Double
The medium and long routes continue deeper into the valleys with two more sustained climbs. Costa d'Olda takes riders through classic Bergamasque scenery — stone villages, terraced hillsides, narrow valley roads. Forcella di Bura reaches 884m above sea level, the second-highest point on the route. The cumulative climbing through these two ascents, after Selvino, begins to genuinely drain reserves. The valleys between offer brief respite and stunning views of the pre-Alpine peaks.
85
MILE
Forcella di Berbenno and Costa Valle Imagna — The Long-Route Finale
Only the long-route riders face these final two climbs. Forcella di Berbenno at km 116.7 comes deep into the day, when legs are heavy and the six valleys have extracted their toll. Costa Valle Imagna is the final sustained effort before the long, rewarding descent back to Bergamo. At this point in the long route, it is not the gradient that hurts — it is the accumulation of 120+ km of climbing. The descent back to Bergamo, with the medieval Città Alta visible on its hilltop, is the earned reward.

Race Logistics

Airport
Bergamo Orio al Serio (BGY) — 5 km from start line (Ryanair hub, budget connections across Europe). Milan Malpensa (MXP) — 1 hour (intercontinental connections). Milan Linate (LIN) — 50 minutes (European connections).
Lodging
Stay in Bergamo — the start/finish city. The lower town (Città Bassa) has the widest hotel selection, with the event start near Gewiss Stadium. The upper town (Città Alta) is a stunning medieval walled city with boutique options — worth the premium for atmosphere. Hotels run EUR70-140/night in early May, before peak tourist season. Book 2-4 months in advance for event weekend. Alternatively, the towns along the route (Selvino, Clusone, San Pellegrino Terme) offer quieter accommodation in the valleys.
Food
Bergamo is Lombardy's culinary hidden gem. Polenta taragna (buckwheat polenta with local cheese), casoncelli alla bergamasca (stuffed pasta with butter, sage, and pancetta), Taleggio cheese (the Val Taleggio is on the route), and stracciatella soup (Bergamo's invention). San Pellegrino Terme — the mineral water's birthplace — is in the Val Brembana on the medium and long routes. Carb loading in Bergamo means handmade pasta in medieval trattorias with views over the Po Plain.
Packet Pickup
Event village in Bergamo, 1-2 days before the race. Registration, bib collection, jersey pickup, and expo. Well-organized for the field size.
Parking
Event parking in designated areas near Gewiss Stadium and the Lazzaretto start area. Follow event instructions — the city center has limited access during the race. Arriving the day before and parking at your accommodation is the simplest approach.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at BGY Airport Granfondo (Granfondo Felice Gimondi)?

+

Early May in the Bergamo pre-Alps is pleasant on a good day — 15-22C, spring light, green valleys. But May is one of Lombardy's wettest months, and the proximity of the Alpine foothills amplifies thunderstorm activity. Wet descents from Selvino at 946m on narrow hairpins are no joke. The morning start at 7 AM can be cool, especially in the valley bottoms that hold overnight chill. Compared to the extremes of Alpine events at 2,500m+ or midsummer desert gran fondos, the climate challenge here is moderate — rain is the primary risk, not temperature or altitude.

How adventurous is BGY Airport Granfondo (Granfondo Felice Gimondi)?

+

The route threads through six distinct Bergamasque valleys — Val Cavallina, Valseriana, Val Serina, Val Brembana, Val Taleggio, Valle Imagna — each with its own character. The pre-Alpine scenery is beautiful: green valley floors, stone villages clinging to hillsides, river gorges, and forested climbs. Bergamo's Città Alta (the medieval walled upper town) is genuinely stunning. The Sanctuary of Our Lady of Cyclists at Colle Gallo is a unique cycling pilgrimage site. But this is not the dramatic high-Alpine spectacle of the Dolomites or the barren lunar landscapes of southern Spain. The adventure is cultural and historical rather than geographical.

Should I race BGY Airport Granfondo (Granfondo Felice Gimondi)?

+

Yes if you want an authentically Italian gran fondo in cycling's heartland — great value, deep heritage, and honest climbing through beautiful pre-Alpine valleys. Yes if Gimondi's name means something to you. Yes if EUR45 for a well-organized FCI event with a Santini jersey option sounds like a bargain. Skip if you want Alpine drama at 2,500m — Selvino tops out under 1,000m. Skip if you need a massive international field and English-language everything — this event is proudly Italian first.
