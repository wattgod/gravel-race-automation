A solid regional event, not a bucket-list destination.

Facts & History

The idea was born in spring 1996, when Sportur (then called BVC Sport) conceived of linking a sports award with a sports event. Cervia was chosen as the starting point because of its ancient salt pans — the 'Via del Sale' (Salt Road) was the historical route that salt miners traveled from the Adriatic coast into the Romagna hills to deliver salt to distant villages. The first edition took place on April 6, 1997, with 750 participants. Among them, unexpectedly, was Marco Pantani. A group of cyclists in unmistakable yellow jerseys arrived at the start line, and when asked if they could participate, one of them replied: 'They told me there is a race. I came with a group of friends. Can we do it too?' It was Il Pirata himself. That moment — Pantani showing up at a brand-new gran fondo — became founding mythology. Founded in 1997. By Sportur (then BVC Sport) — linked a sports award with a cycling event, choosing Cervia as the start/finish point with routes inspired by the historical salt trade.

Reputation: A well-established Italian gran fondo known for its historical salt road narrative, exceptional value, and the founding legend of Pantani's surprise appearance. Respected in the Italian cycling community as a reliable early-season event.

The Course

A road gran fondo that traces the ancient salt trading route from the Adriatic coast into the Romagna hills. The route starts flat from Cervia at sea level, passes the historic salt pans, and heads inland through rolling countryside to a series of punchy climbs. The hills are not long — the longest is 10 km — but gradients reach 18%. The rhythm is distinctive: flat coastal start, progressive climbing through the Romagna foothills, emotional climax at Cima Pantani, a final timed climb, and 32 km of flat back to the Adriatic finish. The salt road runs both ways.

Signature challenge: Cima Pantani on Montevecchio — not the longest or the steepest climb, but the one that matters. 373m of gain at up to 14% with Pantani's name painted on the road. The Grand Prix Via del Sale is awarded here. But the real challenge is cumulative: five distinct climbs across 156 km, with the timed segment at Paderno-Collinello coming after 85+ miles of riding. The salt road demands endurance, not just climbing power.

20
MILE
Monte Cavallo — The First Selection
438m summit reached via 9 km of climbing at 6% average with pitches to 14%. This is the first significant climb on both routes and the point where the field separates. The gradient is honest: steep enough to demand gear selection, long enough to require pacing. The front of the race opens gaps here.
45
MILE
Santa Maria Riopetra — The Steepest Gradient
10 km of climbing at 6% average with a maximum gradient of 18% — the steepest section on the entire course. This climb is only on the long route. The 18% pitches are short but vicious, demanding your lowest gear and your strongest legs. The reward is the view from the summit across the Romagna hills.
60
MILE
Ciola — The Highest Point
The route's ceiling at 557m, reached via 9 km of climbing at a steady 7% average. Maximum gradients are a manageable 10%, making this the most sustained and consistent climb on the course. No single savage pitch, just unrelenting upward progress. At 557m, you are as high as the Via del Sale goes — the salt road starts its return toward the sea.
75
MILE
Cima Pantani (Montevecchio) — The Emotional Heart
373m of elevation gain over 4 km with gradients reaching 14%. This is the climb where 'VAI MARCO... FORZA PIRATA' is painted on the tarmac — words that once encouraged Marco Pantani. Every rider who passes over these words is participating in a small act of cycling memory. The Grand Prix Via del Sale is awarded here. The gradient is fierce for 4 km, but the significance outweighs the suffering.
85
MILE
Paderno-Collinello — The Timed Climb
The official timed segment: 7% average gradient with sections reaching 15%. The last hill before 32 km of flat back to Cervia's seafront. This is where the race is decided on the clock. Legs that have already climbed 1,800+ meters across four previous ascents must now produce one more meaningful effort. The summit at approximately km 104 is the last high point before the coast.

Race Logistics

Airport
Rimini (RMI) — 25 minutes (closest, budget airline connections across Europe). Bologna (BLQ) — 1.5 hours (wider international connections). Forli (FRL) — 30 minutes (very limited flights).
Lodging
Stay in Cervia — the start/finish town on the Adriatic coast. Late March is pre-season, so rates are very reasonable (EUR60-120/night). Hotels and apartments line the seafront. Book 2-4 months in advance for event weekend. Alternatively, Milano Marittima (adjacent to Cervia) offers slightly more upscale options. Rimini (25 min) has the widest hotel selection.
Food
Emilia-Romagna is Italy's culinary heartland. Piadina romagnola (flatbread with squacquerone cheese and prosciutto), passatelli in brodo, tagliatelle al ragu, and fresh Adriatic seafood. Carb loading is not a chore — it is a privilege. Cervia's restaurants along the seafront are excellent and reasonably priced in late March.
Packet Pickup
Event village in Cervia, Saturday before the race. The expo combines registration, sponsor village, and the Cervia Cycling Festival activities. Well-organized for 4,500 riders.
Parking
Cervia manages event parking in designated areas. The seafront area near the start/finish is closed for the event. Arrive the day before and park at your hotel.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Granfondo Via del Sale?

+

Late March on the Adriatic coast of Italy is not extreme — temperatures of 7-13C are cool but manageable. The genuine climate challenge is wind: March is the windiest month in Cervia, with the cold bora from eastern Europe capable of turning flat coastal sections into headwind battles. Rain is possible but not the dominant concern. Compared to Alpine events at altitude or monsoon-season races, the climate challenge is moderate — but the wind can make a meaningful difference to the experience.

How are the logistics for Granfondo Via del Sale?

+

Cervia is well-connected for a mid-size Italian town. Rimini airport is 25 minutes away with budget airline connections across Europe. Bologna airport is 1.5 hours with broader international service. Start and finish are in the same location — no point-to-point transfer logistics. Twenty-nine years of organizational experience from Sportur and Fantini Club means race-day logistics are well-rehearsed.

How adventurous is Granfondo Via del Sale?

+

The route has a compelling narrative: following the ancient salt road from Cervia's salt pans into the hills where salt miners once delivered their cargo. The salt pans themselves — dating to Etruscan times — are a genuine historical landscape. The Romagna hills are charming: rolling green countryside, stone villages, vineyards. Cima Pantani with its painted road ('VAI MARCO... FORZA PIRATA') is an emotional landmark. But this is not the Dolomites or the Stelvio. The scenery is pleasant and culturally rich, not geographically spectacular.

How prestigious is Granfondo Via del Sale?

+

Twenty-nine editions make this a well-established event in the Italian gran fondo calendar. The Pantani connection — his surprise appearance at the first edition in 1997 — gives it a unique piece of cycling folklore. Cima Pantani is a recognized landmark in Italian amateur cycling. Within the Italian scene, the Via del Sale is respected. But globally, it lacks the recognition of Nove Colli (the original GF), Maratona dles Dolomites (the most coveted), or the pro-race sportives (Strade Bianche, Paris-Roubaix Challenge). A solid regional event, not a bucket-list destination.

Should I race Granfondo Via del Sale?

+

Yes if you want an affordable, well-established Italian gran fondo with genuine historical narrative and a Pantani pilgrimage. Yes if punchy Romagna hills with 18% gradients at EUR35 sounds like your kind of value. Skip if you want Alpine spectacle — these are foothills, not mountains. Skip if you need a large international field — this is an Italian event for Italian cyclists.
