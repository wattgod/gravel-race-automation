Course Profile
Logistics 2/5

Start location at 73 Manners Street with showground camping, but course redesign after a sabbatical year creates uncertainty. Prestige 1/5

Caitlin Reid emphasizes it's "organised to be as welcoming as possible to all riders, regardless of their FTP," with prizes only for fastest two per category, confirming participation over competitive depth.

RIDERS REPORT
Steepest Hill
The steepest part of the course reaches 23% incline. Many riders, including e-bike users, had to get off and walk. One rider mentioned finding someone already walking their bike saying they were too tired to continue.
Kildare Road
Freshly bituminized road that is very slippery. Organizers specifically warned riders to back off speed even though you'll want to hammer it down this section.
Final 18 kilometers MI 80
Described as pretty serious hills with 800 meters of elevation gain near the end, making it a cruel finish to the race.
Causeways
Can be slippery with water over them. Riders need to take it easy crossing these sections.
Cattle Grids
Must be crossed straight across due to gaps between the bars. Important to maintain proper line to avoid getting caught in the gaps.
Corrugations on gravel sections - some riders heard that riding on the right hand side (opposite to normal) makes the corrugations less rough, though effectiveness unclear.
Mix of gravel and bitumen sections with multiple water crossings throughout the course.
Freshly bituminized sections that are very slippery and not finished yet, requiring reduced speeds.

RIDERS REPORT
There are two feed stations on the 65k course where you can refill water bottles and get bananas, fruit, and lollies.
Expect strong headwinds that can significantly impact difficulty and pacing throughout the race.
Roads aren't closed to public traffic, but car encounters are minimal - expect maybe 4-5 cars that are friendly and wave.
Watch for wildlife and livestock - you might come around a corner and face cows on the course.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Gravel and Granite?

+

Historical weather data shows average highs of 64°F and lows of 48°F, with a 50% chance of rain on any given day. Jacob rode through active snowfall: "I spent the next 15 minutes grinding in my lowest gear with snow falling off the trees as we ascended," with "muddy and sloppy" conditions creating traction challenges. The May date change specifically targets "cooler weather" after previous timing proved problematic for rider comfort. More weather exposure than Belgian Waffle Ride San Diego.

How are the logistics for Gravel and Granite?

+

Start location at 73 Manners Street with showground camping, but course redesign after a sabbatical year creates uncertainty. Organizers moved 2026 dates to May specifically because "2026 GnG will be held in May, to make the most of the cooler weather," indicating previous timing was problematic. Complete route overhaul happened because "many of the roads that we have used in previous years are now sealed."

How adventurous is Gravel and Granite?

+

Basket Swamp Bash creek crossing and sweeping national park views deliver backcountry character. The Range Rumble winds through protected granite country where organizers emphasize focus "on the adventure and the landscape around you" rather than pure racing. Creek depths vary with conditions, adding weather-dependent adventure elements.

How prestigious is Gravel and Granite?

+

Sixth edition of a regional event where organizers explicitly welcome "gravel bikes, mountain bikes, e-bikes, and adaptive bikes." Caitlin Reid emphasizes it's "organised to be as welcoming as possible to all riders, regardless of their FTP," with prizes only for fastest two per category, confirming participation over competitive depth.

Should I race Gravel and Granite?

+

If you're local to NSW—yes, it's solid. If you're traveling—skip it.
