The Ratings

Course Profile
Logistics 3/5

Bedoin is a picturesque Provencal village at the southern foot of Mont Ventoux, but it is not easy to reach. Avignon TGV station is 60 km southwest — reachable by car in about an hour. Marseille Provence Airport (MRS) is 120 km south. Rental car is the practical option for international arrivals. Bedoin itself has limited accommodation that fills rapidly for the event. The surrounding villages of Malaucene, Carpentras, and Vaison-la-Romaine offer alternatives within 15-30 km. Haute Route handles on-course logistics professionally, but getting yourself to Bedoin is entirely your problem.

Editorial
Prestige 4/5

The Haute Route brand carries genuine weight in serious amateur cycling — it is widely recognized as the most professional multi-day event series for amateurs. The Ventoux edition adds the mythology of cycling's most famous standalone mountain. Three summits in three days is a specific, verifiable accomplishment that carries bragging rights in any cycling group. The event attracts motivated, well-prepared riders from across Europe and beyond. What keeps it at 4 is that the Ventoux edition is a smaller, secondary event compared to the flagship Haute Route Alps, and its future as a standalone offering is uncertain.

Facts & History

The Haute Route Ventoux was created as a shorter-format addition to the Haute Route Series, which began with the flagship 7-day Alps event in 2011. The concept was compelling: take cycling's most legendary standalone mountain and create a 3-day stage race that summits it from every side. Based in the village of Bedoin at the southern foot of Ventoux, the event leveraged the Haute Route brand's professional-level organization and applied it to a focused, geographically concentrated challenge. The 3-day format made the event accessible to riders who could not commit to the week-long Alps or Dolomites events. Mont Ventoux's unique status — featured in the Tour de France 15+ times since 1951, site of Tom Simpson's death in 1967, and crowned by an iconic observatory on its barren summit — made it the perfect location for an event that transcends cycling into pilgrimage. Founded in 2018. By OC Sport (Haute Route Series).

Reputation: Recognized as the definitive Ventoux multi-day challenge. The Haute Route brand brings credibility and professional-level support that distinguishes this from self-organized Ventoux rides. Cycling media consistently rate the Haute Route events as the gold standard for amateur multi-day racing.

2018: Inaugural Haute Route Ventoux — 3-day format established with daily summit finishes
2019: Event grows to attract international field of 400+ riders from 20+ nationalities
2021: Post-COVID return — October edition with memorable summit conditions including near-freezing temperatures
Haute Route Series evolves: Alps remains flagship, shorter-format events (including Ventoux) adjusted based on demand
Mont Ventoux continues to feature in Tour de France, including memorable double-ascent stage in 2021

The Course

A 3-day stage race centered entirely on Mont Ventoux — cycling's most legendary standalone mountain. Each stage radiates from Bedoin through the surrounding Provencal countryside before converging on the summit at 1,912m via a different ascent route. The course design is elegant in its simplicity: three days, three sides, one mountain. The climbing is relentless, the exposure above treeline is savage, and the cumulative fatigue of three consecutive summit finishes separates those who trained from those who hoped.

Signature challenge: The cumulative summit assault: three ascents of Mont Ventoux (1,912m) in three days from three different sides. Each approach has distinct character, but all converge on the same exposed, wind-battered final 6 km of moonscape. By Stage 3, the climb you know is waiting breaks you mentally before the gradient breaks you physically.

MILE
Stage 1 — Bedoin Ascent
The classic Tour de France route. 21.5 km at 7.6% average from Bedoin. The first 6 km are gentle through vineyards, then the road enters the forest and the gradient jumps to 9-10% for 10 relentless kilometers. Above Chalet Reynard at km 15, the trees end and the moonscape begins — 6 km of exposed limestone with the Mistral and the observatory growing in the distance.
?
MILE
Stage 2 — Malaucene Ascent
The north face. 21 km at 7.5% average. Hits you immediately with steep lower gradients of 10-12% before a brief plateau mid-climb gives false hope. The final approach from Chalet Reynard merges with the Bedoin route for the familiar exposed moonscape. Cumulative fatigue from Stage 1 makes this feel 50% harder than the numbers suggest.
?
MILE
Stage 3 — Sault Ascent
The gentlest approach — 26 km at 4.4% average through lavender country and forest. The deceptive ease lulls tired legs into false security. The final 6 km above Chalet Reynard — the same exposed moonscape as the other two sides — deliver the summit with your legs carrying the weight of two prior days. This is where the event is won or survived.

Race Logistics

Airport
Marseille Provence (MRS) 120 km south (~1.5 hr drive). Lyon Saint-Exupery (LYS) 240 km north (~2.5 hr drive). Avignon TGV station 60 km southwest (~1 hr drive). Rental car recommended from any arrival point.
Lodging
Bedoin has limited accommodation — small hotels and gites fill rapidly for the event. Malaucene (15 km north) and Carpentras (20 km south) offer more options. Vaison-la-Romaine (25 km north) is a charming base with good hotel selection. Haute Route offers accommodation packages at additional cost. Book early — Provencal tourism is strong even in October.
Food
Provence delivers exceptional cuisine. Bedoin has several restaurants and cafes. The regional specialties — ratatouille, tapenade, Provencal herbs, local wines from Cotes du Ventoux and Cotes du Rhone — are top-tier. Haute Route provides post-stage hot meals. The ristori on course are well-stocked. After three summit finishes, the evening meals in Provencal restaurants are deeply satisfying.
Packet Pickup
At the Haute Route event village in Bedoin, typically the day before Stage 1. Registration includes bike check, bib number collection, and safety briefing.
Parking
Available in Bedoin — village parking areas designated for the event. Limited capacity, arrive early.
RIDERS REPORT
Position yourself safely in first couple kilometers as people jostle from back to front - tension builds in the fast-moving peloton and crashes happen.
Be prepared for very hot conditions - temperatures expected to reach mid-30s, so finish the climb before it gets too hot.
Navigate feed stations carefully as complications can lead to long chasing efforts before the final climb.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Haute Route Ventoux?

+

At the base in Bedoin, October in Provence is lovely — 20C, blue skies, vineyards turning gold. At the summit of Ventoux, 1,600 meters higher, it is a different planet. The Mistral wind is the defining weather feature: this cold, dry northwesterly can exceed 90 km/h on exposed days, making the barren final 6 km above Chalet Reynard genuinely dangerous for cyclists. Temperature at the summit can be 0-5C when the base is a comfortable 18C. Three consecutive summit finishes means three exposures to these conditions. The 2021 edition reportedly had one stage with summit temperatures near freezing. Bring a vest, arm warmers, and a plan for the descent.

+

Haute Route handles on-course logistics professionally, but getting yourself to Bedoin is entirely your problem.

How adventurous is Haute Route Ventoux?

+

Mont Ventoux is one of cycling's most mythological mountains. Tom Simpson's memorial at km 18 on the Bedoin side. The moonscape above treeline that looks like a lunar surface. The observatory at the summit. The Tour de France history etched into every hairpin. Combining this with Provencal wine country, the Gorges de la Nesque, lavender fields (past season in October, but the landscape remains), and the charming village of Bedoin creates a complete cycling pilgrimage. What keeps it at 4 rather than 5 is that Ventoux is well-traveled — this is not remote or undiscovered territory. It is cycling tourism at its most developed.

+

Should I race Haute Route Ventoux?

+

Ride this if Mont Ventoux is your cycling grail and you want all three ascents in a professionally supported format. Skip it if you are on a budget (EUR 600-900 entry), prefer flat or gravel terrain, or cannot commit to 3 consecutive days of serious climbing. If you just want to climb Ventoux once, ride it independently — it is a public road.
