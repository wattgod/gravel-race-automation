The Course

Chip-timed mass-start gravel race from Cable WI (~1,350ft): Hungry Bear 100 (100mi flagship), Snacking Bear 60, Climbing Cub 30 — 'fortified gravel' through the Chequamegon-Nicolet National Forest and Lincoln Community Forest; Rock Lake section = 'the punchiest ten miles', plus Porcupine Road and the Morgan Falls Climb; aid at miles 26 and 67; finish near The Portage/Rivers Eatery. Stage of the Iron Bear 1000 series. Gain unpublished by organizer (~5,000ft per independent recap — display Varies). ORGANIZER = CAMBA, NOT New Moon Ski & Bike (prior profile wrong). Corrected 2026-07-14.

Signature challenge: 100-mile distance on rolling to hilly Northwoods terrain.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Hungry Bear 100?

+

Early May weather creates planning challenges, as Dan's group discovered when they "underdressed for the day" after waking to 34°F despite forecasts "promising sun and temps over 50." The sun finally appeared and temps "just reached up above 50 degrees," but that morning surprise shows why northern Wisconsin spring racing requires weather contingency planning. More weather exposure than Belgian Waffle Ride San Diego.

How are the logistics for Hungry Bear 100?

+

Dan from One On One Bicycle Studio missed the start "for the second year in a row" and "didn't care" - that low-stress approach masks basic organizational gaps. Packet pickup happens at Tilly's Pies with free coffee, which sounds charming but hardly screams professional logistics for 800+ riders spread across three different start locations and times.

How adventurous is Hungry Bear 100?

+

Parker's course design "winds its way deep into the north woods" through forest service roads where "you're rarely traveling in the same direction for long." Linda Shydlowski felt "gratitude for the sunshine, blue skies, forests, lakes, rivers" during her ride. The route offers "6 or 7 hours" through Chequamegon forest with limited cell service, creating proper backcountry isolation.

How prestigious is Hungry Bear 100?

+

Dan epitomizes the race culture: missing the start twice running because "fun and adventure always trump competition." Tim Savre won in 4:40:58, but the emphasis stays on inclusivity over racing pedigree. One rider loved "how chill this race was" and called it "the antithesis to so many amped up races," confirming this isn't a resume-building competitive showcase.

Should I race Hungry Bear 100?

+

If you're in Wisconsin or the Upper Midwest and want a 100-miler in a beautiful setting—yes. If you're traveling from far away—probably not worth the trip.
