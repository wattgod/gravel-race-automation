The Course

Jeroboam masquerades as gravel but delivers two-thirds asphalt—don't be fooled, this isn't easy road riding. The Lombard Prealps serve up Roman cobbles, vineyard doubletrack, and 30% mountain bike trails that'll have you hiking sections. Wine country never tasted this bitter.

Signature challenge: The 4pm start guarantees you'll ride most of this beast in complete darkness, navigating technical terrain with a headlamp while your body shuts down. It's not the 7,000m of climbing that breaks people—it's doing it blind for 14 hours straight.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Jeroboam?

+

Northern Italian autumn brings variable conditions that caught Paul Errington off guard: 'Not more than a few pedal turns later the rain started, then it got heavier, then it turned to hail. Our small group rode up on to the pavement and sheltered in a doorway. 0.5km into our 300km adventure and already we were soaked.'

How are the logistics for Jeroboam?

+

Milan-area airports provide access to Erbusco, Lombardy with free camping at the event village. However, Jack Luke notes 'Little goes to plan in Italy' and organizers 'give 3T a little leeway regarding some of the amusing chaos we experienced during the ride.' Wine country accommodation runs $100-600/night in Italy's premier Franciacorta sparkling wine region.

How adventurous is Jeroboam?

+

Absolute bucket list material. Mandatory night riding through alpine passes, watching sunrise after 20+ hours of suffering, circumnavigating Lake Iseo, passing through legendary Franciacorta vineyards. The wine bottle naming, 3T heritage, and 'world's toughest gravel' reputation create an unforgettable experience.

How prestigious is Jeroboam?

+

Legendary status as 'world's toughest gravel ride' backed by 3T's Gerard Vroomen. Jack Luke received an invite noting organizers 'quite openly admitted that they expected to have few finishers' on terrain that was 'unrideable in places.' However, European focus and no UCI affiliation limits global recognition outside dedicated ultra circles.

Should I race Jeroboam?

+

Show up undertrained and the Lombard Prealps will humble you by kilometer 30. The 4pm start means you're grinding technical singletrack in the dark with dead legs, so your bike handling better be dialed. This isn't a gravel race—it's a 24-hour suffer fest through vineyards that thinks it's the Giro.
