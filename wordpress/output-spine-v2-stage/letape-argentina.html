The Ratings

Course Profile
Logistics 3/5

The event infrastructure is promising: Estadio Mario Alberto Kempes is an iconic venue (1978 World Cup final, 57,000 capacity) providing a memorable start/finish location with ample staging area. ASO franchise standards mean professional timing, medical teams, mechanical support, feed zones, and sweep wagons. Cordoba has its own international airport (Ingeniero Aeronautico Ambrosio L.V. Taravella, COR) with direct flights from Buenos Aires, Lima, Santiago, and Panama City -- eliminating the need to transit through Buenos Aires by ground. However, this is a relaunch edition after 11 years, so the local organizing team is unproven in this specific configuration. The route uses a mix of highways and mountain roads that require significant road-closure coordination. For international visitors outside South America, reaching Cordoba requires a connection through Buenos Aires or another regional hub.

Editorial
Prestige 3/5

The Tour de France brand is the most recognized name in professional cycling, and the L'Etape franchise transfers meaningful prestige to its satellite events. The co-location of the Argentine National Road Championship (returning to Cordoba after 25 years) elevates the weekend's significance beyond a typical franchise event. But this is a relaunch after an 11-year absence -- the event needs to re-establish itself. It is one of 30+ L'Etape events worldwide, and the Argentine edition does not yet carry the established reputation of the French original, Brazilian franchise (10+ years running), or the Australian edition. Within Argentine cycling, the 2026 edition will be significant. Globally, it is a new data point.

Facts & History

L'Etape Argentina was originally part of ASO's early expansion of the L'Etape franchise to South America, with editions held in Argentina around 2014-2015. The event went dormant, and the franchise's South American presence shifted to Brazil, where L'Etape Brasil became the continent's flagship amateur cycling event from 2015 onward. After an 11-year absence, ASO announced the return of L'Etape Argentina for 2026, this time in the cycling-friendly province of Cordoba rather than Buenos Aires. The choice of Cordoba reflects the province's investment in sports tourism and cycling infrastructure, as well as its Sierras providing genuine mountain terrain. The Government of Cordoba Province has been actively involved in the organization, positioning the event as a centerpiece of regional sports tourism. The concurrent return of the Argentine National Road Championship to Cordoba after 25 years -- organized by UCRA (Union Ciclista de la Republica Argentina) alongside FACPyR and local entities -- transforms the March 2026 weekend into the most significant cycling event in Argentine history in a generation. Founded in 2015. By Local organizing partner under license from ASO (Amaury Sport Organisation -- Tour de France organizer), with support from the Government of Cordoba Province.

Reputation: As a relaunch event, L'Etape Argentina does not yet have an established modern reputation. The Tour de France brand provides immediate credibility, and the Government of Cordoba's institutional backing adds legitimacy. The co-location with the returning Argentine National Road Championship creates a historically significant cycling weekend. Within the L'Etape franchise ecosystem (30+ events in 21 countries), Argentina represents a new frontier in a cycling-mad but underserved market. The 2026 edition will define the event's reputation going forward.

The Course

A road course through the Sierras de Cordoba that climbs from the city's valley floor into the Punilla Valley mountain region. The 134 km long course starts and finishes at Estadio Mario Alberto Kempes (~400m elevation), heads north through the Sierras Chicas to Rio Ceballos, climbs the Camino del Cuadrado to approximately 1,280m, then descends through the Punilla Valley past Valle Hermoso before returning via the Autovia Punilla and Cordoba-Carlos Paz highway. The route character alternates between fast highway sections and winding mountain roads, with the Camino del Cuadrado serving as the decisive climbing challenge. The scenery transitions from suburban Cordoba through forested foothills to open mountain vistas across the Punilla Valley.

Signature challenge: The Camino del Cuadrado -- the mountain pass connecting the Sierras Chicas to the Punilla Valley. Rising approximately 800m with sustained steep gradients on recently repaved mountain road, this climb determines the race. The combination of the concentrated ascent followed by the grinding return leg across the valley and back to Cordoba creates a course that punishes riders who go too deep on the big climb and have nothing left for the final hour.

MILE
Kempes Stadium Departure
Early morning rollout from the iconic stadium. The first kilometers head north through suburban Cordoba on the approach to the Sierras Chicas. The field sorts itself on the gently rising terrain. March morning temperatures are comfortable (18-20C at start). Nervous energy and the Tour de France atmosphere keep the pace honest from the gun.
30
MILE
Camino del Cuadrado Ascent
The signature climb of the course. The road rises steeply from the Rio Ceballos side, gaining approximately 800m over a concentrated distance. Gradients reach 8-12% on the steepest pitches. Sweeping curves reveal increasingly dramatic views of the Punilla Valley below. This is where the race is decided -- the climbing separates those who trained specifically from those who hoped general fitness would carry them. The recently repaved surface is smooth, but the gradient is relentless. The KOM classification is contested here.
55
MILE
Punilla Valley Descent and Rollers
The descent from Camino del Cuadrado drops into the Punilla Valley -- a sweeping downhill that rewards confident descenders and punishes those who brake through every curve. The valley floor provides brief respite on rolling terrain through Valle Hermoso and surrounding towns. But the rollers between valley towns accumulate fatigue on already-taxed legs. This is the mental crux: the big climb is behind you, but 40+ km of riding remains with no extended flat recovery.
70
MILE
Return to Cordoba
The final 25 km uses the Autovia Punilla and Cordoba-Carlos Paz highway to return to the city. This is the grinding finale -- largely flat to gently rolling, but after 100+ km and 1,800+ meters of climbing, the legs are heavy. Headwinds are possible on the open highway sections. The approach to Kempes Stadium reignites the final push. The Tour de France finish-line experience at the stadium provides the emotional fuel for the last kilometers.

Race Logistics

Airport
Ingeniero Aeronautico Ambrosio L.V. Taravella International Airport (COR) -- Cordoba's own international airport, 11 km from city center and Kempes Stadium. Direct flights from Buenos Aires (1.5 hrs), Lima, Santiago, Panama City, and seasonal international connections.
Lodging
Cordoba is Argentina's second-largest city with abundant accommodation at all price points. The Nueva Cordoba and Centro neighborhoods offer the most options within easy reach of Kempes Stadium. Budget hotels and hostels from $20-40 USD/night; comfortable mid-range hotels $50-100 USD/night. Airbnb options are plentiful and affordable. Book 1-2 months in advance for race weekend. The Punilla Valley towns (La Falda, Villa Carlos Paz, Cosquin) offer scenic alternatives 30-60 minutes from the start.
Food
Cordoba is a major Argentine city with excellent dining. Asado (Argentine barbecue), empanadas cordobesas (Cordoba's signature deep-fried empanadas), locro, and top-tier Malbec wine. Carb-loading is effortless in Argentina -- pasta, bread, and medialunas (Argentine croissants) are abundant and cheap. Post-race asado celebration is practically mandatory. Feed zones on course stocked with water, energy drinks, and food.
Packet Pickup
Event village at Estadio Mario Alberto Kempes, March 20-21 (Friday-Saturday before race day). Registration, kit collection, and brand activations.
Parking
Kempes Stadium has substantial parking infrastructure (built for 57,000-capacity football matches). Additional designated event parking areas expected. Arrive early on race morning.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at L'Etape Argentina by Tour de France?

+

Late March in Cordoba is climatically favorable for road cycling. The worst of summer heat has passed, with temperatures settling into a comfortable 20-27C range. Humidity is moderate, not the oppressive subtropical moisture of Buenos Aires or coastal Brazil. The Sierras provide cooler air at altitude. The main climate risk is afternoon thunderstorms, which are common in this transitional season -- but the early morning start should have most riders finished or nearly finished before afternoon convection develops. Climate is not a defining challenge of this event. It scores low because the weather is essentially pleasant rather than punishing.

How adventurous is L'Etape Argentina by Tour de France?

+

The Sierras de Cordoba offer genuine scenic value: granite mountains, river valleys, small towns with their own character, and the sweeping views from the Camino del Cuadrado across the Punilla Valley. Cordoba province's landscape diversity -- sierras, valleys, rivers, and traditional towns -- creates an attractive backdrop for cycling. The sense of riding through the 'heart of Argentina' rather than the cosmopolitan Buenos Aires gives the event a more authentic regional character. However, this is a fully supported road event with feed zones and sweep wagons. The adventure comes from the destination and scenery rather than remoteness or self-reliance. For non-Argentine riders, the cultural adventure of cycling in Argentina's interior adds genuine novelty.

Should I race L'Etape Argentina by Tour de France?

+

Yes if you are based in South America and want a professionally organized Tour de France-branded sportive at exceptional value. Yes if the historic co-location with the Argentine National Championship appeals. Yes if you want to be part of a relaunch edition that could become significant. Skip if you want a proven event with multi-year track record -- this is edition one in its modern form. Skip if you need deep international competition or extreme mountain climbing. Skip if you are traveling from Europe or North America solely for this event -- the Brazilian franchise is more established.
