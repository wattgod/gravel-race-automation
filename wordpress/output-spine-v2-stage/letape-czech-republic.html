The Ratings

Course Profile
Logistics 3/5

Prague is one of Europe's best-connected cities -- Vaclav Havel Airport (PRG) has direct flights from most European capitals and seasonal long-haul connections. The city has extensive public transport, affordable taxis, and every major hotel chain. The 2026 start/finish at Pankrac (Prague 4) is metro-accessible. Packet pickup is at the L'Etape Village in Centralni park Pankrac. The rural course through Central Bohemia means no mid-route bail-out options. For a closed-road event of this scale, the logistics are well-handled, but international visitors still need to navigate Czech-language signage and limited English outside Prague.

Editorial
Prestige 3/5

The Tour de France brand is the most powerful in cycling, and ASO's patronage gives this event legitimate credentials. Bradley Wiggins, Zdenek Stybar, Andy Schleck, Jan Svorada, and Martina Sablíkova have all participated. It is the largest amateur cycling event in Central Europe. But this is still a franchise event -- a satellite of the real L'Etape du Tour, which runs on the actual Tour de France stage in France. The Czech edition is 6 years old with a regional draw. Prestigious within Central Europe; a footnote globally.

Facts & History

L'Etape Czech Republic was born in 2021 as part of ASO's global expansion of the L'Etape by Tour de France franchise. Organized by Petr Cech Sport under the direction of Tomas Pravenec, the event immediately tapped into the Czech Republic's deep cycling culture. The inaugural edition took place in heavy rain on August 28, 2021, starting from Prague's Strahov district. Despite the weather, the event was a hit. By its fifth edition in 2025, it had grown to over 3,000 participants, attracted Bradley Wiggins as a guest rider, expanded to two annual stages (Hilly and Mountain), and earned recognition as the largest amateur cycling event in Central Europe. The addition of a Mountain Stage in Prachatice (2024) and a Flat Stage in Pardubice (2026) shows continued growth. Founded in 2021. By Petr Cech Sport (organizing body), under ASO (Amaury Sport Organisation) patronage.

Reputation: The largest and most professionally organized amateur cycling event in Central Europe. Tour de France branding and ASO patronage give it credibility that few regional sportives can match. Rapidly growing community with year-round engagement through the Bootcamp program. Within the Czech Republic, it has become a 'nationwide celebration' that draws entire villages to the roadside.

2021: Inaugural edition in heavy rain -- Daniel Turek and Martina Sablíkova among the first winners
2024: Second stage added -- Mountain Stage in Prachatice with 2,300 m climbing in Sumava mountains
2025: Record-breaking 3,000+ participants; Bradley Wiggins rides as guest star
2025: First-ever cumulative overall winner crowned across Hilly and Mountain stages
2026: 6th edition moves start/finish to Pankrac (Prague 4); third stage added (Flat Stage in Pardubice, October)
Celebrity riders across editions: Jan Svorada, Martina Sablíkova, Zdenek Stybar, Tomas Babek, Andy Schleck, Bradley Wiggins

The Course

Rolling Bohemian countryside on fully closed roads. The hilly stage winds through the Central Bohemia region, passing through the Krivoklat forests, Berounsko river valley, and the Czech Karst -- a landscape of limestone formations, deep forests, ancient alleys, and historic villages. The terrain is characterized by punchy hills rather than sustained mountain climbs: categorized ascents of 2-5 km with gradients that reward explosive power as much as steady-state climbing. The 2024 edition introduced four new summits to freshen the route. Descents wind through forested sections on well-maintained roads. The course delivers a classic European hilly sportive profile -- genuinely challenging but not destructive.

Signature challenge: The accumulated vertical across punchy Bohemian hills. No single climb is a monster, but 1,700 m of elevation gain delivered in short, sharp efforts across rolling terrain demands repeated high-intensity efforts. The long route's 25 km/h minimum average time limit adds pressure -- you cannot soft-pedal the hills and expect to make cutoff.

MILE
Prague Start -- Pankrac
Rolling out from Pankrac in Prague 4, the first kilometers navigate Prague's western suburbs before breaking into open countryside. The field of 3,000 riders sorts itself on the early rollers. Pace is lively as competitive riders push to the front.
20
MILE
Krivoklat Forests
Deep into the Krivoklat region -- UNESCO-recognized biosphere reserve. Dense forests, rolling terrain, and the first categorized climbs. Punchy ascents of 3-5% test early legs. The scenery is stunning: thick Bohemian woodland, medieval castle glimpses, and narrow valleys along the Berounka river.
45
MILE
Berounsko and Czech Karst
The route passes through the karst landscape south of Prague -- limestone gorges, dramatic rock formations, and quiet villages. This is the climbing heart of the course where the steepest pitches live. KOM primes are contested on the categorized ascents. The iconic Uhonickak climb (2.9 km at 3.8%) is a recognized segment from the ROUVY partnership.
70
MILE
Final Approach to Prague
The closing kilometers bring riders back toward Prague through the southern suburbs. Tired legs face the last rollers before the finish at Pankrac. Sprint primes reward the fast finishers. The atmosphere builds as roadside fans thicken near the city. Crossing the finish line under the Tour de France arch in Prague is the payoff.

Race Logistics

Airport
Vaclav Havel Airport Prague (PRG) -- direct flights from all major European hubs, seasonal long-haul. 30 minutes to city center by bus/metro. Car rental available but unnecessary for the event.
Lodging
Stay in Prague -- the start/finish is at Pankrac (Prague 4), easily metro-accessible. Wide range from EUR 40/night hostels to EUR 150+ luxury hotels. Book early for June as it overlaps with peak tourist season. Old Town and Vinohrady neighborhoods are cycling-friendly with good restaurants. No need for rural accommodation -- Prague is the venue.
Food
Czech cuisine is hearty and carb-friendly: svickova (beef in cream sauce with dumplings), koleno (roast pork knee), trdlo (pastry). Pre-race pasta is easy to find in Prague's many Italian restaurants. Czech beer (Pilsner Urquell, Staropramen, craft breweries) is top-tier and EUR 1-3 per pint. Post-ride recovery at a beer garden is mandatory. Feed zones on course provide water, energy drinks, fruit, and bars.
Packet Pickup
L'Etape Village at Centralni park Pankrac (Prague 4). Opens Friday before the Saturday race. Presentations, workshops, and expo. Expect moderate queues -- arrive early.
Parking
Start/finish at Pujmanove street, Prague 4 (Pankrac). Metro accessible (Line C). Limited street parking. Shuttles or metro recommended over driving.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at L'Etape Czech Republic by Tour de France?

+

Late June in Central Bohemia is about as benign as European cycling weather gets. Temperatures of 20-25C are ideal for racing. The 2025 edition saw 25C and sunny -- Bradley Wiggins called it 'a brilliant day on the bike.' The main risk is afternoon thunderstorms, which are common in Central European summers but typically brief. No altitude-driven temperature extremes, no desert heat, no Alpine cold. Climate is a non-factor here -- you dress for a nice summer day.

How adventurous is L'Etape Czech Republic by Tour de France?

+

The route passes through genuinely beautiful Czech countryside -- the Krivoklat forests, Czech Karst limestone formations, the Berounka river valley, and ancient villages with medieval churches. Starting and finishing in Prague adds a major cultural dimension. But this is a fully supported, closed-road sportive with 3,000 riders, feed zones, and Shimano mechanics. The adventure is cultural and scenic, not wilderness or survival. If exploring Central Bohemia by bike with Tour de France production values counts as adventure, this delivers.

Should I race L'Etape Czech Republic by Tour de France?

+

Yes if you want a professionally organized, closed-road sportive with Tour de France branding in one of Europe's most appealing and affordable cities. Yes if you want the L'Etape experience without Alpine-scale suffering. Skip if you need Alpine passes and 5,000 m of climbing to feel challenged. Skip if you want a globally prestigious event with international field depth -- this is still regional.
