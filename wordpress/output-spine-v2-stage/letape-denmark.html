Facts & History

The event began in 2013 as Haervejsloebet (The Viking Gran Fondo), organized by DGI as a recreational cycling event following the ancient Haervejen road through the Jutland peninsula. The Haervejen — meaning 'the army road' — is a route traveled by Vikings, pilgrims, merchants, and migrants for over a thousand years, following a natural glacial ridge that kept travelers dry through Denmark's marshlands. The event grew steadily, celebrating its 10th edition in 2022 — the same year the Tour de France Grand Depart was held in Copenhagen, igniting a cycling boom in Denmark. In 2023, Haervejsloebet partnered with ASO to become an official L'Etape by Tour de France event, adding TdF jersey classifications and international branding. The marriage of Denmark's oldest cycling road with the world's most famous cycling brand proved immediately successful: participation jumped from 2,500 in 2023 to 5,700 to 7,000 in 2024, with riders from 30+ countries. Founded in 2013. By DGI (Danish Gymnastics and Sports Associations) — Denmark's second-largest sports organization.

Reputation: Denmark's largest recreational cycling event and the fastest-growing L'Etape franchise worldwide. The Viking road narrative and Danish community organization give it an identity that transcends the generic TdF franchise model.

2013: Inaugural Haervejsloebet (Viking Gran Fondo) along the ancient Haervejen road
2022: 10th anniversary edition — same year as Tour de France Grand Depart in Copenhagen
2023: Official partnership with ASO — becomes L'Etape Denmark by Tour de France (2,500 riders, 11 routes)
2024: Explosive growth to 7,000 riders from 30+ countries on 16 routes — Denmark's largest cycling event
2025: Continued expansion with 7,500+ expected participants
2026: Fourth edition as L'Etape Denmark, June 27 — five road routes from 50 km to 300 km

The Course

A point-to-point journey along Denmark's ancient glacial ridge — the Haervejen road that Vikings, pilgrims, and merchants traveled for a thousand years. The terrain is rolling rather than mountainous, with the Jutland ridgeline delivering constant undulation through heathlands, forests, and historic towns. The landscape is understated and pastoral — wide horizons, open farmland, ancient churches, and Viking-era landmarks. Wind is the primary adversary, channeled across the open terrain by prevailing westerlies. Each route starts from a different Jutland town and converges on Viborg, Denmark's former capital, for a shared finish celebration. The 300 km Queen Stage begins across the border in Flensburg, Germany, adding an international dimension to an already mythic route.

Signature challenge: The 300 km Queen Stage from Flensburg to Viborg — 186 miles of sustained effort along the Viking road with 2,500 meters of accumulated climbing. The challenge is not any single climb but the relentless accumulation of effort across rolling terrain, exposed to Jutland's winds, for 14-18 hours. Denmark does not have mountains, but it has distance, wind, and the psychological weight of a route that stretches from Germany to the heart of the Danish peninsula.

MILE
Flensburg to the Danish Border (300 km route)
The Queen Stage begins in Germany. The first 30 km through Schleswig-Holstein and across the Danish-German border are relatively flat but psychologically daunting — 300 km of riding stretches ahead. Settle into pace early. The journey from Germany into Denmark following the ancient route adds a dimension no other L'Etape event can match.
70
MILE
Vejle Aadal — The Massif Central of Denmark
The Vejle river valley is where Denmark gets as close to hilly as it gets. Repeated short, punchy climbs of 6-10% gradient through the 'Danish Alps' — the locals say it with a straight face. After 100+ km, these rollers accumulate fatigue that the elevation profile does not adequately convey. The wind across the exposed ridgeline adds a layer of difficulty that flat-country cyclists understand and mountain cyclists underestimate.
85
MILE
Mur de Refsgaarde
The hardest single climb on the course — 550 meters at 8.2% average gradient. By Danish standards, this is the Alpe d'Huez. By international standards, it is a punchy kicker that lasts about 2-3 minutes. But after 140+ km of riding into the wind, 8.2% hurts more than the numbers suggest. The timed KOM segment here determines the polka-dot jersey.
92
MILE
Dollerup Bakker to Viborg
The final rolling hills south of Viborg — 1,300 meters at 3.8% average through the scenic Dollerup Bakker landscape. By this point on the 160 km or 300 km routes, accumulated fatigue makes even modest gradients feel significant. The finish through Viborg's historic streets to Paradepladsen (The Parade Square) delivers the yellow celebration atmosphere that justifies the TdF branding.

Race Logistics

Airport
Billund (BLL) — 1.5 hours drive to Viborg. Copenhagen (CPH) — 3-4 hours drive or train. Aalborg (AAL) — 1.5 hours drive. Hamburg (HAM) — 4 hours drive (for 300 km Flensburg start).
Lodging
Viborg is the universal finish city — book accommodation there. Mid-size Danish city with hotels, B&Bs, and vacation rentals at reasonable (non-resort) prices. For the 300 km route starting in Flensburg, overnight accommodation near the German-Danish border is needed the night before. Book 2-3 months in advance — the event fills Viborg's accommodation capacity. Camping options available through local clubs.
Food
Danish cycling culture meets Viking tradition. Feed stations staffed by local sports clubs are famous for their quality — expect fresh pastries, fruit, energy foods, and enthusiastic volunteers. Viborg's finish festival features food vendors and live music. Pre-race carb loading: Danish bakeries deliver excellent pastries and bread. Post-race: the Viborg festival area has ample food and drink options.
Packet Pickup
At respective start locations or the Viborg finish area. Check event website for specific pickup times and locations.
Parking
Available at start locations. Bus transfers organized from Viborg finish back to start cities. For 300 km Flensburg start: organized bus transfer from Viborg to Flensburg available the day before.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at L'Etape Denmark by Tour de France?

+

Late June in Denmark is about as pleasant as cycling weather gets in northern Europe. Temperatures of 15-20C, long daylight hours, generally mild conditions. The climate poses minimal challenge. Wind is the one real factor — Jutland's open landscape channels prevailing westerlies across exposed sections, and a headwind day can turn any Danish ride into a grind. But compared to Alpine weather roulette, desert heat, or tropical humidity, this is cycling in comfortable conditions.

How adventurous is L'Etape Denmark by Tour de France?

+

The Haervejen concept is genuinely compelling — you are riding an ancient road traveled by Vikings, pilgrims, and merchants for centuries, following a glacial ridge through the heart of Denmark. The 300 km Queen Stage starts in Germany and crosses the border into Denmark, which adds an international dimension. The landscape is pastoral and beautiful in a understated Scandinavian way — heathlands, forests, and historic towns. But Denmark is safe, well-organized, and flat. The adventure is cultural and historical rather than physical or environmental.

Should I race L'Etape Denmark by Tour de France?

+

Yes if you want a well-organized TdF franchise experience in Scandinavia with genuine community atmosphere. Yes if the 300 km ultra-distance Queen Stage appeals to your endurance ambitions. Yes if you want an accessible entry point to international road cycling events — the five distance options accommodate everyone. Skip if you want serious climbing — Denmark's glacial ridgeline is charming but not challenging by Alpine or Pyrenean standards. Skip if you prioritize deep competitive racing over mass-participation experience.
