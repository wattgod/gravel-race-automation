Course Profile
Logistics 4/5

Ljubljana is a small, navigable European capital with its own international airport (LJU) just 26 km from the city center. The start/finish at BTC City is well-connected by public transport. Registration is open — no lottery, no ballot, no waitlist. Sign up, pay EUR 44-55, show up. Packet pickup is straightforward. Ljubljana's compact size means everything is close. Facts & History

The first Maraton Franja took place on July 22, 1982, with approximately 700 participants — most on regular, non-competitive bicycles. The event was named after the Franja Partisan Hospital, a secret WWII medical facility hidden in the Pasice gorge near Cerkno. Built in December 1943, the hospital was managed by Dr. Franja Bojc Bidovec and treated wounded partisans and Allied forces in complete secrecy, protected by retractable bridges, minefields, and camouflaged buildings. The marathon route passes near this UNESCO-nominated site, paying tribute to the courage of its medical staff. From those 700 inaugural riders, the event grew steadily through Slovenian independence (1991), reached record participation of 1,886 in 2002, and was selected as a founding member of the UCI Gran Fondo World Series in 2011. Over 150,000 cyclists from 80+ countries have completed the event. Founded in 1982. By Slovenian cycling community organizers in Ljubljana.

Reputation: The quiet elder of European gran fondos. Not the flashiest, not the hardest, but one of the most enduring — running since before Slovenia was even an independent country. A founding pillar of the UCI Gran Fondo World Series and a Slovenian national cycling institution.

1982: Inaugural edition — July 22, approximately 700 participants on mostly non-competitive bicycles
1991: Continued through Slovenian independence — the event predates the nation itself
2002: Record participation of 1,886 in the main marathon, plus first Family Marathon
2011: Selected as founding member of the UCI Gran Fondo World Series
2025: 44th edition draws 3,000+ riders from 31 countries
Lifetime: 150,000+ finishers from 80+ countries, including 23,000+ women

The Course

A 156 km loop through the Ljubljana Basin and Julian Alps foothills. The route leaves Ljubljana, climbs gently over the Vrhnika Hill, traverses the Logatec Plateau, descends through the historic mercury-mining town of Idrija, then tackles the defining Kladje climb — 7 km at 6.3% average with a 10% maximum pitch. After a long, rough descent through alpine forest, the route passes through medieval Skofja Loka before rolling back to Ljubljana across pastoral terrain. The course is diverse: urban start, rolling plateau, alpine forest climbs, historic towns, and flat agricultural finish. Maximum elevation is 801m — this is a foothills event, not an Alpine epic.

Signature challenge: The Kladje climb: 7 km at 6.3% average with a maximum gradient of 10%. It arrives at approximately km 90 after the plateau crossings and the Idrija valley, on legs that have already absorbed 60+ km of rolling terrain. The climb itself is honest — no false flats, no respite sections. But the real test comes immediately after: a 10 km descent on rough road surface that demands concentration when you most want to coast.

MILE
Ljubljana to Vrhnika — The Warm-Up
Flat departure from BTC City through Ljubljana's outskirts before the first climb begins. The peloton thins out naturally as riders find their pace. Urban roads give way to the Vrhnika Hill — a 4.6 km opener at 4.1% average, 7% maximum. Not steep enough to cause damage but steep enough to remind you what is coming.
25
MILE
Logatec Plateau to Idrija — Rolling and Descending
The route crosses the Logatec Plateau — rolling terrain through the Slovenian Karst region — before turning toward Godovic and dropping into the Idrija valley. The descent from Godovic to Idrija is flagged as dangerous: steep, technical, and demanding attention. Idrija itself is a UNESCO-listed mercury mining town with centuries of history.
50
MILE
Cerkno to Kladje — The Crux
After passing through Cerkno and the valley near the Franja Partisan Hospital site, the road tilts upward for the defining climb. Kladje is 7 km at 6.3% average with a maximum gradient of 10%. This is the hardest sustained effort of the day — not extreme by Alpine standards, but honest and relentless. A refreshment point at the summit provides critical refueling.
60
MILE
Kladje Descent to Skofja Loka — The Dangerous Descent
From the Kladje summit, a 10 km descent begins that is notorious for rough road paving. The organizers flag this as dangerous, and with good reason — bad surfaces at speed with tired legs demand concentration. The route then enters medieval Skofja Loka, one of Slovenia's best-preserved old towns, before turning toward Vodice and the final run home.
80
MILE
Vodice to Ljubljana — The Run Home
From Skofja Loka through Vodice and Gameljne, the course descends gently and flattens for the final approach to BTC City. A short macadam section near Povodje adds texture before the Crnuski bridge crossing and the run along Smartinska cesta to the finish. The last 25 km are mentally easy but physically draining on empty legs.

Race Logistics

Airport
Ljubljana Joze Pucnik Airport (LJU) — 26 km from city center, 30-40 min by bus (EUR 4) or taxi. Limited international routes but growing. Alternative airports: Venice Marco Polo (VCE) — 4 hours. Vienna (VIE) — 3.5 hours. Munich (MUC) — 5 hours. Zagreb (ZAG) — 2 hours.
Lodging
Stay in Ljubljana city center — compact, walkable, and well-connected to the BTC City start/finish by public transport or a short ride. Hotels EUR 60-120/night, Airbnb options plentiful. The city center is charming with excellent restaurants and cafes. No need for a car — Ljubljana is one of Europe's most cycling-friendly capitals. For a quieter option, stay in Skofja Loka (on the course, 30 min from Ljubljana) for a medieval town atmosphere.
Food
Slovenian cuisine is a crossroads of Italian, Austrian, and Balkan influences. Struklji (rolled dumplings), potica (nut roll), jota (bean stew), local wines from Goriska Brda. Ljubljana's restaurant scene is excellent and affordable. Carb loading is effortless. Feed zones on the course provide food, drinks, and energy supplies at multiple points including the Kladje summit.
Packet Pickup
Number pickup in Ljubljana, typically 1-2 days before at the event venue. Bring ID and confirmation email. Well-organized with decades of experience handling thousands of international participants.
Parking
BTC City has ample parking — it is a large commercial center. Follow event-specific parking instructions. Public transport from Ljubljana center is convenient.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Maraton Franja BTC City?

+

Mid-June in Ljubljana is about as pleasant as cycling weather gets. Average 20C, highs of 25-28C, lows around 14C. Long daylight hours. The climate challenge is modest — this is not a heat event, not a cold event, not an extreme weather event. The caveat is that June is Ljubljana's wettest month. Afternoon thunderstorms can develop over the hills with reasonable frequency. But for most riders in most years, the weather is a non-issue.

How adventurous is Maraton Franja BTC City?

+

The route loops through genuinely attractive Slovenian countryside — the Ljubljana Basin, the foothills approaching the Julian Alps, the historic mercury-mining town of Idrija, the medieval center of Skofja Loka. The passage near the Franja Partisan Hospital adds a layer of historical depth that is unique among gran fondos. But this is not wilderness riding. The roads pass through populated areas, the scenery is pastoral rather than dramatic, and the terrain is pretty rather than jaw-dropping.

Should I race Maraton Franja BTC City?

+

Yes if you want a well-organized UCI gran fondo qualifier at a fraction of the cost of Italian or French alternatives. Yes if the historical connection to a WWII partisan hospital resonates. Yes if you want to discover Slovenia — an underrated European cycling destination. Skip if you need Alpine drama — the maximum elevation is 801m. Skip if you need a globally recognized name on your cycling resume.
