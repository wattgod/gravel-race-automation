The Ratings

Course Profile
Logistics 3/5

Competent Italian granfondo organization with chip timing, feed stations, and medical support. The 2025 move to start from Cesana Torinese improved safety on the descent into the Susa Valley. Turin airport (TRN) is 80 km away — roughly 1.5 hours by car or bus, making access easier than many deep-Alpine events. Sestriere and Cesana Torinese are small ski resort towns with limited but adequate summer infrastructure. Packet pickup and event logistics are straightforward for a field of 1,000-1,500 riders. Not the polished machine of the French Marmotte, but functional and improving with each edition.

Editorial
Prestige 3/5

The Colle delle Finestre has enormous prestige in cycling — it is arguably the most famous single climb in modern Giro d'Italia history. But the Granfondo Sestriere event itself is young. Founded in 2015 (initially as Marmotte Granfondo Sestriere), it separated from the Marmotte brand and reached its 10th edition in 2025. It does not carry the institutional weight of the 40-year-old French Marmotte, the Maratona dles Dolomites, or even the Sportful Dolomiti Race. The prestige is borrowed from the mountain rather than earned by the event — but what a mountain to borrow from.

Facts & History

Launched in 2015 as the Italian arm of the Marmotte Granfondo Series — branded 'La Marmotte — Sestriere — Colle delle Finestre' — alongside the original French Alps Marmotte and a Pyrenees edition. The event was conceived to bring organized amateur cycling to the legendary Colle delle Finestre, whose 7.8 km of unpaved road had become one of cycling's most iconic surfaces after its 2005 Giro d'Italia debut. The inaugural edition drew approximately 500 riders. By 2016, the second edition doubled participation to 1,109 entries. The event later separated from the Marmotte brand and now operates independently as the Granfondo Sestriere — Colle delle Finestre, reaching its 10th edition in 2025 with the start relocated to Cesana Torinese. The 2006 Turin Winter Olympics legacy infrastructure in the Via Lattea ski domain provides the event's physical framework. Founded in 2015. By Local Piedmont cycling organization, initially in partnership with the Marmotte Granfondo series.

Reputation: A young event riding on the shoulders of a legendary mountain. Colle delle Finestre needs no introduction in Italian cycling — the Giro d'Italia stages of 2005, 2015, and 2018 saw to that. The Granfondo is the only organized way to ride the full climb including the unpaved summit, and that singular distinction gives it outsized significance for its age.

The Course

A focused Alpine assault centered on one defining climb. The route through the Susa and Chisone Valleys is scenic and demanding, but everyone is here for the same reason: Colle delle Finestre. The course builds through steady valley climbs and fast descents before delivering you to the base of the Finestre's 18.5 km ascent. The first 10 km on tarmac are relentless (9.2% average), but the real event begins when the road surface changes. The final 7.8 km on dirt — switchbacking through larch forests with gradients reaching 14% — is the granfondo's entire reason for existence. The descent and return climb to Sestriere at 2,035m ensures the legs never fully recover.

Signature challenge: The 7.8 km of unpaved road on Colle delle Finestre. The transition from tarmac to dirt at approximately 1,700m altitude — on gradients that reach 14%, at oxygen levels measurably reduced by altitude — is the single most distinctive feature of any road granfondo in the world. You can train for the climbing. You cannot fully prepare for the psychological impact of losing your road surface when the gradient kicks hardest.

MILE
Susa Valley Descent and Approach
From Cesana Torinese (1,354m), the route descends into the Susa Valley through Oulx and Salbertrand before the road turns upward toward the Finestre. The descent is fast and the valley roads are gentle, lulling riders into a false sense of security. Use this section to conserve energy — everything ahead is climbing.
30
MILE
Colle delle Finestre — Paved Section
The first 10.7 km of the Finestre climb are on tarmac but the gradient is unforgiving — 9.2% average with sustained sections above 10%. The road narrows as it climbs through forests, with hairpin after hairpin revealing the scale of the mountain. The knowledge that the asphalt will end adds psychological weight to every pedal stroke.
37
MILE
Colle delle Finestre — The Dirt
The tarmac ends. Ahead: 7.8 km of hard-packed dirt and embedded gravel climbing to 2,178m with gradients reaching 14%. This is the exact surface where Giro d'Italia stages were won and lost — where Froome attacked at 80 km from the finish, where Aru dropped Contador, where Di Luca isolated Savoldelli. The gravel is rideable on road bikes but demands full attention: loose stones on steep gradients, exposed switchbacks with no shelter, and the thin air above 2,000m compounding every effort. The summit cairn is the psychological peak of the event.
55
MILE
Return to Sestriere
After the Finestre descent through Balboutet and Usseaux, the route climbs back through Pragelato and the Via Lattea ski domain to the finish at Sestriere (2,035m). Legs that were emptied on the Finestre must now find another 800-1,000m of climbing on tired muscles. The final ramps into Sestriere are the event's quiet cruelty — not famous, not iconic, just relentlessly uphill to an Olympic finish.

Race Logistics

Airport
Turin (TRN) — 80 km, ~1.5 hours by car. Budget airline options available. Milan Malpensa (MXP) — 220 km, ~2.5 hours. Cuneo (CUF) — 80 km, limited flights.
Lodging
Stay in Sestriere (finish town, 2,035m) or Cesana Torinese (start town, 1,354m) — both are ski resort towns with summer off-season rates. Hotels EUR80-140/night in July. Airbnb and apartment rentals available. Oulx (valley floor, 1,070m) offers additional options with easy access to the start. Book early for event weekend as rooms are limited in these small mountain towns.
Food
Piedmontese cuisine is among Italy's finest — risotto, agnolotti pasta, brasato (braised beef), local cheeses. Carb loading is effortless. Pre-race: seek out a proper trattoria in Cesana or Sestriere. Feed stations provide Italian cycling staples: panini, fruit, energy bars, water, Coca-Cola.
Packet Pickup
Event village area in Cesana Torinese or Sestriere (varies by year), day before or morning of. Manageable queues for a field of 1,000-1,500.
Parking
Available in Cesana Torinese and Sestriere. Ski resort infrastructure means parking is generally adequate in summer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Granfondo Sestriere — Colle delle Finestre?

+

Early July in the Piedmont Alps delivers manageable but variable conditions. Valley starts around Cesana Torinese (1,354m) are comfortably warm at 18-22C, but the Finestre summit at 2,178m will be 8-14C with wind — and on the exposed gravel road, there is no shelter from anything. The temperature range is less extreme than the French Galibier events (no sub-zero wind chill), but afternoon thunderstorms are a real possibility and can arrive fast in the Susa Valley. Bring arm warmers for the Finestre descent; you will regret not having them.

Sestriere and Cesana Torinese are small ski resort towns with limited but adequate summer infrastructure. Packet pickup and event logistics are straightforward for a field of 1,000-1,500 riders. Not the polished machine of the French Marmotte, but functional and improving with each edition.

How adventurous is Granfondo Sestriere — Colle delle Finestre?

+

This is where the Granfondo Sestriere separates itself from every other road granfondo in the world. The transition from tarmac to dirt on Colle delle Finestre is one of cycling's most iconic moments — the same surface where Savoldelli lost the Giro, where Froome launched his legendary 80 km attack, where Landa and Aru wrote their names into cycling history. Riding that gravel on a road bike at 2,000m, surrounded by larch forests and Alpine peaks, with the knowledge that the professionals suffered on this exact road, creates an adventure that no fully paved event can match. The Susa Valley, 2006 Winter Olympics infrastructure, and the Via Lattea ski domain add layers of scenic and cultural depth.

How prestigious is Granfondo Sestriere — Colle delle Finestre?

Should I race Granfondo Sestriere — Colle delle Finestre?

+

Yes if you want to ride cycling's most famous unpaved climb in an organized event. Yes if you value Giro d'Italia heritage and want to feel what the professionals felt on that dirt. Yes if you want a high-value Italian Alpine experience without the logistics of deep-Alpine events. Skip if you need a large, prestigious international granfondo. Skip if you want a 150+ km all-day epic — at 103 km, this is more focused than sprawling.
