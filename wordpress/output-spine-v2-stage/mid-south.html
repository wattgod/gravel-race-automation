Facts & History

Started in 2006 as 'Land Run 100' with 121 riders, 107 miles, 6,000+ ft elevation. Bobby discovered 'every direction two miles out of Stillwater we've got some of the best dirt roads on planet Earth.' Opened District Bicycles in Sept 2008. Renamed to 'The Mid South' in 2013. The finish-line hug tradition started year one—Bobby hugs every single finisher. 'I still need my Bobby Hug!' is a common refrain from returning riders who travel 1,000+ miles just for this race. The event grew explosively to 2,500+ today. Founded in 2006. By Bobby and Crystal Wintle (owners of District Bicycles, 712 South Main).

Reputation: The unofficial U.S. gravel season opener. Known for Bobby's finish-line hugs, unpredictable weather, iconic red clay, and one of gravel's strongest community vibes. The DFL (Dead F*cking Last) rider gets a giant steer skull trophy and massive celebration.

2019: Fast, dry conditions encouraged tactical pack racing
2024: Record-fast edition with average speeds over 20mph for front groups
2025: Race cancelled due to catastrophic wildfires and hurricane-force winds
2025: Ted King and Chase Wark rode unsupported FKT attempts, raising $8K+ for wildfire relief
2026: New format splits pro race (Friday) from amateur race (Saturday) for safety

Back 50 miles
More hilly than previous years with continuous climbing - riders warn there's going to be one climb coming after every descent.
East-West roads
Don't get sunlight this time of year, expect to see much mud and ice conditions.
Red dirt roads that can be chunky in sections. If the mud gets wet, it becomes very problematic for traction and bike handling.
Surprisingly not sketchy on descents despite being DIY roads, though riders report being puckered up on the descents due to conditions.
Beautiful destruction and despair - terrain that requires big tires for clearance issues, with winding roads through varied conditions.

RACE-SPECIFIC: THE MID SOUTH
HEAT PREP March in Stillwater, Oklahoma — Early March brings 40-75°F swings, potential freezing rain, potential heat, potential wildfires. Begin heat acclimatization 10–14 days before The Mid South — 20–30min sauna sessions or midday rides in full kit. Pre-load sodium 48 hours out. Target 500–750ml/hr with electrolytes on race day.
NUTRITION Target 60–80g carbs/hour for The Mid South’s 100 miles. Start fueling within the first 30 minutes — early fueling prevents late-race collapse. Bonking at mile 60 is a nutrition failure, not a fitness failure.
TERRAIN Mixed terrain at The Mid South requires surface adaptability. Include weekly rides on rolling red clay gravel to build confidence. Expect: oklahoma red clay roads, rolling hills, exposed ridgelines.

Race Logistics

Airport
Oklahoma City (OKC) or Tulsa (TUL) - both ~1.5 hours to Stillwater
Lodging
Book early (October when registration opens). Stillwater has limited lodging but more than you'd expect for a college town. Hampton Inn partnership. Camping options available downtown (including courthouse lawn). Expect $150-250/night for basic rooms. Volunteers get guaranteed 2027 registration as a perk.
Food
College town pricing = cheap. Lots of options downtown. Post-race celebration has food trucks and local beer. Stock your own race nutrition—aid stations are sparse.
Packet Pickup
Friday before race. Downtown Stillwater. Expect lines but not Unbound chaos.
Parking
Free parking available downtown. Easier than Unbound logistics.
RIDERS REPORT
Drink a lot of water the whole day, especially when it gets hot. Temperature management is crucial for the full distance.
Use momentum from descents to carry into the next climb because there's going to be one coming every time.
First 28 miles to Mulhall aid station are shared between 50 and 100 mile courses, expect it to be super fast with 1500 riders together.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at The Mid South?

+

Historical weather data shows average highs of 65°F and lows of 45°F, with a 37% chance of rain on any given day. Weather defines survival and tactics. The 2025 edition saw 60 mph winds and wildfires with Air Quality Index hitting 500+, while historical conditions create 'horror stories. Red mud deep enough for every letter of murder. Chains chewed. Derailleurs gargled and spat.' Rescue jeeps are prepositioned for mass extractions when Oklahoma's red clay becomes impassable. Among the most weather-friendly events, easier than Badlands.

How are the logistics for The Mid South?

+

When 2025's wildfires forced evacuation, Trix8806 had 'police knock on our camper door' with 'campers already burnt to the ground and fire on both sides of the road.' Bobby Wintle's personal touch shows in how he manages logistics—as Nic Morales observed: 'There was no worse place to talk to Bobby Wintle. Sitting in the middle of Stonecloud Brewery... He knew everyone. Everyone had something to say, and he had an impossibly equal, in-depth response to anyone who approached.'

How adventurous is The Mid South?

+

The route crosses tribal lands of 'the Quapaw, Kiowa, Osage, Kickapoo, Pawnee, Iowa and Wichita Nations' through private access and unopened infrastructure. The Gnarnia section provides adventure through unmaintained roads, while the Mega Mid South delivers 300 miles traversing 'the outer perimeter of every MID-SOUTH course over the past 13 years' with significant route variety through Oklahoma's diverse terrain.

How prestigious is The Mid South?

+

Joe Laverick calls this 'the best bike race experience of my life' and 'the true spirit of gravel.' Bobby Wintle notes 'the pros have looked at Mid South as the season opener' with elite riders treating it as their litmus test. When a brass band marches through 1,200 riders and Bobby hugs every finisher past midnight, you've built gravel culture beyond racing.

Should I race The Mid South?

+

If you value community, celebration, and authentic hospitality over predictable racing conditions—yes, absolutely. If you need to know what you're getting into before you commit—maybe reconsider. The Mid South is chaos wrapped in a Bobby hug. Some years it's fast and perfect. Some years it's a mud disaster. Some years it gets cancelled by wildfires. But the people who come back year after year aren't coming for the course—they're coming for the experience. Show up ready for anything. Leave with stories.
