The Ratings

Course Profile
Logistics 2/5

The nearest major airport is Portland International Jetport (PWM) in Portland, Maine — 87 miles and a 2-hour-15-minute drive. Boston Logan (BOS) is 3+ hours. Manchester-Boston Regional (MHT) is about 2.5 hours. None of these are close. The Auto Road sits on NH Route 16 in Pinkham Notch — 8 miles south of Gorham, 12 miles north of Jackson. Both are small White Mountains towns with limited lodging that books up fast for race weekend. No public transit options. Rental car mandatory. Packet pickup logistics are smooth, but getting here requires planning.

Editorial
Prestige 4/5

Running since 1973, this is one of the longest-running cycling events in the United States. The winners list reads like a who's who of American climbing: Tom Danielson (49:24, pre-paving record), Tyler Hamilton (four-time winner, records vacated), Phil Gaimon (50:38, first post-paving record), Ian Boswell (50:18, current record), and Jeannie Longo (58:14, pre-paving women's record). Called 'the toughest hillclimb in the US, if not the world.' Within American hill climb circles, this is the event. It does not have European monument status, but domestically, summiting Mt. Washington on a bicycle carries weight.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Mt. Washington Auto Road Bicycle Hillclimb (MWARBH)?

+

Mt. Washington is home to the worst weather in America. This is not marketing — the Mount Washington Observatory exists because this 6,288-foot peak generates weather conditions that rival mountains three times its height. The summit has recorded the highest wind gust ever directly measured on Earth's surface (231 mph in 1934). The yearly average temperature at the summit is 33.9F. In August, summit temperatures can drop below 40F with wind chill in the 20s. Fog, rain, sleet, and gale-force winds are not unusual on race day. You are racing into a weather system, not through one.

How are the logistics for Mt. Washington Auto Road Bicycle Hillclimb (MWARBH)?

+

The nearest major airport is Portland International Jetport (PWM) in Portland, Maine — 87 miles and a 2-hour-15-minute drive. Boston Logan (BOS) is 3+ hours. Manchester-Boston Regional (MHT) is about 2.5 hours. None of these are close. The Auto Road sits on NH Route 16 in Pinkham Notch — 8 miles south of Gorham, 12 miles north of Jackson. Both are small White Mountains towns with limited lodging that books up fast for race weekend. No public transit options. Rental car mandatory. Packet pickup logistics are smooth, but getting here requires planning.

How adventurous is Mt. Washington Auto Road Bicycle Hillclimb (MWARBH)?

+

You ride your bicycle to the summit of the highest peak in the Northeastern United States. You cross above treeline into an exposed alpine landscape of bare rock and scrub. The summit observatory, the cog railway tracks, and the panoramic views of the Presidential Range create an otherworldly finish. The weather adds genuine unpredictability — you might summit in sunshine or in a cloud. But the course is a paved auto road, 7.6 miles long, in New Hampshire. The adventure is real but compressed.

How prestigious is Mt. Washington Auto Road Bicycle Hillclimb (MWARBH)?

+

Running since 1973, this is one of the longest-running cycling events in the United States. The winners list reads like a who's who of American climbing: Tom Danielson (49:24, pre-paving record), Tyler Hamilton (four-time winner, records vacated), Phil Gaimon (50:38, first post-paving record), Ian Boswell (50:18, current record), and Jeannie Longo (58:14, pre-paving women's record). Called 'the toughest hillclimb in the US, if not the world.' Within American hill climb circles, this is the event. It does not have European monument status, but domestically, summiting Mt. Washington on a bicycle carries weight.

Should I race Mt. Washington Auto Road Bicycle Hillclimb (MWARBH)?

+

Yes if you are a hill climb specialist, power-to-weight obsessive, or simply need to know if you can ride up the steepest sustained paved climb in the Eastern US. Yes if you want a bucket-list summit finish on the highest peak in the Northeast. Skip if you want distance, technical terrain, or a mass-participation festival atmosphere. Skip if remote New Hampshire logistics are a dealbreaker.
