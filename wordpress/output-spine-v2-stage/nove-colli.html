Facts & History

In 1971, the Gruppo Sportivo Fausto Coppi Cesenatico — a cycling club named after Italy's greatest champion — organized a 200 km ride over nine hills in the Emilia-Romagna countryside. Seventeen riders started. The concept — a timed, organized ride over a fixed, challenging route, open to all — was new. They called it a 'gran fondo,' and the name stuck. The event grew steadily through the 1970s and 1980s, becoming a fixture of Italian amateur cycling. By the 1990s, 'gran fondo' had become a global cycling format, and Nove Colli was its acknowledged birthplace. The connection to cycling's legends is deep: the club is named for Coppi, and the start town is the birthplace of Marco Pantani — Il Pirata, the last Italian to win the Tour de France. Founded in 1971. By Gruppo Sportivo Fausto Coppi Cesenatico (cycling club named after the legendary Italian champion).

Reputation: The original gran fondo. Born in 1971, imitated worldwide, never replicated. Where the format that now defines amateur cycling was invented.

The Course

Nine punchy hills strung across 200 km of Emilia-Romagna countryside, starting and finishing at the Adriatic coast. The hills are not long — the longest is 9 km — but they are steep, with Barbotto's 18% pitches standing out as the route's signature gradient. Between hills, the terrain rolls through vineyards, olive groves, and small stone villages. The route is a loop from Cesenatico, climbing inland into the pre-Apennine foothills before descending back to the coast. The rhythm is distinctive: climb hard for 15-30 minutes, descend, roll through the valley, then climb again. Nine times.

Signature challenge: Barbotto at 18% — the steepest sustained gradient on the route and the climb that gives Nove Colli its fearsome reputation among punchy-hill specialists. But the real challenge is the cumulative effect: nine hills over 200 km, each one requiring an effort that leaves you slightly more depleted for the next. The ninth hill is always harder than the first.

25
MILE
Barbotto — The Signature Hill
372m of elevation in 5.5 km with a maximum gradient of 18%. This is the hill that defines Nove Colli. The opening ramps are steep (12-14%), then the road kicks up to 18% for a section that demands your lowest gear and your strongest legs. On 200 km-old legs in the full route, Barbotto is a wall. For many riders, this is the steepest road they'll ever ride.
50
MILE
Monte Pugliano — The Longest Climb
511m of elevation gain in 9 km — the longest and most sustained climb on the route. Gradients are more moderate than Barbotto (6-8% average) but the climb goes on and on. The reward at the top: views across the Emilia-Romagna hills toward the distant Apennine ridge. This is where steady climbers recoup time lost to explosive riders on steeper hills.
75
MILE
Middle Hills — Polenta through Perticara
Five hills in succession through the heart of the Romagnol countryside. Each one is individually manageable — 3-6 km, 200-350m of climbing — but the cumulative effect across 80+ km of rolling terrain drains your reserves. The villages between hills offer brief respite and the sight of espresso bars you cannot stop at. This is the attritional middle third.
110
MILE
Gorolo — The Final Hill
The ninth and final hill before the long descent back to Cesenatico and the Adriatic. Gorolo is not the hardest climb — but it comes at km 180+, on legs that have already climbed 3,400+ meters across eight previous hills. The descent from Gorolo back toward the coast is fast and freeing — the finish in Cesenatico's port area awaits.

Race Logistics

Airport
Rimini (RMI) — 25 minutes (closest, budget airline connections across Europe). Bologna (BLQ) — 1.5 hours (wider international connections). Forli (FRL) — 30 minutes (limited flights).
Lodging
Stay in Cesenatico — the start/finish town on the Adriatic coast. Hotels and apartments line the beach promenade. May is pre-peak season, so rates are reasonable (EUR80-150/night). Book 3-6 months in advance for event weekend. Alternatively, Rimini (25 min) offers a wider hotel selection with excellent beach-town dining.
Food
Emilia-Romagna is Italy's culinary heartland. Piadina romagnola (flatbread), passatelli in brodo, tagliatelle al ragu (the original Bolognese), squacquerone cheese. Carb loading has never been this good. Cesenatico's fish restaurants on the canal port are exceptional. Feed zones serve Italian pastries and local specialties alongside standard energy food.
Packet Pickup
Event village in Cesenatico, 1-2 days before. The expo combines registration, sponsor village, and the annual jersey unveiling. Well-organized for 10,000 riders.
Parking
Cesenatico manages event parking in designated areas. Follow event instructions — the small town center is closed for the start. Arriving the day before and parking at your hotel is the simplest approach.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Gran Fondo Nove Colli?

+

Late May on the Adriatic coast of Italy is generally pleasant — 13-21C, long daylight hours, warm afternoons. Not the extreme Alpine conditions that earn high climate scores. The risk is rain: May is the wettest month in the region, and wet 18% gradients on Italian provincial roads are no joke. But compared to the Galibier at 2,642m or wet cobblestones in April, the climate challenge here is moderate.

How adventurous is Gran Fondo Nove Colli?

+

The Emilia-Romagna countryside is charming — rolling green hills, small stone villages, vineyards, and olive groves. Cesenatico's canal port (designed by Leonardo da Vinci) is genuinely beautiful. But this is not the Dolomites or the cobblestones of northern France. The scenery is pleasant, not jaw-dropping. The adventure is cultural — riding through Pantani's homeland, past the espresso bars and trattorias of the Romagna — rather than geographical.

How prestigious is Gran Fondo Nove Colli?

+

Should I race Gran Fondo Nove Colli?

+

Yes if you want to ride the original gran fondo in the homeland of Italian cycling legends. Yes if nine punchy hills with 18% gradients over 200 km of Romagnol countryside is your pace. Skip if you want Alpine spectacle — these are hills, not mountains. Skip if you need English-language support — this event is authentically Italian.
