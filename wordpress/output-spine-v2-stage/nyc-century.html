Editorial
Prestige 3/5

The NYC Century is America's only all-urban 100-mile bike tour, and that distinction carries weight. Founded in 1990 as Transportation Alternatives' flagship event, it has 35 years of history and a genuine legacy in NYC cycling culture. At its peak in 2013, 7,000 riders registered. The event's return in 2025 after a six-year hiatus generated significant media coverage from Gothamist, Time Out, Streetsblog, and others. Within the NYC cycling community, the Century is a respected milestone. Nationally, it does not carry the prestige of a gran fondo or a major charity ride like the Pan-Mass Challenge — most cyclists outside the tristate area have never heard of it. Three points for strong regional prestige and a unique format, but limited national recognition.

Facts & History

When Transportation Alternatives launched the NYC Century in 1990, riding a bicycle in the five boroughs was practically an act of protest. New York City had a handful of bike lanes, cyclists were treated as obstacles by drivers and city planners alike, and the idea of 100 miles of urban cycling infrastructure was science fiction. TransAlt gathered 200 passionate cyclists and sent them on a 100-mile loop through New York City's neighborhoods — staking a claim to the streets and demanding to be seen and heard as a constituency. Over the next three decades, the NYC Century grew from that scrappy act of advocacy into America's only all-urban 100-mile bike tour, attracting up to 7,000 registrations at its 2013 peak. As New York City built over 1,000 miles of cycling infrastructure — protected bike lanes, greenways, bridge connections — the Century's route improved with the city. In 2019, Transportation Alternatives held what they called the '30th and final' NYC Century, citing declining registrations and the reality that producing a 100-mile urban event requires immense time, money, and coordination regardless of how many riders show up. But the cycling community would not let it go. In 2025, six years after the 'final' edition, the NYC Century returned — rebranded, refreshed, and sold out — proving that New York City's cycling community is larger, louder, and more committed than ever. The 2025 return honored Roger Herz, a longtime cycling and transit advocate who died in 2022 at age 88, and coincided with Climate Week NYC. Founded in 1990. By Transportation Alternatives.

Reputation: America's only all-urban century ride and a founding act of New York City's cycling advocacy movement. The ride that helped build the case for 1,000 miles of bike infrastructure — and then came back from the dead to prove the work is not finished.

The Course

The NYC Century is urban cycling distilled into 100 miles. Starting from the Meatpacking District before dawn, century riders roll out into a city that is still waking up — quiet streets, early light on the Hudson, and the rare sensation of having New York to yourself on a bicycle. The route weaves through four boroughs, each with its own character and rhythm. Manhattan's west side greenway provides a car-free opening stretch along the Hudson. Bridge crossings — Williamsburg, Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queensboro — deliver the postcard skyline views that make outsiders understand why New Yorkers put up with everything else. Brooklyn's waterfront greenway connects Williamsburg to Red Hook to Bay Ridge to Coney Island, with the Verrazano Bridge looming in the distance. Queens offers the southern shoreline and a perspective on the city that most visitors never see. The Bronx, reserved for the century riders, takes you into neighborhoods where cycling infrastructure is still catching up to the rest of the city — a reminder of why Transportation Alternatives exists. The route is self-guided on roads open to traffic, with GPS navigation and marshal support but no street closures. This means the experience is shaped by the city itself: traffic patterns, pedestrians, delivery trucks, construction zones. It is exhilarating and exhausting in equal measure. The final miles back to the Meatpacking District deliver the emotional payoff — 100 miles of the most dynamic city on Earth, absorbed at pedaling speed, culminating in a festival finish among riders who just did something that very few cyclists ever attempt.

Signature challenge: The signature challenge of the NYC Century is not a climb or a sprint — it is the sustained mental engagement of riding 100 miles on open roads through New York City. No street closures means you are sharing every mile with taxis, buses, delivery trucks, pedestrians, and the general organized chaos of a city of 8.3 million people. The physical effort is moderate — the terrain is flat — but the cognitive load of urban navigation, traffic awareness, and route-finding through four boroughs over 8-10 hours is unlike any other century ride in America.

MILE
Meatpacking District — Pre-Dawn Roll Out
Century riders check in at 5:30-6:30 AM on 10th Avenue between 13th and 14th Streets. The Meatpacking District is quiet at this hour — cobblestone streets, shuttered boutiques, and the High Line overhead. The energy at the start is nervous and electric. Roll out onto the Hudson River Greenway heading south, the river reflecting the first light of day. The field is small enough to feel intimate but large enough to feel like a movement.
20
MILE
Brooklyn Bridges and Waterfront
By mile 20, you have crossed your first iconic bridge — the Williamsburg or Manhattan Bridge — and descended into Brooklyn. The borough unfolds along its waterfront greenway: DUMBO with its cobblestones and Manhattan Bridge framing, Red Hook with its industrial grit, and the long stretch of the Brooklyn shoreline heading toward Bay Ridge. Traffic picks up as the city wakes up. The rest stops begin appearing, and you settle into the rhythm of urban century riding — pedal, navigate, absorb, repeat.
50
MILE
Coney Island and the Shoreline
The halfway mark arrives at or near Coney Island — the boardwalk, the Atlantic Ocean, and the surreal juxtaposition of amusement park rides and the open sea. This is the emotional midpoint of the ride. You are 50 miles into a 100-mile urban ride, your legs are talking, and you are watching the ocean. The shoreline routes push along NYC's southern coast with sweeping Atlantic views. The wind off the water can be a factor here.
75
MILE
Queens and the Bronx — The Commitment Miles
The final quarter of the century route takes you into Queens and then the Bronx — boroughs where the cycling infrastructure is thinner and the riding demands more attention. These are the commitment miles. Your legs have 75 miles on them, the city is fully awake and at full volume, and you are navigating neighborhoods where bike lanes give way to shared roads. The Bronx waterfront offers a reward — river views and a sense that you are riding through the part of the city that needs cycling advocacy the most.
100
MILE
The Meatpacking Finish — Festival
The final bridge crossing back into Manhattan delivers the skyline one last time, and the closing miles down the west side greenway return you to where you started — the Meatpacking District, now fully alive with the Finish Festival. Finisher medal, food, music, and the company of several thousand cyclists who just rode 100 miles through four boroughs of the most intense city on Earth. The High Line watches from above. You have done something very few cyclists ever do.

Race Logistics

Airport
Three major airports serve NYC: John F. Kennedy International (JFK), LaGuardia (LGA), and Newark Liberty International (EWR). All are 30-60 minutes from the Meatpacking District start/finish by taxi or transit. JFK and EWR have direct AirTrain connections to the subway/NJ Transit. Most participants are local NYC-area residents.
Lodging
Single-day event — no overnight required for local riders. For out-of-town participants, book a hotel in Manhattan's Meatpacking District, Chelsea, or West Village neighborhoods for walking distance to the start. The 5:30 AM century start means you want to minimize morning logistics. NYC hotel rates in September average $250-$400/night. Budget options include hotels in Jersey City (PATH train to 14th Street) or Brooklyn (L train to 14th Street). Book early — September is peak NYC tourism season and Climate Week drives additional demand.
Food
Six rest stops across Brooklyn, Queens, and the Bronx with snacks, water, and marshal support. Finish Festival in the Meatpacking District with food and celebration. NYC's density means you are never far from a bodega, deli, or coffee shop if you need to supplement the official rest stops. Carry your own nutrition for the first 15-20 miles before the first rest stop.
Packet Pickup
Check-in at the Start Festival in the Meatpacking District (10th Avenue between 13th and 14th Streets). Staggered check-in times by distance: 100-mile riders at 5:30-6:30 AM, shorter distances later. VIP riders get priority first-wave access. Details shared via email approximately one week before the event.
Parking
NYC parking is expensive and limited. Strongly recommend taking public transit to the start — the Meatpacking District is served by the A/C/E and L subway lines (14th Street and 8th Avenue stations). If you must drive, expect to pay $30-$60 for a garage in the area. Street parking in the Meatpacking District is extremely limited on a Sunday morning.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at NYC Century Bike Tour?

+

Late September in New York City is pleasant cycling weather — typically 60-75F with moderate humidity. The early morning century start at 5:30 AM means cool conditions for the first few hours, warming to comfortable temperatures by midday. The urban heat island effect adds a few degrees in dense areas, and sun exposure over 100 miles of riding is real. Occasional September thunderstorms are possible but not probable. This is not a climate challenge event — the weather window is well-chosen. Two points because the conditions are manageable but not trivially easy: humidity, sun exposure, and the possibility of rain on open roads with traffic are legitimate considerations.

How adventurous is NYC Century Bike Tour?

+

Riding 100 miles through four boroughs of New York City is, objectively, an adventure. You cross iconic bridges with skyline views, ride through Coney Island and the Brooklyn waterfront, push into the Bronx neighborhoods most cyclists never visit, and navigate the organized chaos of the world's most famous city entirely by bicycle. The diversity of neighborhoods — Meatpacking District to Williamsburg to Coney Island to the South Bronx — creates a genuine urban exploration experience. This is not wilderness adventure, but it is culturally rich and visually spectacular. Three points because the urban adventure factor is real and unique in American cycling, even if the terrain itself is unremarkable.

Three points for strong regional prestige and a unique format, but limited national recognition.

Should I race NYC Century Bike Tour?

+

You do not race the NYC Century — you ride it as a statement. Yes if you want to experience New York City by bicycle in a way no other event offers — 100 miles through four boroughs, across iconic bridges, along waterfronts, and through neighborhoods that most cyclists never visit. Yes if you support cycling advocacy and want your registration dollars to fund Transportation Alternatives' work building safer streets. Yes if you are an experienced urban cyclist comfortable navigating traffic without closed roads. Skip if you need competitive timing, challenging terrain, or a closed-course experience. Skip if you are uncomfortable riding in traffic — this is not a beginner-friendly century, despite the shorter distance options.
