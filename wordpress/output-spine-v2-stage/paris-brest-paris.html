Course Profile
Logistics 4/5

The qualification pathway alone is a multi-year commitment. You need to complete a Super Randonneur series — 200, 300, 400, and 600 km brevets — in the year of PBP, plus a BRM in the prior year for priority registration. Facts & History

In 1891, journalist Pierre Giffard organized a bicycle race from Paris to Brest and back — 1,200 km — as a publicity stunt for his newspaper Le Petit Journal. Charles Terront won in 71 hours 37 minutes on a fixed-gear bicycle, captivating the entire nation. The race's success directly inspired the creation of the Tour de France (1903) and contributed to Pierre de Coubertin's revival of the Olympic Games (1896). The Paris-Brest pastry was invented by pâtissier Louis Durand to commemorate the first edition. Professional racing continued through 1951, but the Audax Club Parisien began organizing a parallel randonneur edition in 1931. Today, PBP is exclusively a randonneur event — non-competitive, timed, and open to all who qualify. Twenty editions have been held in the randonneur format, with the 21st scheduled for August 2027. Founded in 1891. By Pierre Giffard (Le Petit Journal newspaper).

Reputation: The oldest cycling event still running. The event that started everything. Every serious long-distance cyclist's ultimate goal.

1891: Charles Terront wins the first PBP in 71h37m, captivating France and inspiring the Tour de France
1891: The Paris-Brest pastry is created by Louis Durand to commemorate the event
1931: First randonneur edition organized by Audax Club Parisien — 44 participants including 5 women
1951: Final professional race; PBP becomes exclusively a randonneur event
2023: Record 6,496 participants from 67 countries; Cerberus heatwave tests riders with 30C+ temperatures
2023: Nick DeHaan sets new unofficial fastest time of 41:46:30 (averaging 29.18 km/h)

The Course

A 1,219 km journey through the heart of France. The route rolls southwest from Rambouillet through the gentle hills of Normandy and the Perche, then west through Brittany's increasingly rolling interior to the Atlantic coast at Brest. The return follows a similar path. No single climb is memorable. No section is particularly technical. The character emerges from the accumulation — 11,750 meters of relentless undulation over 757 miles, three nights of riding, and the psychological weight of knowing you are doing what cyclists have done since 1891.

Signature challenge: Not a single climb or section — it is the totality. 1,219 km, 11,750m of climbing, 90 hours, three nights, sleep deprivation, and the knowledge that 130 years of cyclists have faced the same road. PBP's challenge is existential, not physical.

Third Night / Hallucination Zone
Sleep deprivation peaks. Riders report seeing things — animals, people, buildings — that are not there. Decision-making deteriorates. The body operates on reserves it did not know it had. This is where PBP separates finishers from abandoners.

The Final Approach
Rambouillet is close enough to smell. The last 50 km are ridden on pure emotion. Tears at the finish are not uncommon. You have joined the Great Book.
RIDERS REPORT
First 600 kilometers headwinds
The first half features strong headwinds that can be mentally challenging. One rider considered quitting after 200 kilometers due to feeling depleted from constant headwinds.
Overnight period
Cycling through the night requires lights and proper preparation. Riders describe very often cycling through the dead of night with little or no sleep.
Control points timing
Reaching control points within allotted time is critical. Time becomes of the essence especially in the latter portions of the race.
Strong headwinds dominate the first 600 kilometers making it very challenging and mentally taxing for riders.
Conditions can vary from icy cold, driving snow, heavy rain, strong winds with very often cycling through the dead of night.

Race Logistics

Airport
CDG (Paris Charles de Gaulle) or ORY (Paris Orly) — TGV/RER + regional train to Rambouillet (~1-1.5 hours). Rambouillet is 50 km southwest of Paris.
Lodging
Book Rambouillet accommodation 6-12 months early — the town of 26,000 fills completely. Alternatives: Versailles (20 min by train), Paris (45 min). During the ride, sleep at controls (cots available for a fee) or micro-nap roadside. Post-ride: stay in Rambouillet for recovery or take the train to Paris.
Food
Controls provide hot meals, sandwiches, coffee, and snacks (paid, not included in entry). Between controls, French bakeries and cafes along the route. Carry sufficient food for 50-80 km gaps between controls. Night closures of village shops require planning.
Packet Pickup
Saturday before the start at Rambouillet (Chateau de Rambouillet grounds). Includes mandatory reflective vest, brevet card, frame plates, timing chip, and safety briefing.
Parking
Limited parking at Rambouillet start/finish. Public transport strongly recommended. Shuttle services typically organized by ACP.
RIDERS REPORT
Riders burn about 10,000 calories in the first half, expecting 40,000 calories total by the end.
Pack Imodium as a precaution because when riding long distances and eating a lot, stomach issues can occur.
Make sure bicycle is in the best possible condition you can afford to optimize chances of success at completing the event.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Paris-Brest-Paris Randonneur?

+

Late August in France is usually pleasant for cycling — warm but manageable. The 2023 edition proved the exception: the Cerberus heatwave pushed temperatures above 30C and caused widespread suffering among riders unaccustomed to sustained heat. Brittany brings its own challenges — cooler temperatures, potential rain, and coastal wind. But the real climate challenge is exposure duration: riding through 3 AM cold after a 30C afternoon, then back into heat the next day. The body's thermoregulation breaks down after 48+ hours of exertion.

How adventurous is Paris-Brest-Paris Randonneur?

+

PBP is one of cycling's great adventures — not because of extreme terrain, but because of what 1,200 km does to the human mind. Sleep-deprived riders hallucinate. Villages appear like mirages at 4 AM. The turnaround at Brest, seeing the Atlantic Ocean after 600 km of riding, is a moment of profound emotional release. Marc-Olivier Meunier called it '1219km of party.' The adventure is internal — a multi-day exploration of your own limits, framed by the French countryside, village support, and the company of thousands of fellow travelers from every corner of the world.

There is simply nothing else like it in cycling.

Should I race Paris-Brest-Paris Randonneur?

+

Yes if you are a committed endurance cyclist willing to invest two years in qualification and training. Yes if the history and community of cycling matter to you. Yes if you want to join the most international cycling event in the world. Skip if you want dramatic terrain, technical challenge, or competitive rankings. Skip if you cannot commit to the multi-year qualification pathway. And skip if you cannot handle the idea of riding through the night for three consecutive nights.
