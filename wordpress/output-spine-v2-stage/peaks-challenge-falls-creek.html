The Ratings

Course Profile
Logistics 3/5

Falls Creek is remote by international standards — 4.5 hours from Melbourne, which is itself a long-haul flight from anywhere outside Oceania. The drive up through the Victorian High Country is scenic but winding. Accommodation in Falls Creek village is limited and books out months in advance for event weekend. The event organization by Bicycle Network is professional and experienced, but the geographic isolation is the limiting factor.

Editorial
Prestige 3/5

Within Australian cycling, this is the event. Completing the 3 Peaks carries real cachet — it's the local equivalent of finishing La Marmotte or the Maratona. But internationally, the event is largely unknown outside Oceania. It lacks the Tour de France associations, the decades of history, or the global participant base that elevate European cyclosportives.

Facts & History

Created by Bicycle Network to give Australian cyclists a genuine alpine challenge that could stand alongside the European mountain cyclosportives. The route connecting three Victorian alpine ski resorts — Falls Creek, Mount Hotham, and via Tawonga Gap — leverages the best climbing terrain in mainland Australia. The 3 Peaks concept became the defining challenge in Australian road cycling within a few years of launch. Founded in 2010. By Bicycle Network (Australia's largest cycling organization).

Reputation: The 3 Peaks. If you've finished it, you belong to a club. Australia's most respected single-day cycling achievement.

2012: Inaugural edition establishes the 3 Peaks format
Mount Hotham's 30 km climb becomes recognized as one of Australia's great road climbs
Event grows to 2,000-3,000 riders, becoming Australia's premier climbing sportive
Bushfire years test event resilience and rider toughness

The Course

A linear epic across the Victorian Alps. The three-peak structure gives the ride a natural narrative: conquer Hotham, survive the valley heat, endure Tawonga, and somehow find the legs for Falls Creek at the end. The terrain alternates between long sustained climbs to cool alpine summits and hot, exposed valley roads where the sun beats down relentlessly. There is almost no flat ground — you are either climbing, descending, or wishing you were descending.

Signature challenge: The cumulative weight of 235 km and 4,000m. No single climb is the hardest you'll ever ride, but stacking three alpine summits in a single day — with valley heat between them — creates an event where the whole is devastatingly greater than the sum of its parts.

MILE
Falls Creek to Hotham
The day begins in darkness with the first major climb: Mount Hotham. 30 km of sustained climbing from the valley floor to 1,861m — the longest single effort of the day. Gradients average 4-5% with steeper sections near the summit. Dawn breaks during the ascent, and the views from the top across the High Country are the first reward. The descent into Harrietville (30+ km) is fast and requires concentration on cold, stiff legs.
70
MILE
Valley Inferno and Tawonga Gap
The valley roads between Hotham and the Tawonga Gap climb are where the heat hits hardest. By late morning, temperatures can reach 30-35C on exposed roads with minimal shade. Hydration management becomes critical. Tawonga Gap is shorter but punchy — a sharp climb that tests legs already worn from Hotham and the heat.
120
MILE
The Final Ascent to Falls Creek
The final climb to Falls Creek begins at kilometer 200+. You've been riding for 10+ hours. Your legs know the mileage. The climb to 1,780m is the psychological crux — not the steepest or longest, but the one that demands the most willpower. The summit finish in Falls Creek village, surrounded by exhausted riders who have shared the same 235 km journey, is the defining moment.
RIDERS REPORT
Tawonga Gap
An FTP test climb of just over 20 minutes that's consistently steep. Riders describe it as essentially riding the climb at threshold, especially when strong riders like Mark O'Brien push the pace.
The Monster
An hour to hour and a half of climbing including Hotham, frying pan sections that riders describe as particularly challenging.
Opening descent
45 minute Alpine descent in the dark with 2,500+ riders that's fast flowing but has potholes that can cause riders to lose bottles.

Race Logistics

Airport
Melbourne (MEL) — 4.5 hours drive. No closer commercial airports. Most riders drive up and stay in Falls Creek or nearby Bright/Mount Beauty.
Lodging
Stay in Falls Creek village for proximity to the start/finish. Ski lodges operate in summer at lower rates but availability is limited — book 3-6 months ahead. Bright (1.5 hours from Falls Creek) is the larger town alternative with more options. Mount Beauty (base of the Falls Creek climb) is another option.
Food
Alpine Australian — pub meals, bakeries, and basic supplies in Falls Creek village. More dining options in Bright. Self-catering in a ski lodge is common. Feed stations provide standard cycling nutrition — gels, bananas, sandwiches, electrolytes.
Packet Pickup
Falls Creek village, day before the event. Combined with rider briefing.
Parking
Falls Creek village parking. Limited but manageable for the 2,000-3,000 rider field.
RIDERS REPORT
Hydration is critical - one rider lost a bottle on the opening descent and described having only 750ml for a 7-hour ride as a serious problem that just won't fly.
Pack emergency bailout bags for feed stations - one rider sent bags to both dinner point and Angler's Rest with extra carbs, bars, and even a mini can of Coke.
Focus on managing efforts, keeping fuel going in, and having strategy to follow moves and adapt accordingly rather than just completing the event.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Peaks Challenge Falls Creek?

+

The heat is the defining climate challenge. Valley floors between the peaks can hit 35C in March, and you're riding through them in the middle of the day when the sun is at its most intense. Australian UV is no joke — this close to the ozone hole, sunburn happens fast. The contrast with cool summit temperatures creates clothing and hydration challenges. Bushfire smoke in bad years adds a respiratory dimension that European events don't contend with.

How are the logistics for Peaks Challenge Falls Creek?

+

Falls Creek is remote by international standards — 4.5 hours from Melbourne, which is itself a long-haul flight from anywhere outside Oceania. The drive up through the Victorian High Country is scenic but winding. Accommodation in Falls Creek village is limited and books out months in advance for event weekend. The event organization by Bicycle Network is professional and experienced, but the geographic isolation is the limiting factor.

How adventurous is Peaks Challenge Falls Creek?

+

The Victorian High Country is genuinely remote and beautiful — eucalyptus forests, alpine meadows, panoramic views from summit ridgelines. The three-peak structure gives the ride a narrative arc: conquer one mountain, descend into the valley, face the next one. The isolation between peaks — long stretches with minimal habitation — creates a sense of journey that loop courses lack. This feels like an expedition, not a lap.

How prestigious is Peaks Challenge Falls Creek?

+

Within Australian cycling, this is the event. Completing the 3 Peaks carries real cachet — it's the local equivalent of finishing La Marmotte or the Maratona. But internationally, the event is largely unknown outside Oceania. It lacks the Tour de France associations, the decades of history, or the global participant base that elevate European cyclosportives.

Should I race Peaks Challenge Falls Creek?

+

Yes if you want a genuine ultra-distance mountain cycling challenge outside Europe. Yes if completing Australia's most respected single-day cycling event is on your list. Skip if you can't commit to training for 10-14 hours in the saddle. Skip if the 4.5-hour drive from Melbourne (or long-haul flight from overseas) is a dealbreaker.
