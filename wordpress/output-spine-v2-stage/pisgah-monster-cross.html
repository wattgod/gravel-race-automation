Facts & History

Pisgah Monster Cross founded ~2012. Southeast gravel classic. Pisgah National Forest. 69 miles, 300-400 riders. Technical Appalachian terrain. Pisgah Forest, North Carolina (near Brevard). Founded in ~2012. By Pisgah Productions.

Reputation: Southeast gravel classic. Technical Pisgah terrain. 15-20% DNF.

2024: Course altered by Hurricane Helene storm damage in the region (per rider accounts) — terrain 'kind of different' from prior editions
2025: Course held with a mix of chunky gravel, double-track, and technical singletrack; a competitive rider called the singletrack climb 'the highlight of the course because it's like a mountain bike race'
[03]

The Course

69 miles, ~8,777 ft (organizer figure; has varied by year/direction). Gravel and paved forest roads through Pisgah and Nantahala National Forests with brief technical singletrack sections. Technical rating: 5/5.

Signature challenge: Technical Appalachian terrain (5/5) + steep, year-variable climbing (~8,777 ft over 69mi in the current course).

RIDERS REPORT
Joel Branch climb
Over 20 minutes of climbing that includes a super steep switchback single track section where riders hit threshold power. Adam was hitting threshold up this part according to Sierra.
Fish hatchery descent
Downhill section with water puddles that come out of nowhere, lots of brake bumps. Sierra called it the cross part of monster cross due to the water hazards.
Opening climb
10-15 minute climb on double track ATV trail with rutting and deeper gravel in the tracks. Riders go pretty hard from the start.
Gravel roads are pretty chunky and rugged. Hurricane damage made the roads more torn up and rougher than before. You have to take choice lines because of rutting and deeper gravel in the tracks.
Double track feels like an ATV trail or jeep trail with some single track sections that include roots to navigate through.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Pisgah Monster Cross?

+

Early September in the North Carolina mountains typically brings mild temperatures but unpredictable conditions. The forest canopy provides shade but also holds humidity, while elevation changes create microclimates. Without specific weather data, you're rolling dice on conditions that could range from perfect to miserable. Similar climate challenges to SBT GRVL. As Dicky/TeamDicky puts it: "It honestly was a fantastical day on a bike in perfect weather with occasional good company."

How are the logistics for Pisgah Monster Cross?

+

Friday evening and Saturday morning packet pickup from a field across US276 from Davidson River Campground covers the basics. Carey Lowery praised "NASCAR style aid stations" and "well organized" volunteer efforts, though Dave Jordan noted navigation issues when his Garmin loaded the wrong year's map mid-race.

How adventurous is Pisgah Monster Cross?

+

Threading through Pisgah and Nantahala National Forests via routes like 475B and Indian Creek Road delivers backcountry remoteness. Matt Sweeney's modified route covered "110.5 miles and 18,600 ft of elevation," noting it was "tough to make that left to go back up 475b instead of just rolling back to the parking lot."

How prestigious is Pisgah Monster Cross?

+

Pisgah Productions bills this as the 'premiere all-road racing event in the region,' but that's regional fame at best. The Pisgah name carries weight among Southeast gravel nerds who know what 8,777 feet of climbing through Nantahala National Forest can do to your legs. Without notable winners or major media coverage in the search results, it's respected but not revered.

Should I race Pisgah Monster Cross?

+

You need legitimate climbing legs and comfort grinding for 14 hours through National Forest terrain that rewards bike handling over pure watts. The three aid stations mean you're mostly self-sufficient between long stretches of Pisgah's relentless rollers. Show up undertrained to this one and you'll discover new definitions of suffering before that 10PM cutoff.
