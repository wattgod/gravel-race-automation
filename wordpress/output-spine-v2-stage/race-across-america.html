The Ratings

Course Profile
Logistics 2/5

RAAM is a logistical nightmare by design. Solo riders need a crew of 8-12 people, two or more support vehicles, and a leapfrogging operation that runs 24 hours a day for 8-12 days across 3,000 miles and 12 states. Marko Baloh explains: the crew must manage 'nutrition, navigation, safety, medical monitoring, and morale around the clock while leapfrogging vehicles across 3,000 miles.' You need qualifying ultra-distance results just to enter. Crew members take shifts following the rider through the night on unlit highways. The entry fee is just the beginning — vehicles, fuel, food, lodging for a dozen people across 12 days is where the real cost lives. There is no event infrastructure beyond 10 time station checkpoints. Everything else is on you.

Editorial
Prestige 5/5

RAAM is the most prestigious ultra-endurance cycling event in the world. Founded in 1982 as The Great American Bike Race, it has crowned legends like Lon Haldeman, Pete Penseyres, Jure Robic, and Christoph Strasser. Fewer than 300 solo cyclists have ever finished. The 50% solo DNF rate means that starting RAAM is an achievement; finishing it is extraordinary. The race has been featured in multiple documentaries, books, and global media coverage. Christoph Strasser's six victories and the late Jure Robic's legendary battles have become cycling mythology. In 2021, Leah Goldstein became the first woman to win the overall solo division, adding another chapter to RAAM's storied history.

Facts & History

In 1982, John Marino organized The Great American Bike Race with four competitors: himself, John Howard, Michael Shermer, and Lon Haldeman. They raced from Santa Monica, California to the Empire State Building in New York City. Haldeman won that first edition. The following year, the event was renamed Race Across America and participation shifted from invitation to qualification-based entry. Over four decades, RAAM has grown from a four-person novelty into the most prestigious ultra-endurance cycling event on the planet, spawning solo, two-person, four-person, and eight-person team categories. Founded in 1982. By John Marino.

Reputation: The most extreme ultra-endurance cycling event in the world. The Everest of cycling. Featured in the documentaries 'Bicycle Dreams' and 'Race Across America.' Fewer than 300 solo finishers in 40+ years of history.

1982: First edition as The Great American Bike Race — Lon Haldeman wins the inaugural coast-to-coast race
1986: Pete Penseyres sets solo record of 8 days, 9 hours, 47 minutes that stood for years
2004: Team Action Sports sets 4-person record averaging 23.06 mph across 2,959 miles
2005-2010: Jure Robic's legendary era — the Slovenian won five times and became RAAM's most iconic figure before his death in 2011
2014: Christoph Strasser sets the current solo record of 7 days, 15 hours, 56 minutes
2019: Strasser claims his sixth victory, passing Robic as the all-time solo wins leader
2021: Leah Goldstein becomes the first woman to win the overall solo division
2022: Team Bemer sets 8-person record of 5 days, 1 hour, 52 minutes at 24.85 mph average

The Course

A 3,000-mile transcontinental odyssey across the full diversity of the American landscape. Desert, mountains, plains, ridgelines, and coastline — all in a single continuous effort.

Signature challenge: 3,000 miles across the entire continental United States — desert heat, Rocky Mountain passes, Great Plains wind, Appalachian climbs, and 175,000 feet of elevation gain — all in a single continuous, nonstop effort under 12 days.

MILE
Mojave and Sonoran Deserts
Temperatures exceed 110F as riders cross the California and Arizona deserts. The first major attrition point. Heat management defines survival.

MILE
Monument Valley to the Rockies
The Utah-Colorado transition. Iconic Southwest scenery gives way to serious mountain climbing. Elevation begins its relentless accumulation.
1000
MILE
Rocky Mountain Passes
Multiple passes above 10,000 feet including Wolf Creek Pass. Freezing temperatures, thin air, and the first major climbing block. The Rockies break riders who survived the desert.
1500
MILE
Kansas and the Great Plains
Hundreds of miles of flat, exposed prairie. Wind becomes the primary adversary. Thunderstorms appear without warning. The psychological toll of endless straight roads compounds sleep deprivation.
2200
MILE
Mississippi River to the Ozarks
Crossing the Mississippi marks the eastern half of the race. Missouri's Ozark hills are deceptively punishing for riders on day 7-9 of continuous effort.
2700
MILE
Appalachian Mountains
The final mountain range. West Virginia and Pennsylvania deliver relentless climbing on exhausted legs. Humidity and rain compound the suffering. The last major physical barrier before the Atlantic.
3000
MILE
Atlantic City Finish
The final miles to the Atlantic City boardwalk. After 3,000 miles, riders finish where the continent ends. The emotional release at the finish line is legendary.

RACE-SPECIFIC: RACE ACROSS AMERICA (RAAM)
HEAT PREP June in Oceanside, CA to Atlantic City, NJ — RAAM traverses every climate zone in the continental United States. Riders start in California coastal morning and within hours enter the Mojave and Sonoran Deserts where temperatures exceed 110F (43C). The Rockies bring freezing mountain passes above 10,000 feet. Kansas and the Great Plains deliver 95F+ heat, brutal headwinds, and violent thunderstorms. The Appalachians and Eastern Seaboard bring humidity, rain, and fog. All of this happens in a single continuous effort over 8-12 days with no shelter except what the crew provides. Begin heat acclimatization 10–14 days before Race Across America (RAAM) — 20–30min sauna sessions or midday rides in full kit. Pre-load sodium 48 hours out. Target 500–750ml/hr with electrolytes on race day. Race-day realities: Desert heat exceeding 110F (43C) through California, Arizona, and Utah; Eastern humidity and summer heat through the Appalachians.
NUTRITION Ultra-distance fueling for 3000 miles: 80–100g carbs/hour from mile 1 — don’t wait until you’re hungry. Practice your exact race-day nutrition in every long training ride. Carry backup calories. 3000 miles burns 8,000–12,000+ calories — you cannot replace them all, but you must try.
ALTITUDE Race Across America (RAAM) with 175,000ft of climbing, much of it above 8,000ft demands altitude preparation. Arrive 5–7 days early for acclimatization. Expect 5–15% power reduction at altitude. Increase iron intake 4 weeks out. Hydrate aggressively — altitude increases fluid loss by 20–40%.

Race Logistics

Airport
San Diego International (SAN) — 35 miles from Oceanside start. For finish: Atlantic City International (ACY) or Philadelphia (PHL).
Lodging
Pre-race lodging in Oceanside, CA. During the race, crew and rider operate from support vehicles — RVs, vans, and motels along the route. Post-race accommodation in Atlantic City, NJ. Crew lodging is the largest variable cost.
Food
All nutrition is self-supplied via crew. Riders consume 8,000-10,000 calories per day. Crew prepares food in support vehicles and hands off at designated exchange points. No aid stations or race-provided nutrition.
Packet Pickup
Pre-race briefing and vehicle inspection in Oceanside, typically 2-3 days before start. All vehicles and crew must pass safety inspection.
Parking
Oceanside Pier area for start. Support vehicles follow prescribed rules for leapfrogging along the route.
RIDERS REPORT
The race has a bike path that allows riders to get from the beach inland through Oceanside safely, avoiding cars on the road and providing easier access out of town with police escorts.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Race Across America (RAAM)?

+

No cycling event on Earth subjects riders to a wider range of climate conditions. The Mojave Desert pushes temperatures above 110F. Rocky Mountain passes bring near-freezing temperatures and potential snow in June. Kansas delivers 95-degree heat, massive headwinds, and violent thunderstorms with lightning. The Appalachians bring humidity and fog. Riders experience thermal swings of 60+ degrees Fahrenheit within a single 24-hour period. One RAAM veteran described crossing the desert as 'riding inside a convection oven' followed by 'shivering uncontrollably on a Rocky Mountain descent twelve hours later.' The body never adapts because the climate never stabilizes.

How are the logistics for Race Across America (RAAM)?

+

How adventurous is Race Across America (RAAM)?

+

RAAM is a coast-to-coast crossing of the United States by bicycle. Monument Valley at sunrise. The Continental Divide. The Kansas prairie stretching to every horizon. Appalachian ridgelines in morning mist. The Atlantic City boardwalk finish after 3,000 miles. The adventure is not just physical — it is psychological. Riders hallucinate from sleep deprivation. They experience emotional breakdowns and euphoric breakthroughs. They watch the sun rise and set eight to twelve times in a single continuous effort. The documentary 'Bicycle Dreams' captures this perfectly: riders pushed so far beyond normal human limits that the experience becomes almost spiritual. This is adventure in its most distilled form.

+

Should I race Race Across America (RAAM)?

+

If you are an elite ultra-endurance cyclist with $15,000-$100,000 to spend, a crew of 8-12 people willing to sacrifice two weeks, qualifying results from approved ultra events, and the physical and mental capacity to ride 20+ hours a day for 8-12 consecutive days across the entire continental United States — yes. For everyone else, RAAM is best appreciated from the crew van, the documentary couch, or the dot-watching screen.
