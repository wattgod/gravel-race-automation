Facts & History

Rad am Ring began in 2003 as a small 24-hour road bike race with approximately 70 participants on the Nurburgring. The concept was simple but audacious: use the world's most famous motorsport circuit for bicycle racing. The Nurburgring's Nordschleife, built in 1927 and nicknamed the 'Green Hell' by Jackie Stewart, had never hosted organized cycling competition at scale. The event grew steadily, adding public races, mountain bike disciplines, and touring options. In 2016, a professional road race — the Rudi Altig Race — was added to honor German cyclist Rudi Altig, who won the Road World Championship at the Nurburgring in 1966. Altig died weeks before the inaugural pro race, adding poignant significance. Paul Voss won the first edition in a 70 km solo breakaway. The pro race ran as a UCI Europe Tour 1.1 event before moving to the German Bundesliga. By 2025, the event had grown to nearly 12,000 participants, with 24h race slots selling out in under one hour. In 2025, a gravel race was added, and the 2026 edition will host the German Gravel Championship. Founded in 2003. By Eventwerkstatt GmbH (Hanns-Martin Fraas, chief organizer).

Reputation: Germany's premier motorsport-circuit cycling festival. The 24h race is the crown jewel — a unique endurance challenge on sacred motorsport ground. Growing rapidly with a passionate community and institutional support from Nurburgring 1927 GmbH.

2003: First edition with approximately 70 riders in the 24h race
2016: Inaugural Rudi Altig Race (pro road race, UCI 1.1). Paul Voss wins with 70 km solo breakaway. Emanuel Buchmann and Davide Rebellin in the field
1966: Rudi Altig wins Road World Championship at the Nurburgring — the event's spiritual origin
2025: Record 11,948 participants across all disciplines

The Course

A relentless, undulating closed circuit combining the legendary Nordschleife (20.8 km) with the modern Grand Prix circuit (5.1 km). No flat sections exist — you are always either climbing or descending through 84 turns per lap. The forest-enclosed Nordschleife feels otherworldly, while the GP circuit provides the F1 arena start/finish spectacle. Perfect tarmac throughout, but the elevation profile punishes anyone who starts too fast.

Signature challenge: The Hohe Acht climb at 17% gradient — the roof of the Nordschleife where every lap extracts its toll. Combined with the relentless undulation of the full circuit (560m elevation per lap), there is no recovery. The Green Hell simply grinds you down.

Race Logistics

Airport
Cologne-Bonn (CGN) 70 km / 1 hour. Frankfurt (FRA) 160 km / 1.5 hours. Dusseldorf (DUS) 130 km / 1.5 hours. Rental car required — Nurburg has no rail connection.
Lodging
Book early. Nurburg village is tiny with limited capacity. Adenau (5 km) has more options. Mayen, Bad Munstereifel, and Kelberg offer budget alternatives 20-30 km away. 24h participants can use paddock camping plots (book with race entry). Consider Cologne as a base with 1-hour commute if local lodging is sold out.
Food
Limited dining in Nurburg village. Adenau has restaurants and supermarkets. The Nurburgring complex has catering during the event weekend. Stock race nutrition in advance — this is rural Eifel, not a city. 24h teams should plan self-sufficient catering from paddock area.
Packet Pickup
Participant Center in Pit Lane buildings (Pit 18-21). Friday 16:00-22:00 and Saturday morning from 07:00. Late registration available.
Parking
Multiple large parking areas across the Nurburgring complex. Entry tickets provided to participants (1-6 depending on team size). Paddock access for 24h participants from Friday.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Rad am Ring?

+

Late July in the Eifel is mild by any standard — average highs of 22C, lows of 12C. Not hot enough to create heat stress, not cold enough to be uncomfortable during daytime riding. The risk factor is rain: the Eifel receives precipitation on 17 days in July, and the Nurburgring's forested microclimate amplifies local weather variability. A sudden rain shower on the Nordschleife turns the high-speed descent sections genuinely dangerous. But compared to Alpine passes, desert gravel, or tropical humidity — this is benign climate territory.

How adventurous is Rad am Ring?

+

The adventure here is entirely about context. You are racing a bicycle through the Green Hell — the most famous, most dangerous, most storied motorsport circuit ever built. The Nordschleife that Jackie Stewart named. The track where Niki Lauda nearly died. You ride through Caracciola-Karussell, past Flugplatz, down through Fuchsrohre, up and over Hohe Acht. The forest closes in around you. The elevation changes are relentless. At night during the 24h race, with headlamps cutting through fog on the Nordschleife, this is adventure riding that no other road event can replicate.

Should I race Rad am Ring?

+

If you have any connection to motorsport culture, this is a mandatory pilgrimage on two wheels. The 24h race is the crown jewel — 24 hours of closed-circuit racing through the Nordschleife, including surreal night laps through forest fog. The public races (25/75/150 km) offer the venue experience in a single day. For German and European cyclists, this is one of the continent's most unique events. For international visitors, combine with a broader European cycling trip.
