The Ratings

Course Profile
Logistics 3/5

The point-to-point format from Terre Haute to Richmond (160 miles apart) creates genuine logistical complexity. You need to arrange transportation back to Terre Haute, or have someone drop you off and pick you up in Richmond. Indianapolis International (IND) is the closest major airport, 75 miles from the start. Terre Haute has limited accommodation. Personal SAG vehicles are allowed and common. The 2026 event starts at Fairbanks Park in Terre Haute. Packet pickup is at the start the morning of the ride.

Editorial
Prestige 3/5

RAIN is in its 37th year (2026), making it one of the longest-running cycling events in America. Named a Midwest Best-in-Class event by Strambecco. The Bloomington Bicycle Club has run this event since 1989 (now transitioned to Central Indiana Cycling). Within the Midwest cycling community, RAIN is iconic -- a rite of passage. Nationally, it is known among ultra-distance enthusiasts. Internationally, it is obscure.

Facts & History

RAIN was born in 1989 as a one-day challenge organized by the Bloomington Bicycle Club: ride across the entire state of Indiana in a single day, from West Terre Haute to Richmond, following the Historic National Road (US 40). The concept was simple and has barely changed in 37 years. The ride grew through word-of-mouth in the Midwest cycling community, attracting riders who wanted to test their endurance on a grand scale. In 2026, Central Indiana Cycling took over operations and added a 210-mile ultra option. The event remains entirely volunteer-operated, with proceeds supporting communities along the route. Founded in 1989. By Bloomington Bicycle Club.

Reputation: The Midwest's ultimate endurance ride. 37 years of crossing Indiana from dawn to dusk.

1989: Inaugural RAIN ride from West Terre Haute to Richmond on the Historic National Road
2000s: Event grows to 2,000+ riders, establishing itself as a Midwest cycling institution
2024: Named Midwest Best-in-Class event by Strambecco

2026: Central Indiana Cycling takes over operations. Route updated with more county roads. 210-mile ultra option added. Start moves to Fairbanks Park.

The Course

A straight line across Indiana. The course follows the Historic National Road (US 40), America's first federally funded highway, from Terre Haute in the west to Richmond in the east. The terrain is flat by any standard. The challenge is pure distance and the Indiana summer conditions.

Signature challenge: The distance itself. 160 miles on flat roads with no descents to rest on, no dramatic terrain to distract you, and Indiana summer heat accumulating over 10-14 hours. The challenge is pure endurance.

Race Logistics

Airport
Indianapolis International (IND), 75 miles from the start in Terre Haute. Dayton International (DAY), 45 miles from the finish in Richmond.
Lodging
Terre Haute hotels for the night before (Holiday Inn, Drury Inn, etc. at $60-$120/night). Richmond hotels for the night after if not driving home. Many riders arrange personal SAG vehicles that drive to Richmond and meet them at the finish. Group carpools from Indianapolis are common.
Food
Midwest diner fare in Terre Haute and Richmond. Nothing fancy, nothing expensive. Rest stop food during the ride is the highlight: volunteer-prepared, well-stocked, and genuine. Lunch is provided at one of the five rest stops.
Packet Pickup
Morning of the event at the start in Fairbanks Park, Terre Haute. Pre-ride briefing at 6:55 AM, ride starts at 7:00 AM.
Parking
Fairbanks Park in Terre Haute has start-area parking. Many riders arrange drop-off and pick-up to avoid the point-to-point parking challenge.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at RAIN - Ride Across Indiana?

+

Late June/July in Indiana is hot, humid, and unshaded. Temperatures of 85-95F with 60-80% humidity are normal. The course crosses open farmland with zero shade for miles at a stretch. Afternoon thunderstorms can deliver torrential rain and lightning. The heat is the ride's true adversary: over 10-14 hours, even moderate temperatures accumulate into a debilitating heat load. RAIN has earned its reputation partly because riders discover that 160 flat miles in Indiana summer heat is harder than they imagined.

How are the logistics for RAIN - Ride Across Indiana?

+

The point-to-point format from Terre Haute to Richmond (160 miles apart) creates genuine logistical complexity. You need to arrange transportation back to Terre Haute, or have someone drop you off and pick you up in Richmond. Indianapolis International (IND) is the closest major airport, 75 miles from the start. Terre Haute has limited accommodation. Personal SAG vehicles are allowed and common. The 2026 event starts at Fairbanks Park in Terre Haute. Packet pickup is at the start the morning of the ride.

How adventurous is RAIN - Ride Across Indiana?

+

Riding across an entire state in a single day is inherently adventurous, even if the terrain is flat. You start in Terre Haute as the sun comes up and finish in Richmond as it goes down. The Historic National Road carries 200 years of American transportation history. Small Indiana towns welcome riders through their main streets. Cornfields stretch to the horizon. It is not dramatic scenery, but the sheer scale of crossing a state under your own power creates a genuine sense of journey.

How prestigious is RAIN - Ride Across Indiana?

+

RAIN is in its 37th year (2026), making it one of the longest-running cycling events in America. Named a Midwest Best-in-Class event by Strambecco. The Bloomington Bicycle Club has run this event since 1989 (now transitioned to Central Indiana Cycling). Within the Midwest cycling community, RAIN is iconic -- a rite of passage. Nationally, it is known among ultra-distance enthusiasts. Internationally, it is obscure.

Should I race RAIN - Ride Across Indiana?

+

Yes if you want a pure ultra-distance endurance challenge on flat roads, appreciate volunteer-driven cycling culture, and can handle Indiana summer heat. Skip if you need mountains, technical terrain, competition, or Instagram-worthy scenery.
