Facts & History

The Morgul-Bismarck circuit near Superior, Colorado has been one of America's most important road racing courses since the 1970s. The Red Zinger Bicycle Classic (1975-1979), founded by Celestial Seasonings co-founder Mo Siegel, and its successor the Coors International Bicycle Classic (1980-1988) used this loop as a key stage. Greg LeMond won the Coors Classic in 1981 and 1985. Bernard Hinault, Connie Carpenter, Davis Phinney, and Ron Kiefel all raced here. The 1987 US National Road Race Championships were held on the Morgul-Bismarck. The course's notoriety reached Hollywood when the 1985 film American Flyers, starring Kevin Costner, used the circuit for its climactic 'Hell of the West' race. After the Coors Classic ended in 1988, racing on the Morgul-Bismarck continued intermittently through local events. In 2010, the Town of Superior formally resurrected the race as the Superior Morgul Classic, a multi-day event including street sprints, a criterium, and the circuit road race. Suburban development has altered the original 13-mile loop — the current race uses a modified 7-mile circuit on Highway 128. The event is now organized by Without Limits and continues as a pillar of the Rocky Mountain Road Cup calendar. Founded in 2010. By Town of Superior, Colorado (resurrected the Morgul-Bismarck race; original course used since the 1970s).

Reputation: The most historically significant road racing circuit in the United States. The Wall is to American cycling what the Koppenberg is to Belgian cycling — a short, steep climb that has decided championships and careers. The current event honors that legacy on a modified course.

The Course

A 7-mile paved circuit on Highway 128, fully closed to traffic. Wide, smooth asphalt through rolling Colorado Front Range terrain. The course is dominated by one feature: The Wall (Super Flag Hill), a steep climb of approximately 750 vertical feet that riders face on every lap. The Wall is the selection point — it is where the race is won and lost on every lap. Between climbs, the course rolls through open grassland with views of the Flatirons. Wind on the exposed loop can fracture a field. The corner at McCaslin Boulevard is the tactical setup point — positioning into The Wall determines who attacks and who suffers. For the Pro/1/2 category doing 8 laps, the cumulative fatigue of repeated Wall efforts is the race's defining challenge.

Signature challenge: The Wall (Super Flag Hill) — repeated every lap. For Pro/1/2 riders doing 8 laps, that is 8 times up the most historically significant climb in American road racing. The cumulative fatigue of repeated Wall efforts, combined with wind on the exposed loop and tactical positioning at McCaslin Boulevard, makes this a race that rewards power, repeatability, and racing intelligence.

MILE
Start/Finish on Highway 128
Rolling out on wide, smooth Highway 128. The field is together but everyone knows what is coming. Positioning begins immediately as riders jockey for The Wall approach. The first lap is tactical — probing who is strong, who is bluffing.
3
MILE
McCaslin Boulevard Corner
The right-hander at McCaslin is where The Wall approach begins. Position here determines everything. The inside line saves meters but risks being boxed in. The outside line is longer but keeps options open. Elbows out. This corner has decided races before the climbing even starts.
4
MILE
The Wall (Super Flag Hill)
The Wall. Approximately 750 vertical feet of steep, exposed climbing on pavement. This is the climb that made the Morgul-Bismarck famous — the same gradient LeMond and Hinault rode, the same road Kevin Costner attacked in American Flyers. On lap one, it hurts. On lap eight, it breaks you. The crowd lines the upper sections, ringing cowbells and screaming. The finish line is at the top.
5
MILE
The Descent and Return
Over The Wall and descending back toward Highway 128 for the next lap. Brief recovery before the rolling terrain and wind reassert themselves. For multi-lap races, this is where you assess damage — did you gain time? Lose a wheel? The rolling return is the only respite before The Wall comes again.

Race Logistics

Airport
Denver International Airport (DEN) — 45 minutes. Superior is along US-36 between Denver and Boulder.
Lodging
Superior, Louisville, and Broomfield have abundant hotels along the US-36 corridor. Boulder (15 minutes) offers more character and dining. Denver (25 minutes) has the full range. This is suburban metro Denver — lodging is plentiful and affordable.
Food
Louisville's Main Street has excellent restaurants. Boulder is 15 minutes away with top-tier dining. Superior itself has suburban dining options. Post-race food at the Superville festival.
Packet Pickup
Race-day pickup opens at 7:00 AM. Must pick up numbers 40 minutes before start time.
Parking
On-site parking in Superior. Arrive early as suburban infrastructure manages but fills.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Superior Morgul Road Race?

+

May on the Colorado Front Range is typically benign for cycling. Sunny, 55-75F, low humidity. The main risks are cool morning starts and the possibility of afternoon thunderstorms, though the race usually finishes before weather develops. Wind on the exposed loop can be a tactical factor. This is not a climate challenge — it is pleasant spring racing weather in most years.

How are the logistics for Superior Morgul Road Race?

+

Superior sits between Boulder and Denver along US-36, 30 minutes from Boulder and 25 minutes from downtown Denver. Denver International Airport (DEN) is 45 minutes. Hotels are abundant in Louisville, Broomfield, and Superior itself. The course on Highway 128 is fully closed to traffic. Without Limits has organized this race for years and runs a tight ship. Registration via BikeReg is straightforward. Parking is manageable in the suburban setting.

How adventurous is Superior Morgul Road Race?

+

The course is on Highway 128 in suburban Boulder County. Views of the Flatirons and the Front Range are visible on clear days, and the open grassland landscape has a windswept Colorado character. But this is not wilderness adventure — it is a road race in a developing suburban corridor. The original 13-mile Morgul-Bismarck loop ran through more isolated ranch land; the current 7-mile course is more constrained.

How prestigious is Superior Morgul Road Race?

+

The Morgul-Bismarck is one of the most historically significant road racing circuits in the United States. The Red Zinger Bicycle Classic (1975-1979) and Coors Classic (1980-1988) — America's most important stage races of their era — used this course. The 1985 film American Flyers (Kevin Costner) was filmed here. The current event is a regional road race, not the international spectacle it once was — hence 4 and not 5.

Should I race Superior Morgul Road Race?

+

Yes if American cycling history moves you and you want to race the same circuit as LeMond and Hinault. Yes if you are a Colorado road racer looking for a well-organized circuit race on closed roads with state championship stakes. Skip if you want a long-distance endurance event or a nationally competitive field.
