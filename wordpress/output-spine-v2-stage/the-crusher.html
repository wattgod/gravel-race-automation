The Ratings

Course Profile
Logistics 1/5

Copper Harbor in the remote Upper Peninsula requires satellite communication devices—Garmin Inreach recommended for the point-to-point format. Greg Gentle notes "GPS is required" with zero course signage, while riders carry mandatory gear including "a snorkel" checked at registration. Chris Spurrier's team started 30 minutes late due to logistics coordination, highlighting the self-supported complexity of reaching this wilderness location.

Editorial
Prestige 1/5

Started in 2014 as a grassroots Upper Peninsula adventure, The Crusher remains exactly that—no prize money, no pro field, no UCI points. It's a wilderness bikepack challenge that Patrick Ogden calls 'very fun' but the gravel world barely notices. The 906 Adventure Team runs it like a camping trip with timing chips, not a race that builds careers.

First 16 miles were dark, fast and exciting. Riders warn this is where mechanical issues often surface, with one rider experiencing heart rate monitor failure, boa shoe break, and tire puncture that wouldn't seal.
Spicy EX Sections
This year's mass start course included EX-style sections with unbridged rivers, mud pits, sand, overgrown rocky tracks, and steep single track - sections that are supposed to be reserved for the EX version.
The mass start course is supposed to be relatively easy gravel roads, but this year's course got pretty EX-style with spicy sections that initially frustrate riders before they start to enjoy the variety and hardship.
EX version features unbridged rivers, mud pits, sand, overgrown rocky tracks, and even some steep single track - it's called extra enhanced for more suffering.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at The Crusher?

+

July through September in northern Michigan means everything from summer heat to early fall cold, often in the same ride. The three-month window makes weather prep impossible—you might get perfect 70s or 40-degree rain. Dense forest cover helps with sun exposure but traps humidity and makes weather changes hit harder. More weather exposure than Belgian Waffle Ride San Diego.

How are the logistics for The Crusher?

+

Copper Harbor in the remote Upper Peninsula requires satellite communication devices—Garmin Inreach recommended for the point-to-point format. Greg Gentle notes "GPS is required" with zero course signage, while riders carry mandatory gear including "a snorkel" checked at registration. Chris Spurrier's team started 30 minutes late due to logistics coordination, highlighting the self-supported complexity of reaching this wilderness location.

How adventurous is The Crusher?

+

Bears, river crossings, wilderness sections with zero cell service, and navigation by GPS through logging roads and jeep trails—it's adventure riding. The remote UP terrain delivers the 'feel alone yet present' experience riders rave about. Just don't confuse adventure with racing; they're different sports entirely. As Greg Gentle puts it: "I've never ridden that far before. Horrible idea or not, I'm committed. The Crusher Gravel Race will send about 150 brave souls across the wilderness of the Keweenaw Peninsula in route to Marquette."

How prestigious is The Crusher?

+

Started in 2014 as a grassroots Upper Peninsula adventure, The Crusher remains exactly that—no prize money, no pro field, no UCI points. It's a wilderness bikepack challenge that Patrick Ogden calls 'very fun' but the gravel world barely notices. The 906 Adventure Team runs it like a camping trip with timing chips, not a race that builds careers.

Should I race The Crusher?

+

Only if you're in the Upper Peninsula or planning UP tourism. Not worth the long drive for the race alone.
