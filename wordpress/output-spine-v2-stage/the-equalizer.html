The Course

Rolling Missouri River Valley terrain. Roughly 3,800 ft over 62 miles = constant ups and downs, no sustained climbs. Mix of smooth gravel county roads (85%) and paved connectors (15%). Terrain is rolling but not extreme. Cold November mornings and late-season fitness are the defining challenge.

Signature challenge: The constant rollers, not one single climb. 3,800 feet arrives in dozens of short punches with no flat mile to recover on, and by November most of the field is riding on fading late-season fitness.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at THE EQUALIZER - Missouri State Championship?

+

In its July-era editions (through ~2024), Missouri heat delivered 90-98°F and a 20% DNF year; the race now runs in early November, where cold replaces heat as the weather story but still producing 15% heat casualties. The oppressive humidity pushes heat index over 100°F, turning the second half into a survival march through wine country that looks better than it feels. More weather exposure than Belgian Waffle Ride San Diego.

How are the logistics for THE EQUALIZER - Missouri State Championship?

+

Marthasville Community Center provides solid race HQ with parking and packet pickup both day-before and race morning. Free onsite camping at Wessel Park with showers across the street, plus STL airport just an hour drive. The course is well marked with orange arrows and roadside directional stakes, backed by corner marshals and roaming sag support.

How adventurous is THE EQUALIZER - Missouri State Championship?

+

The course takes you through roads that look untouched by time into Warren County highland farm country. Three creek crossings provide cooling relief during summer heat, while rolling through Missouri wine country offers scenic beauty. Pretty countryside that won't change your life but delivers solid Midwest gravel character.

How prestigious is THE EQUALIZER - Missouri State Championship?

+

Official Missouri State Championship status draws competitive gravel racers and provides USAC sanctioning credibility. Top 5 overall finishers get their names printed on next year's t-shirt, while state champions receive Roy Doskal Realty jerseys. Part of Show Me Mo Gravel Series but still regional scope.

Should I race THE EQUALIZER - Missouri State Championship?

+

If you can handle 95°F with 80% humidity while grinding constant rollers for six hours, this is solid Midwest racing. The course won't break you—smooth gravel, manageable elevation—but November cold will punish anyone who shows up dressed for September. Perfect for riders wanting state championship bragging rights without the technical demands of mountain races.
