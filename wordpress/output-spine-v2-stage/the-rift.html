Facts & History

Created by Reykjavík Cycling Club to showcase Iceland's volcanic terrain. The course crosses the Mid-Atlantic Ridge—geological boundary between continents. Founded in 2016. By Reykjavík Cycling Club.

Reputation: The bucket-list adventure race. Not just gravel—geological pilgrimage.

2018: Joined UCI Gravel World Series
Expanded to 1,000+ riders
Course literally rides the continental divide

RACE-SPECIFIC: THE RIFT
HEAT PREP July in Hvolsvöllur, Southern Iceland — 10-15°C (50-59°F) with extreme variability. Cold + wet + wind. Hypothermia risk is real. Begin heat acclimatization 10–14 days before The Rift — 20–30min sauna sessions or midday rides in full kit. Pre-load sodium 48 hours out. Target 500–750ml/hr with electrolytes on race day.
NUTRITION Target 60–80g carbs/hour for The Rift’s 124 miles. Start fueling within the first 30 minutes — early fueling prevents late-race collapse. Bonking at mile 60 is a nutrition failure, not a fitness failure.
TERRAIN Highly technical terrain at The Rift demands practice on similar surfaces. Ride black volcanic sand and sharp lava rock at race-day cadence weekly. Expect: black volcanic sand, sharp lava rock, loose highland roads. Practice cornering, descending, and power delivery on unstable surfaces. Dial in tire pressure before race week — 5 PSI wrong costs you 15+ minutes over 124 miles.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at The Rift?

+

Iceland's interior weather 'can turn south in minutes' with July highs averaging only 14°C, making this a masterclass in unpredictable suffering. Riders report starting in light jerseys then getting hammered by rain and sleet, with temperatures dropping 5-10°C from coastal forecasts as you climb toward the mountains. The constant wind drives what locals call 'light rain' but feels like getting sandblasted, and weather forecasts are essentially useless for planning. Among the most weather-friendly events, easier than Badlands. As Anne Moore puts it: "The climbs got longer and steeper and the weather turned FAST. As we reached the higher elevations the wind picked up to at least 25 – 30 mph."

How are the logistics for The Rift?

+

Getting to Hvolsvöllur means flying into Keflavik then driving 90 minutes south to a town of 600 people. Anne Moore stayed at Midgard, calling it "AMAZING and the owners/staff are the some of the nicest, most accommodating people you could ever meet." Five mandatory river crossings through glacial water demand hypothermia planning, though Jim Cuene notes "they made a big deal of the river crossings, but there weren't, really, an issue."

How adventurous is The Rift?

+

You're literally riding the tectonic rift between continental plates across volcanic lava fields. Connor describes "200km of lava fields, fog, and roads carved from volcanic rock" with "endless hike-a-bike sections." Sarah Swallow shifted focus to "just riding the ride as if it was just another day on the Tour Divide" through terrain that looks like another planet.

How prestigious is The Rift?

+

Bucket-list destination race drawing 420 riders to volcanic highlands. Founded by Lauf, the race has grown from limited early editions to over 600 starters by 2024. Christoph Döttelmayer finished "27th spot from 420 riders" and called it a "personal goal achieved." Growing international recognition among gravel explorers seeking expedition-level challenges.

Should I race The Rift?

+

Yes, if bucket-list adventure matters more than racing. The Rift delivers geological pilgrimage, otherworldly terrain, and a story you'll tell forever. Just be ready for Iceland's weather and the expense of getting there.
