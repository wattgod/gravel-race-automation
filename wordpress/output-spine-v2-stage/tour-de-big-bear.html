The Ratings

Course Profile
Logistics 3/5

Big Bear Lake is 100 miles from Los Angeles, but the drive takes 2-2.5 hours on winding mountain roads. Ontario International (ONT) is the closest airport at 75 miles. San Bernardino (no commercial flights) is closest by distance. The mountain roads to Big Bear are narrow and can be congested on summer weekends. Bear Mountain Resort serves as the start/finish with free lower-lot parking and paid upper-lot parking. No public transit to Big Bear. Accommodation should be booked early.

Editorial
Prestige 3/5

Named the #1 Gran Fondo in North America by Gran Fondo Guide in 2023 and consistently in the Top 10. Big Bear has hosted AMGEN Tour of California stages (2010, 2012, 2015, 2017) and the Redlands Bicycle Classic. The Tour de Big Bear has been running since 2010 and is well-established in the SoCal cycling community. But it remains primarily a regional event with limited international draw.

Facts & History

The Tour de Big Bear launched in 2010, the same year Big Bear Lake hosted the AMGEN Tour of California. The Big Bear Cycling Association saw an opportunity to establish a permanent cycling event that would leverage the mountain town's natural advantages: altitude, stunning scenery, ideal summer weather, and roads tested by professional cycling. The event grew rapidly from a modest first edition to 2,500+ riders across multiple distances. The legendary aid stations became the event's signature, with local volunteers transforming rest stops into mini-festivals. Named the #1 Gran Fondo in North America by Gran Fondo Guide in 2023. Founded in 2010. By Big Bear Cycling Association / Chris Barnes (Director).

Reputation: Southern California's favorite ride. North America's #1 Gran Fondo (2023). Known for legendary aid stations and altitude challenge.

2010: Inaugural Tour de Big Bear launches alongside AMGEN Tour of California at Big Bear
2023: Named #1 Gran Fondo in North America by Gran Fondo Guide
2024: Moves to Bear Mountain Resort as permanent start/finish venue
2025: Adds Dirty Burro 50K XC mountain bike race and Dirty Bear/Dirty Cub gravel events
2025: Jonas Woodruff wins 100-mile in 4:31:54, Thomas Sullivan 1 second back

The Course

A deceptive course that lulls you with rolling lakeside roads for 50 miles before delivering the altitude hammer in the second half. The Onyx Summit climb at 8,443 feet is the crown jewel, and the Cedar Falls descent-and-return is where tired legs at altitude learn about humility.

Signature challenge: Onyx Summit at 8,443 feet. The highest paved road in Southern California, reached after 50+ miles of riding at altitude. The KOM/QOM competition makes the climb honest. Then you descend 2,000 feet to Cedar Falls and must climb back up.

MILE
Bear Mountain Start
Rolling out from Bear Mountain Resort at 7,000 feet in the cool mountain dawn. Big Bear Lake visible below. Pine-scented air. This feels easy. It will not last.
25
MILE
Big Bear Lake Circuit
Circling Big Bear Lake on Highway 18 and local roads. Rolling terrain with views of the alpine lake. Aid stations with entertainment. The course is building, not attacking.
50
MILE
Onyx Summit Approach
The ride changes character. Highway 38 east begins the sustained climb to Onyx Summit. The grade is 3-5% for miles. The altitude starts to bite. The air thins. The timed KOM/QOM segment begins. The fastest man in 2025 climbed it in 25:17.
60
MILE
Blue Sky Meadows Cutoff
The 12:30 PM cutoff checkpoint. Trained medical personnel evaluate riders for altitude distress and dehydration. If you are not here by 12:30, you are rerouted to the 70-mile course. This is not arbitrary -- it is for your safety on the exposed descent to Cedar Falls.
70
MILE
Cedar Falls Descent
After cresting Onyx Summit, a long, fast descent on Highway 38 into the Jenks Lake area. Temperatures jump 20-30F as you drop 2,000+ feet. The descent is exhilarating but the knowledge that you must climb back up is sobering.
85
MILE
The Return Climb
Climbing back from Cedar Falls to Onyx Summit and then returning to Big Bear Lake altitude. This is mile 85 at altitude after 7,000+ feet of climbing. The legs that felt good at mile 25 around the lake are now communicating through the language of pain.
100
MILE
Bear Mountain Finish
Rolling back into Bear Mountain Resort through Big Bear City. The finish line, the medal, and the knowledge that you just conquered the highest paved road in Southern California.
RIDERS REPORT
Onyx Pass MI 43
The first big climb that riders specifically mention as challenging. Features a KOM/QOM competition timing segment that starts at the bottom of the ascent and ends at the summit.
Onyx Summit
Described as brutal climbing. Riders mention conserving energy for the long ride back up after descending for 30 minutes.
Cedar Falls
The turnaround point where riders report feeling pretty dead. Still has 4,000 ft of climbing left with about 30 miles to go.
Riders describe having to zigzag through neighborhoods at the start, making it hard to move up in position.
The course includes sections around Big Bear Lake with beautiful scenery on the north and northeast sides of the lake.

HEAT PREP August in Big Bear Lake, California, USA can bring heat. Complete 3–4 heat exposure sessions in the final 2 weeks before Tour de Big Bear. Increase sodium intake 48 hours before race day.
NUTRITION Target 60–80g carbs/hour for Tour de Big Bear’s 100 miles. Start fueling within the first 30 minutes — early fueling prevents late-race collapse. Bonking at mile 60 is a nutrition failure, not a fitness failure.
ALTITUDE Tour de Big Bear with 8,500ft of climbing, much of it above 8,000ft demands altitude preparation. Arrive 5–7 days early for acclimatization. Expect 5–15% power reduction at altitude. Increase iron intake 4 weeks out. Race Logistics

Airport
Ontario International (ONT) 75 miles, 2 hours. Los Angeles International (LAX) 110 miles, 2.5 hours. San Diego (SAN) 130 miles, 2.5 hours.
Lodging
Book Big Bear Lake accommodation early. Visit Big Bear (visitbigbear.com) lists cabins, condos, hotels, and vacation homes. Stay on the lake or near Bear Mountain Resort. Cabins are the local specialty and range from rustic to luxury. Prices are more reasonable than coastal resort towns: $100-$300/night.
Food
Big Bear Village has casual mountain dining: burgers, pizza, BBQ, and brewpubs. Peppercorn Grille is the local favorite for upscale. Nottingham's Tavern for post-ride beer. But honestly, the aid stations on course will feed you so well that dinner plans may change.
Packet Pickup
Upper Deck at Bear Mountain Resort, Thursday and Friday before the event. Late registration available. Strongly encouraged to check in before Saturday to avoid lines and missing start times.
Parking
Bear Mountain Resort: Upper Lot (limited, paid, advance purchase required) and Lower Lot (free). Arrive early on Saturday as parking fills up.
RIDERS REPORT
Get there early and get as close to the front as possible if you're an experienced cyclist. The start is chaotic and it's really hard to move up once you're in the neighborhoods.
Drive to the start line rather than riding if you think you'll be dead at the end of 100 miles. Make sure to get there early to find parking because it fills up fast.
If starting towards the back, give yourself space so you don't cross up wheels - riders saw almost two crashes within the first five minutes.
Conserve energy for the climbs. After descending for 30 minutes, there's still brutal climbing back up.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Tour de Big Bear?

+

This is the secret weapon. While the rest of Southern California swelters in August heat, Big Bear Lake sits at 7,000 feet with morning temperatures in the 55-65F range and highs of 75-85F. The pine forest provides shade. The dry mountain air is invigorating. The only climate risk is the descent to lower elevations on the 100-mile course, where temperatures can jump 20-30F, and the intense UV radiation at altitude that demands aggressive sunscreen and hydration.

How are the logistics for Tour de Big Bear?

+

Big Bear Lake is 100 miles from Los Angeles, but the drive takes 2-2.5 hours on winding mountain roads. Ontario International (ONT) is the closest airport at 75 miles. San Bernardino (no commercial flights) is closest by distance. The mountain roads to Big Bear are narrow and can be congested on summer weekends. Bear Mountain Resort serves as the start/finish with free lower-lot parking and paid upper-lot parking. No public transit to Big Bear. Accommodation should be booked early.

How adventurous is Tour de Big Bear?

+

Big Bear Lake is a beautiful mountain setting with pine forests, alpine lake views, and towering San Bernardino peaks. The Onyx Summit approach through the national forest is scenic. But this is Southern California suburbia transported to the mountains: cabins, resorts, ski lifts, and tourist infrastructure. It is not wilderness. The adventure is in the altitude and the climbing, not in the remoteness.

How prestigious is Tour de Big Bear?

+

Named the #1 Gran Fondo in North America by Gran Fondo Guide in 2023 and consistently in the Top 10. Big Bear has hosted AMGEN Tour of California stages (2010, 2012, 2015, 2017) and the Redlands Bicycle Classic. The Tour de Big Bear has been running since 2010 and is well-established in the SoCal cycling community. But it remains primarily a regional event with limited international draw.

Should I race Tour de Big Bear?

+

Yes if you want the definitive SoCal mountain cycling experience with altitude, climbing, and aid stations that redefine hospitality. Skip if you cannot handle 7,000+ foot altitude, need a sanctioned race, or live far from California.
