Course Profile
Logistics 4/5

Twenty-eight years of production and 2,500 volunteers create a charity ride that runs like a military operation — fitting, given the event honors military and first responders. SAG stops every 20 miles staffed with bike mechanics, first aid, and live bands serving hot pasta and wings. Twenty SAG vehicles and RACES HAM radio operators patrol the course. Prestige 2/5

Tour de Palm Springs is well-known in Southern California cycling circles and draws riders from 46 states, but it does not carry national cycling prestige. It is a regional charity ride — beloved and well-attended but not in the same conversation as Pan-Mass Challenge, AIDS/LifeCycle, or even El Tour de Tucson for name recognition. Tim Esser built something genuinely impressive from 400 riders in 1998, but the event's identity is charity-first, cycling-second.

Facts & History

On January 31, 1998, Tim Esser — president of Esser Air Conditioning and Heating in Palm Springs — had an idea for a charity bike ride in the desert. He envisioned routes of 5, 15, 25, and 50 miles to raise money for local nonprofits. The first event, originally called 'Spokes for Different Folks,' drew 400 cyclists. Tim and his mother Fran formed a nonprofit called CVSPIN (Coachella Valley Serving People In Need), and the first official Tour de Palm Springs was held in 1999 with 1,000 riders. From that modest beginning, the event grew into one of the largest charity cycling events in America — now attracting up to 10,000 riders from 46 states and 4 countries, supported by 2,500 volunteers, and raising nearly $5 million for over 150 Coachella Valley nonprofits. Esser Air Conditioning remains the title sponsor 28 years later. Founded in 1999. By Tim Esser (Esser Air Conditioning and Heating).

Reputation: The Coachella Valley's annual cycling celebration. Not a race — a rolling desert festival that raises millions for local nonprofits while putting 10,000 riders on the road with 20 bands, 2,500 volunteers, and February sunshine.

1998: Tim Esser launches 'Spokes for Different Folks' with 400 riders
1999: Rebranded as Tour de Palm Springs under the newly formed CVSPIN nonprofit. 1,000 riders participate.
2000s: Event grows steadily, adding distance options and entertainment along the route
2010s: Event matures into one of America's largest charity cycling events, attracting 6,000-10,000 riders annually
2023: 25th Annual Tour de Palm Springs honors first responders and veterans
2025: 27th Annual event, February 8. Continued growth and community impact.
2026: 28th Annual event, February 7. Honorary guest Frankie Andreu (9x Tour de France, 2x Olympian). Cumulative fundraising approaches $5 million for 150+ nonprofits.

The Course

A 102-mile loop through the Coachella Valley on flat to gently rolling paved roads. The century route starts in downtown Palm Springs, heads north toward Twentynine Palms on gradual ascending grades, follows Dillon Road through open desert into Indio, then returns northwest through La Quinta, Palm Desert, Cathedral City, and back to Palm Springs. Five SAG stops at approximately miles 17, 42, 57, 68, and 90 — each staffed with mechanics, first aid, live bands, and hot food. The terrain is flat desert with gentle grades and occasional rollers. The challenge is distance and wind, not elevation.

Signature challenge: Wind. The Coachella Valley sits in a natural wind corridor between the San Jacinto and San Gorgonio mountain passes. On calm February mornings, the ride is a desert paradise. When the wind arrives — and it can arrive without warning — riders face 40 mph headwinds and sandblasting gusts that turn a flat century into a grueling endurance test. The Dillon Road section is the most exposed and most notorious. You cannot train for this wind. You can only prepare mentally and ride through it.

MILE
Downtown Palm Springs — The Mass Start
10,000 riders funnel out of downtown Palm Springs onto the desert roads. The energy is electric — bands playing, cheerleaders cheering, and the San Jacinto Mountains catching first light above the valley floor. The first miles are flat and social as the field stretches out by distance group.
17
MILE
Northern Desert — First SAG Stop
The route heads north toward the open desert. This is where the wind makes its first introduction — or its first assault. The road grades up gently toward higher desert terrain. First SAG stop with a live band, hot food, and mechanical support. The shorter-distance riders have already peeled off.
42
MILE
Dillon Road — The Desert Crossing
Dillon Road cuts through open desert east toward Indio. This is the most exposed stretch of the century route — flat, straight, and windswept. Riders have reported sandblasting conditions with 40 mph headwinds and 60 mph gusts near the dunes. On a calm day, this is a pleasant desert cruise. On a windy day, this is where the ride earns its difficulty score. Second SAG stop with full support.
68
MILE
La Quinta to Palm Desert — The Suburban Return
The route turns northwest and begins threading through the Coachella Valley cities — La Quinta, Palm Desert, Rancho Mirage. The terrain is flat suburban riding with some gentle rollers. The wind typically shifts as you change direction. Fourth SAG stop provides relief before the final push.
90
MILE
Cathedral City to Palm Springs — The Home Stretch
The final SAG stop at mile 90 marks the last 12 miles back to downtown Palm Springs. Cathedral City and Palm Springs blur together as you ride toward the finish. The San Jacinto Mountains grow larger ahead. The post-ride festival is audible before it is visible — 20+ bands, beer garden, vendor expo, and thousands of finishers celebrating.

Race Logistics

Airport
Palm Springs International Airport (PSP) — 10 minutes from downtown Palm Springs start. Also reachable from Ontario (ONT, 90 min) or LAX (2.5 hours).
Lodging
Single-day event — book one or two nights in Palm Springs or neighboring Coachella Valley cities. February is shoulder season for the resort crowd, so hotel rates are reasonable. Downtown Palm Springs puts you walking distance from the start. Cathedral City, Rancho Mirage, and Palm Desert offer budget-friendly alternatives 10-20 minutes away.
Food
SAG stops every ~20 miles serve hot pasta, wings, energy drinks, water, and snacks. Post-ride festival includes beer garden and vendor expo with food options. Palm Springs and the Coachella Valley have abundant dining from casual to upscale. Pre-ride dinner: downtown Palm Springs has dozens of restaurants on Palm Canyon Drive.
Packet Pickup
Check-in and packet pickup near S Palm Canyon Drive & Baristo Road in downtown Palm Springs. Available day-of (arrive early). On-site registration adds $10 surcharge.
Parking
Downtown Palm Springs. Arrive early for best spots — 10,000 riders fill parking quickly. Carpooling encouraged.
RIDERS REPORT
Start early - riders wake up at 3:00 AM for the event. Take advantage of the closed roads like Dylan Road where you can maintain higher speeds without car traffic.
Utilize the Fantasy Springs aid station at mile 38 which offers substantial food options including pasta, tamales, bananas, oranges, and popcorn.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Tour de Palm Springs?

+

February in Palm Springs is near-ideal cycling weather — 65-75F, dry, sunny. That earns a low climate challenge score because the weather is a benefit, not a threat. The wind, however, is the great equalizer. Riders have been sandblasted crossing the dunes by 40 mph headwinds with 60 mph gusts. On a calm day, this ride is a desert paradise. On a windy day, it is survival cycling. Desert UV is sneaky — you burn before you feel it.

How are the logistics for Tour de Palm Springs?

+

Twenty-eight years of production and 2,500 volunteers create a charity ride that runs like a military operation — fitting, given the event honors military and first responders. Packet pickup at downtown Palm Springs. Palm Springs International Airport (PSP) is 10 minutes from the start. Hotels, restaurants, and Uber are everywhere in the Coachella Valley. The only logistical challenge is wind — and no amount of volunteers can fix 40 mph gusts.

How adventurous is Tour de Palm Springs?

+

The Coachella Valley delivers decent desert scenery — San Jacinto Mountains rising 10,000 feet to the west, Santa Rosa Mountains to the south, wind farms, date palm groves, and open desert vistas. But the route passes through a chain of suburban desert cities — Palm Springs, Cathedral City, Rancho Mirage, Palm Desert, La Quinta, Indio — connected by commercial corridors. This is a charity ride through the greater Palm Springs metropolitan area, not a backcountry desert expedition. The adventure is social and atmospheric, not geographic.

How prestigious is Tour de Palm Springs?

+

Tour de Palm Springs is well-known in Southern California cycling circles and draws riders from 46 states, but it does not carry national cycling prestige. It is a regional charity ride — beloved and well-attended but not in the same conversation as Pan-Mass Challenge, AIDS/LifeCycle, or even El Tour de Tucson for name recognition. Should I race Tour de Palm Springs?

+

You do not race Tour de Palm Springs — you ride it, you eat hot pasta at mile 42, and you dance to live music at every SAG stop. Yes if you want a fully supported century in perfect February weather with a festival atmosphere that no other charity ride matches. Yes if you want to escape winter and ride through the Coachella Valley desert with 10,000 other cyclists. Yes if you want your registration fee to benefit 150+ local nonprofits. Skip if you want competitive timing, challenging terrain, or backcountry adventure — this is a celebration, not a suffer-fest.
