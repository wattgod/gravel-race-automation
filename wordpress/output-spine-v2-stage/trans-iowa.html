The Ratings

Course Profile
Logistics 2/5

Guitar Ted kept logistics deliberately rough—secret routes until race day, cue sheet navigation only, and checkpoints in random parks with volunteers tracking riders. Greg Gleason describes the whole event as 'like a train wreck in many ways. You can see the imminent disaster coming from a long ways off.' The anti-logistics were intentional, designed to test problem-solving without race support. As Guitar Ted [ELITE/Race Director] puts it: "Most every Iowa gravel road has a 'street sign' on the corner of intersections. When this isn't the case, (Rare), we will mark a corner with flags."

Editorial
Prestige 3/5

Trans Iowa earned cult status as gravel's original sufferfest—14 years of breaking souls before most people knew what gravel racing was. V2's zero finishers and the mystique around Guitar Ted's deliberately rough organization built legend status among the hardcore. It never cracked mainstream awareness like Unbound, capping its prestige at underground classic rather than household name.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Trans Iowa?

+

Late April in Iowa delivered the worst of Midwest spring weather—V2, V4, V6, and V11 were all truncated or affected by brutal conditions. Rain turned the B-roads into impassable mud that could 'swallow riders whole,' while cold and wind made the 34-hour exposure dangerous. The weather wasn't just uncomfortable, it was race-ending. More weather exposure than Belgian Waffle Ride San Diego. As Greg Gleason puts it: "Not knowing where and how far the next convenience store was, I started planning when it would be best for Walter and me to knock on a farmhouse door and ask for water."

How are the logistics for Trans Iowa?

+

Guitar Ted kept logistics deliberately rough—secret routes until race day, cue sheet navigation only, and checkpoints in random parks with volunteers tracking riders. Greg Gleason describes the whole event as 'like a train wreck in many ways. You can see the imminent disaster coming from a long ways off.' The anti-logistics were intentional, designed to test problem-solving without race support. As Guitar Ted [ELITE/Race Director] puts it: "Most every Iowa gravel road has a 'street sign' on the corner of intersections. When this isn't the case, (Rare), we will mark a corner with flags."

How adventurous is Trans Iowa?

+

Trans Iowa delivered pure adventure racing—secret routes, paper navigation, complete self-sufficiency, and real consequences. Jeff Kerkove witnessed riders 'sitting quietly in a convenience store staring with eyes glazed over onto a slice of pizza covered in Cheetos.' Kate Ankofski learned how badly she wanted this during mile-long portage sections through B-road mud. This was gravel's last truly wild race.

How prestigious is Trans Iowa?

+

Trans Iowa earned cult status as gravel's original sufferfest—14 years of breaking souls before most people knew what gravel racing was. V2's zero finishers and the mystique around Guitar Ted's deliberately rough organization built legend status among the hardcore. It never cracked mainstream awareness like Unbound, capping its prestige at underground classic rather than household name.

Should I race Trans Iowa?

+

If you want an extreme 300+ mile self-supported ultra-distance challenge—yes, it's a legitimate test. If you want terrain variety or support—look elsewhere.
