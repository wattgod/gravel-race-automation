Facts & History

In 1988, a group of avid cyclists from Team Evergreen decided it would be fun and challenging to ride from Evergreen to the Vail Valley, crossing three mountain passes in a single day. What started as an informal sufferfest among friends grew into Colorado's signature cycling event. Team Evergreen — Colorado's largest cycling club — ran the event for decades before reorganizing into Shift Events on the production side and Evergreen Ride Club on the social side. Thirty-seven years and over $4.6 million in charitable donations later, the Triple Bypass is more than a ride — it's a Colorado institution. Founded in 1988. By A group of avid cyclists from Team Evergreen Cycling.

Reputation: Colorado's cycling rite of passage. The three-pass challenge that every Front Range road cyclist puts on their list.

1988: Inaugural ride — a group of Team Evergreen cyclists ride Evergreen to the Vail Valley
Grew from informal challenge to Colorado's largest single-day cycling event
Over $4.6 million donated to community nonprofits including Special Olympics, American Diabetes Association, and Bicycle Colorado
Team Evergreen split into Shift Events (event production) and Evergreen Ride Club (2020s)
Field grew from a handful of friends to 5,000-rider sellout — annually one of the first events to fill

The Course

A three-act mountain epic. The ride builds from forested foothills through the alpine tundra of the Continental Divide and into the heart of Colorado ski country. Each pass has its own personality — Juniper is the opener, Loveland is the crux, Vail is the closer. The final 28-mile descent from Vail Pass to Avon on the dedicated bike path is one of the great finishing stretches in American cycling. You earn every foot of that descent.

Signature challenge: Loveland Pass — the switchbacks above treeline to 11,990 ft, crossing the Continental Divide. The thin air, the exposed alpine tundra, the endless turns — this is the moment that defines the Triple Bypass and the image that stays with every rider who completes it.

MILE
Pre-Dawn Evergreen
Rolling out of Buchanan Park in the dark, 7,220 ft above sea level. Pine trees, cool mountain air, headlights on. The first pass is already climbing. Your lungs notice the altitude before your legs do.
20
MILE
Juniper Pass — 11,140 ft
The first major summit. A sustained climb through ponderosa forest that opens to alpine views as you crest above 11,000 feet. The reward: a hair-raising descent into Idaho Springs where you join the I-70 bike path. First pass down, two to go.
40
MILE
Georgetown — Aid Station 2
The historic mining town at 8,530 ft is a welcome mid-morning respite. Refuel here — the Loveland Pass climb starts from the base of the Loveland Ski Area ahead, and it does not forgive empty tanks.
55
MILE
Loveland Pass — 11,990 ft
The crux of the Triple Bypass. From Loveland Ski Area, the road tilts upward and stays there — switchback after switchback above treeline, the air thinning with every turn. At 11,990 feet you cross the Continental Divide. The views are staggering. Your lungs are screaming. This is where the Triple earns its reputation. The descent past A-Basin and Keystone is the release valve.
75
MILE
Swan Mountain — The Fourth Pass
Nobody warns you about Swan Mountain. Between Keystone and Frisco, this rolling climb is the unofficial fourth pass — not long, but on legs that have already crossed the Continental Divide, every gradient feels doubled. The locals know. Now you know.
90
MILE
Vail Pass — 10,660 ft
The final pass. A 7-mile gradual climb from Copper Mountain on the dedicated bike path. Less dramatic than Loveland but on empty legs after 90 miles, every percent of gradient registers. Summit at 10,660 ft. Once you crest Vail Pass, the suffering ends and the celebration begins.
91
MILE
The Victory Descent — 28 Miles to Avon
This is the payoff. Twenty-eight miles of paved bike path descending from Vail Pass through the town of Vail and into Avon. Downhill, wind-assisted, legs spinning freely, the Rockies behind you and a finisher medal ahead. One of the great reward descents in American cycling.

Race Logistics

Airport
Denver International (DEN) — 1 hour to Evergreen. Straightforward I-70 West access.
Lodging
Evergreen has very limited lodging — the one hotel sells out fast. Golden (20 min east) has multiple hotel options and is the recommended pre-ride base. For the finish, Avon and Vail have abundant (but expensive) hotels — SpringHill Suites and TownePlace Suites in Avon are official event sponsors. Book early: July is peak season in Summit County and Vail Valley.
Food
Aid stations provide real food including lunch at Loveland Ski Area. Finish line in Avon has beer and food. Evergreen has restaurants for pre-ride dinner. Avon and Vail have extensive post-ride dining.
Packet Pickup
Buchanan Park in Evergreen. Rider bag drop-off 5:00 AM – 7:30 AM on event day.
Parking
Buchanan Park, Evergreen. Arrive very early — 5,000 riders fill the area quickly. Event provides bus shuttle service from Avon back to Evergreen.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the climate like at Triple Bypass — Colorado's Three-Pass Challenge?

+

July Colorado weather is generally favorable — sunny mornings, warm valleys, spectacular visibility. But the afternoon thunderstorm risk above treeline is real and potentially lethal. Lightning at 11,990 ft on Loveland Pass is not theoretical. The temperature swings from dawn to the high passes require layering. You can experience four seasons in a single ride. Manageable with preparation, but the mountains always have the final word.

How are the logistics for Triple Bypass — Colorado's Three-Pass Challenge?

+

Denver International Airport is barely an hour from the Evergreen start — that's exceptional for a mountain cycling event. The I-70 corridor provides straightforward access to the entire route. Accommodation in Evergreen proper is limited (one hotel that sells out immediately), but Golden is 20 minutes away with multiple options, and Denver is 30 miles east. The point-to-point format means arranging transport back from Avon, but the event provides bus shuttles. Organization has been refined over 37 editions.

How adventurous is Triple Bypass — Colorado's Three-Pass Challenge?

+

Crossing the Continental Divide at nearly 12,000 feet is a transformative cycling experience. The route traces the spine of the Rockies through some of Colorado's most iconic terrain — from the foothills above Denver to the ski resorts of Summit County to the Vail Valley. You ride past A-Basin, Keystone, Copper Mountain, and Vail. The scenery above treeline on Loveland Pass is alpine tundra — raw, exposed, and vast. Not remote wilderness like the Atlas Mountain Race, but genuine Rocky Mountain grandeur.

How prestigious is Triple Bypass — Colorado's Three-Pass Challenge?

+

Alongside Ride the Rockies, the Triple Bypass is Colorado cycling. 37 years of continuous history. Sells out every year. In the Front Range cycling community, 'I did the Triple' carries weight. Nationally recognized as one of America's premier single-day climbing challenges. Not quite the international name recognition of l'Etape du Tour, but within US cycling, the Triple Bypass is a bucket-list institution.

Should I race Triple Bypass — Colorado's Three-Pass Challenge?

+

Yes if you want to cross the Continental Divide on a bicycle and experience the full spectrum of Colorado mountain riding in a single day. Yes if sustained high-altitude climbing on paved roads is your definition of a good time. Skip if you need race competition or can't tolerate riding above 10,000 feet.
