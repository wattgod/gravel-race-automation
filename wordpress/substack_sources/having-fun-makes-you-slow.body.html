For the last 15 years, I felt like a failure unless I spent at least 4 hours every weekend day training and went to bed hungry.

No one forced me to do this. I wanted it. I tried to pay the price I thought it took to be good at bikes, even if it mostly felt like constant torment with returns that rarely confirmed I was anything but mediocrity.

This week, we had a three-day weekend, and initially, everything was coming together for a clean three-day mini-camp where I could try to waterboard my CTL into the low 120s.* But then it snowed and got cold.

How much? Generously three to four inches, not much, but enough, enough to ignite a passion few people know about me - I love building sledding hills.

Snow is Fun

Early on Saturday morning, when I should have been on the bike for a few hours already, my wife tried to find me to plan the weekend so both of us got what we needed to feel sane, but she couldn't find me. Where was I? Outside with all my snow gear, shovel in hand, pulling a collapsible Costco wagon, curiously dumping load after load on the back stairs.

Three days, fifteen hours, and ~120 loads of ~3.5 packed cubic feet of snow later, I had shoveled, moved, compacted, shaped, and tortured about 15,000 lbs of snow into a deluxe backyard sledding hill with a total vert of 12, maybe even 13.5 feet, and two runs. Word got out around the neighborhood, and we had to start turning away kids** because so many wanted to go down our sledding hill.

Seemingly every kid in the neighborhood got bundled up and threw themselves at the hill, rattling the air with unbound shrieks, sending our confused dog in circles around, torpedoing face-first runs.

I fretted over my creation, worried some wayward, destructive, prepubescent d**k would wantonly kick or dislodge critical pieces of the hill's carefully designed support structure.

Over the three days, my wife kept nudging me to ride so that I didn't want to commit a suey later on that night and blame it on everyone else in the family.

I would acknowledge her, briefly slip into the house to make coffee or steal a child's half-eaten butter biscuit, pretend to prepare to descend into the basement to ride, and then go back outside to check on my masterpiece. I would repeat this pattern over and over until it was clear my ride wouldn't just be delayed but skipped. Eventually, she gave up on trying to pressure me to ride for my own sake and let me continue being strange.

Fun Makes You Slow

Look, I was happy, happy doing what I was doing now for its own sake, reluctant to descend into the basement in mid-January to torture myself for four to five hours in Zwift so that in late April, I don't suck at pedaling.

Instead, I went back for another load after load of snow, and somehow, even though this wasn't training, shoveling, moving, and compacting snow for an absurd three-run back porch snow slide felt more important.

I couldn't tell you why, but I knew this was what I needed to do right now. That trainer in the basement seemed vile, a negation of life when I could be manfully moving discarded snow banks into my backyard to thrill my children, who are still at the age where they would enjoy it rather than take one look outside and then go back in the house to play Fortnite.

What Did I lose?

Respect from my neighbors, wife, the trust of a few kids I yelled at for running the jump, and easily about fifteen hours of riding indoors, or roughly 2-3 watts on my future threshold. So yeah, it was an expensive weekend.

I'm sharing all this because the problem I try to coach people out of and secretly struggle with myself is something called human conditions. As much as I've tried, I can't seem to eliminate it from people, so they get as fast as they pay me to be.

Because here's the thing, I really did miss out on a lot of potential training. It's no different than an hourly employee missing out on three work days. Sure, it doesn't matter in the big picture, but that doesn't mean having a few hundred more bucks in your pocket wouldn't help.

Then again, when you die, you're not going to remember that weekend you rode in the basement or grabbed some extra work shifts; you're far more likely to remember the spontaneous thing you did that served someone else's happiness***.

Frustratingly, life continues to be beset with gray areas and tradeoffs. My advice to you, metaphorically, is to build the damn sledding hill**** fully accepting that no matter what you do, you're going to regret it.

*If any of my athletes are reading this, don't lose your shit - I don’t actually train that much. I keep my threshold set low, so I accumulate more TSS for every workout, which makes me feel better about myself.

**That's not true at all.

***And scratched your weird itch.

****Unless you want to be fast. If you want to be fast, train.
