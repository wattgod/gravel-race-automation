Once Upon a Time in Norway

Last year, my wife and I went to Norway (without the kids!) because a friend was getting married there, and I was the master of ceremonies. We didn’t know many people at the wedding, so at our first breakfast, we heaped our plates with Norwegian food and then ambushed a poor couple who had the misfortune of leaving two seats open at their breakfast table. It didn’t take long for us to regret our choice.

Shortly after introducing ourselves, we happened to mention we had kids, and the man said unsolicited, “Oh God, I HATE kids!” while I was mid-sentence.

Taken aback, I trailed off whatever I was saying, which allowed him enough daylight to jump in and give a rapid-fire tour through his life accomplishments, highlights, professional credentials, and recent adoption of mushroom farming. But of all the things he said (while his wife looked on with a pained look), he spent the most time talking about the new dog they’d flown (because he had his pilot’s license, naturally) to pick up in Eastern Washington.

Despite obvious and many body language cues that I wasn’t interested in the thirty minute version of their dog’s adoption story, this guy proceeded to water board my wife and I with the 500+ high resolution iphoto album of their new puppy, never seeming satisfied to moving on to the next picture until we made some sort of cooing noise indicating we thought their furr baby was adorable.

Midway through the nonconsensual slideshow, the five-year-old daughter of the soon-to-be-married couple came over and tugged at the dress of the man’s wife. She showed her a picture she had drawn. The wife sometimes babysat the girl. The man shuddered, scooted away from the kid, and gave us a pained look.

“That’s HER thing, NOT mine!” He said. “I HATE kids!” (As if to remind us of the fact, just in case we hadn’t heard him the first time).

The real kicker was the last photo we saw. He scrolled madly through his third or fourth album of his dog, finally found what he was looking for and said, without a shred of irony - I shit you not - ”Look at this one. Look at his eyes! They look so human!”

“Crotch Goblins”

I’ll just lay my cards on the table: since moving from a primarily agricultural society that incentived lots of kids to provide the raw horsepower to run a farm, birth control, the death of God and the replacement of Him at the pinnacle of life with a materialist individualism, a majority of society has not only lost the incentive to reproduce, but doesn’t see the point.

And this makes sense. Children are an incredible burden. They’re ‘experience limiters’, as some once told me, and fatally interfere with the ability to do whatever you want to do whenever you want to do it, which is unforgivable if your highest good (i.e., ‘God’) is yourself. Why? Because anything that gets in the way of YOU, of having more experiences and freedom and hobbies and accomplishements is criminal because you could be more, do more, have more but you can’t because of those stupid ‘crotch goblins’ slowing you down with their demands for butt wiping and mashed peas.

There’s just one problem with that—the self as the highest good is a dead end. The highest sources of meaning and fulfillment can’t be satisfied through the self, only through others.

Sorry! The experiment has been run, and people have tried to make their Northstar their own fulfilment or, worse, their own pleasure (look up the Hedonists). The end result isn’t so much bad as deeply disappointing because no matter how much you pour into that cup, it never fills.

What does? Other people, and this is not true because I think it is, but because the Crotch Goblin people make this argument for me with their actions!

Many child-free zealots still marry, adopt dogs, and pour themselves into causes outside themselves, they highly value; otherwise, they wouldn’t do it, EVEN THOUGH those relationships often come at the expense of them doing whatever they want whenever they want.

You think my kid is an experience limiter, but that dog you have to take out to shit every day, walk, feed, and kennel whenever you go on that trip to Ibiza with the girlies, isn’t a bit of an experience limiter at times, to say nothing of your hectoring spouse and all of their moodiness, ingratitude, and ceaseless demands without the limpest reciprocal reciprocities like active listening or being open to something other than missionary?

K.

The Dog Test

If all of what I just said was bracing or unwelcome to you, I’m not sorry, because the Crotch Goblin types, or people that are openly antagonistic to kids and contemptuous of people that have them, get a free pass in society to use children as their punchline. In contrast, parents do not get to use the people (or animals) in their lives as one.

Consider one of the previously mentioned exchanges:

“Yeah, we’re expecting our third kid in October.”

“Oh God! Why would you do that to yourself?”

Now, swap out a few words like this and see how this lands:

“Yeah, we’re getting married in October.”

“Oh God! Why would you do that to yourself?”

Pretty rude, huh? In fact, saying that wouldn’t just be impolitic, but kinda offensive.

And yet, if you swap out the married part for the kid not only would shitting on the birth of a child be widely accepted, it would be something that I’m supposed to laugh at. Yes - the implication of the punchline that I’m supposed to agree with in my laughter is that having a kid sucks because it interferes with ‘me’ time which is the most important thing in life.

Now, I accept that some people might feel the same way about marriage because, like children, it implies placing someone at least equal to yourself in importance, which is the modern equivalent of becoming a nun, so let’s try one more example to drive my point home.

“Yeah, we’re getting a dog in October.”

“Oh God! Why would you do that to yourself!?”

Maybe that seems like a harmless exchange, but I’m willing to assert that currently in society I would be MUCH more nervous implying that dogs suck and needlessly destroy your life in the company of generation Z and Millenials (especially Millenials) than I am kids.

Dogs! Freaking dogs!

Think about that. We have become so child averse that the Overton window, the topics and ideas that are acceptable to discuss in polite society, doesn’t mind if we shit on kids but God forbid if you rip on someone’s schmoopy whoophy furr baby.

Here’s a simple rule: If you wouldn’t say it about a dog, don’t say it about a kid.

Turn the Other Cheek?

Look, I get so wound up about people using my kids as a punchline for obvious reasons - because they’re my kids. They’re my soft spot. I love them, and yes, it pisses me off when people imply they suck or they’re ruining my life when they’re -by far- the best thing to ever happen to me - behind winning Alt for Norge Season 2, available to stream on YouTube.

You’ll have to forgive me some pettiness, but back when my first child was born, the same childless, tattooed, politically active brunch enthusiast millennial bike racers came out of the woodwork to tell me gleefully that my life was over, I’d never bike race again, and that I was an idiot for doing so. That was 2016.

Now, up until this point, I had actually forgotten that those people said those ugly, jagged things to me, especially some of whom I considered friends (at the time). Well, as the announcement of my third child has gradually trickled out, I’ve been reminded that these classless cretins exist. Instead of just turning the other cheek and laughing as they casually shit on my children, I’m going to allow myself to do a little follow up on the people that prognosticated my ruin eight years ago.

Person 1

What they said: ☝🏻

What I did: I had my best year (up until then), beat them in every race we were both in, and upgraded to Cat 1.

Where are they now: They’ve gained 20 lbs, barely train or race at all, and are a functional alcoholic.

Person 2

What they said: I can’t pull an exact quote because this individual offered an avalanche of unsolicited advice about how being a parent would stop me from bike racing, then when I became a parent he told me how to be a parent (he had one kid), as well as how to be a good husband all with a weapons grade smugness that took my breath away.

What I did: I kept bike racing, had my best placings ever in an NRC race, and branched out into gravel racing.

Where they are now: His wife left him, and he spends all his time lapping people in the 40+ category instead of spending time with his son.

Am I petty? Sure, and maybe it would reflect better on me if I held my tongue, but like I said, why do the assholes get to be assholes while the ‘right’ thing to do is to just take it and hold my tongue?

I bring all this up because:

  1. People who rip on you for having kids aren’t the people you want to be friends with.

  2. As I’ve been at pains to say, kids don’t make you slow. Yes, Parenting Makes You Faster.

  3. People who seem to take outsized glee in your impending kid-caused cycling demise invariably get leveled by karma and become a bloated object lesson in midlife decline.

Aren’t They Just Giving You Shit Tho?

I guess the anti-kid pot shots is just a form of good natured ribbing and I should just laugh it away and file every exchange into my anti-motivation ditty bag and print off their face and stare at it instead of Zwift I mean TrainingPeaks Virtual at 2 am during the only part of the day that I can ride because the stupid baby won’t go to sleep and dream of taking my revenge against them and that they experience some form of hardship hopefully financial and end up moving back in with their parents in their 40s.

So, Yeah.

Next time you hear your buddy or your girlie announcing they’re about to become a parent, do them and you a favor and spare them the anti-kid claptrap and say just this:

“Congrats! I’m so happy for you!”

That’s it. You don’t have to really think about it—you’re free to fantasize about how this means they’ll probably have less time, energy, and resources to put toward consistently modest placings in local pedaling competitions. This will likely mean that you’ll place better than they do, and you can use that as evidence that you are, after all, a better, cooler person. But this isn’t something you should say out loud.

Remember that mewling, good-for-nothing, life-limiting meatbag your friend is having is their furr baby, their little puppy, so try to relate to their understandable decision to expand their search for meaning past the usual mainstays of hungover brunch feeding Shmoopy overpriced strips of maple glazed bacon to sleepless nights and long days filled with everything but bike riding.

I’m just hope you’ll be faster than they are, because it sure would be sad if you weren’t.
