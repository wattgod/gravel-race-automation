A few Saturdays ago, my phone rang. It was my athlete.

Now, if you pay me enough, I'm the sort of coach who will pick up your phone call on a Saturday morning, but even though this athlete definitely did, I didn't pick up because my six-year-old son had stolen another neighbor's electric dirt bike and ripped out of the neighborhood in sandals without a helmet or his airtag, and his mother was HEATED.

I was loading up the tranquilizer gun and gassing the old Bultaco Pursang when Lars pulled into view with a girl on the back, and rice krispie treat wrappers from my private cycling stash fluttering in their pockets. We locked eyes, and before I could lunge for him, he and his broad took off just as his mother opened the garage door.

"Did you find him?" she said.

"Yep. Pulled up with some biddie and contraband."

"You going to run him down?"

"Nah. Let the boy taste freedom a little longer before he feels the switch."

"Holy Shit Matttttiiiii"

With Lars's position roughly determined within a ten-mile radius, I called my athlete back. When he picked up, it didn't even sound like him.

"M-A-T-T-I," He said.

"Yes? Hello, yeah, yeah, it's me." I replied.

"I FREAKING WON!!"

"Hello? My phone says this is Mikey, but I don't know any Mikeys that could win a bike race."

"I freaking did it! I broke away with twenty miles to go and soloed to the line!"

"Alright, again, I'm not sure who this is, but this is a really weird sales call, and we already like our internet provider, so I'm going to hang up on you now."

"Yeah, it was the shorter distance of this race, but still, I won. I can't believe it."

"Talk to you never."

Mikey freaking won. You don't know Mikey because you're not coaching him and would have no reason to, but somewhere, a few weeks ago, a guy crossed the line first for the first time in years a few short months after not just trying to bail on me (who could blame him?) but because he was done with the sport and trading his bike for...I don't know, maybe a bowling ball? Is that what people do in Wisconsin?

So, what the hell happened? How did he avoid becoming a middle-aged marvel-crankled Midwesterner filled with tuna melts and warm Millers into a man who pedaled so fast that he gave a few cyclists arrhythmias from which they'll never recover and always blame on him?

Well, me, lol.

What Did You Just Say To Me?

Last Fall, Mikey got real with me and poured it all out, which is hard for any man, much less a Wisconsin man, but even though it was like trying to get a hook out of a Northern's mouth, I got some feelings out of him. Basically:

  • His dog died, and the world went gray (or grey? Why do the British spell it like that?).

  • He has a daughter and a wife and likes them.

  • Every other non-cycling friend seems happier and cooler.

  • He's accomplished 80-90% of what he set out to accomplish.

  • He's hit his potential.

I internally laughed at three of the five things he said and broed out to the other two. After working with him for two years, I always hoped we'd have this conversation.

The coach-athlete relationship can be great if both parties like each other and build some trust. When the circumstances are right, you can go from mechanically exchanging sterile yet necessary information about schedules and workout execution to finally detouring into the interior world behind everything else. It doesn't happen with everyone, but when it does, I remember why I do this shit beyond the fact that it pays well and allows me to invest in Bitcoin.

Anyway, I spent the call listening and staring uncomfortably into his pixelated eyes until I made him look off into the distance. A few hours later, I used my notes from the call to fire back a series of nasty, penetrating questions that implied he was a loser for quitting.

It worked! A few weeks later, he stayed on, and I charged him even more money for all the fuss.

The Darkness and Dawn Thing

Six months later, everyone trying to hang onto Mikey's wheel got blown off. They called their coaches the day after, saying that they'd had a change of heart, were selling their bikes, and were going back to the bowling alley. That's remarkable.

He almost quit it all but then reexamined everything and found a wellspring of motivation that reinforced his already excellent training process (you should see this guy's TrainingPeaks athlete account. He's an alien, like a case study in a cycling textbook) through the grim Wisconsin winter to the start lines of April, and suddenly, shockingly, to the top of the podium. Wow.

Mikey's story is hardly an outlier in cycling. This sort of story happens so frequently in so many different parts of life that it's been metaphorically enriched by the proverbial expression, 'It's always darkest before the dawn.'

Meaning?

Right before a turning point, everything sucks the most. Not only do you want to quit, but everything points to the fact that you should quit, but you shouldn't because, paradoxically, that is the exact moment you're closest to what you seek, to what you've been working for. The hotter the friction, the closer you are to breaking through.

Why is life like that? Idk - insert your favorite explanatory religion, theory, philosophy, whatever. I'm not here to explore why it is, just what it is.

What Do Warming Ice Cubes, Earthquakes, Heated Water, and Popcorn Kernels Have in Common?

They all sit there, doing nothing, until they don't. They all look static and unchanged, even while seemingly endless energy gets dumped into them until BAM! Transformation. The cubes melt, the earth shakes, the water boils, and the kernel pops.

This is a universal pattern in the metaphysical and physical world - effort and work appear to do nothing to something until an unseen threshold is crossed, and everything changes out of nowhere.

Except it was never anywhere. The change was always building, spreading, and mounting under the surface; just because it's nearly imperceptible doesn't mean it's not happening.

When I first worked with athletes, I wished this was something I could bottle and FedEx for them to consume, like a supplement. As my website says, I've worked with TENS of athletes (lmao), and I would estimate a third of them up and quit before the popcorn popped or the water boiled because they felt, understandably, that all that heat wasn't doing anything.

And just because I like abusing metaphors and sayings, I'd add that it wasn't boiling because they were too busy watching it because, as they say, 'A watched pot never boils.' When you're too fixated on the endpoint of a process, it warps your perception and makes it feel like it's longer and harder than it is, and you end up questioning everything and quitting unless you're Mikey.

So, if you see him riding around America's Dairyland, holla at your boi and maybe even give him some shit for thinking that non-cycling normies have better or cooler lives. I'm sure he'll take it personally.
