August 30th, 2018

We made it to the condo outside Sugar Bush Resort, and my bike actually arrived from Bike Flights. We gathered Hads from the rental van and went through the door. I had a 4-pack in my hand and was ready to crack one after a travel day that began 13 hours prior.

Betsy went through the door first. She turned to look at me, all business. "Oooooookay. Operation Cat Up! How can we make this happen?"

I set my beer down. This was not a vacation. This was a mission. It was a trip to get the points I needed to finally get to the final category of amateur road cycling in America. It was a trip, so I could live with myself when the dad bod pushed out of my jeans, when baby number 2 was pooping all over the floor, and I hadn't slept in 20 hours. Now or never.

Vermont is a beguiling place, which is probably why we didn’t get into a fist fight with the cashier when he revealed that our modest collection of groceries was going to cost us $117. I winced, but inwardly, because we’d been talking for 5 minutes about nothing, principally because a sign over the register said ‘Not the express line! Subject to unnecessary questioning, slow cashier operation, and storytelling.’ The conversation was good enough, and the pain of the bill was lessened because we may or may not have walked out with an extra four-pack of craft Vermont beer.

I liked that this wasn’t Safeway. In fact, nothing in Vermont appears to be very commercial. It’s not like the rest of America, where you deplane and find yourself next to the same collection of Starbucks, Targets, and Grocery chains, so it feels as if you haven’t really traveled at all, merely switched climates for the same scenery.

Vermont is advertised as beautiful in August, and though I’m no stranger to beauty and dislike to agreeing to something I’m told to think, I had to admit it was true. The roads from Burlington to Warren had a speed limit of 50mph, which you more or less had to follow because they were so twisty and steep. Our little family drove along in an upgraded Budget Kia Sedona (for which I was growing afraid Betsy was going to make an offer to the rental company), plunging from one green-filled valley to the next. Bright red, carefully maintained farms popped up here and there from the deep undergrowth, and mountains lurked in the background.

Before touching down in Vermont, I had a vague notion that this was a sort of liberal-leaning state, but I had never read much about it. Signs we passed on our drive confirmed that intuition. ‘Join our local CSA..’ ‘Eat local kale.’ ‘Organic ice cream for sale.’ ‘Mad Tacos gourmet taco bar’. It was sort of like driving through yuppier Wisconsin farmland, where everyone got a little higher more often, with the added bonus of baby mountains in the background. In other words, I loved it.

Me - I felt great out there today! It was so easy!

Arriving at our Condo at Sugarbush (every street in Vermont is culinarily themed as if everything was named by a group of hangry people sitting in a bakery), I was pleasantly surprised to find my bike delivered from Bike Flights at the front door.

Rule number 1 of traveling stage races - something will always go wrong, it will be a crisis, and it will come at the worst time.

Maybe nothing bad would happen this time around?

I was wrong. I didn’t actually take the bike out of the case (which, for future reference, I will always do) for a few hours because we were all dying of hunger and travel fatigue and Jill, the condo AirBnB owner, left nothing more in the whole condo for us but chunky clover honey and old Keurig coffee - hence the $117 Warren Store grocery bill.


When I did finally get around to looking at the bike at 6:48 pm, right after we put a very upset baby to bed, the first thing I saw was a crank plug and a tool, which is not the first thing you want to see when you open up your padded bike case. I dug further and found that the Ziploc bag, which I had placed 6 critical bolts into when disassembling the bike, had mysteriously disappeared. I could not build the bike without those pieces. Tomorrow the race starts, and I’m pretty sure every bike shop is closed. Good chance I wouldn’t be able to race. Rule #1 strikes again.

I did what I always do when Rule #1 strikes - I panicked. A bike shop was closing in 5 minutes 8 miles away and I was in the Kia Sedona hugging curves almost immediately after Google Maps loaded.

I blew by the bike shop on Highway 100 a few times because the instructions were being yelled at me in Norwegian on Google Maps, and my brain wasn’t firing. I finally spotted it, threaded in the Sedona’s gigantic rump in between two sedans, and leaped out of the door into the shop, which said ‘closed.’

Two mechanics were inside sippin’ well-earned beer and talking to their buddy. I breezed past him dismissively and said, ‘I have problems.’ They all looked at me quizzically, and after an awkward moment of silence, I realized to my horror that I had just become the rude, stereotypical loud-mouth vacationer whose problems were everyone else’s emergencies.

Luckily, they must have been partway into a buzz, because the head mechanic obliged and set about looking for the critical bolts that would make or break my race. He found them and charged me $5. His friend had left. It was 7:20ish, long past closing time, and he had completely saved my race start and avoided calling me a douche, which I most certainly was. So I looked around hastily for something else to buy to make it worth his while and snagged a T-shirt.

A scorched Sedona transmission and $25 later, I had saved my race, or at least given myself a chance to start.

On paper, two adults traveling to Vermont with a 17-month-old seems like a great idea. It’s just one 3-hour flight, a short layover, and another 1-hour flight. Straightforward.

What you forget is that flying with a 17-month-old is far more demanding than anything you’ve ever done, except if you’ve been to Afghanistan. Time stands still, because life for a 17 m.o. Is moment to moment, sportingly jumping from tantrum to helpless giggles, both of which without explanation.

Sure, there are dots we connected. We noticed that when Hadley hits her head, it’s followed by long, high-decibel screaming that is only really assuaged through Gerber fruit chews. Another is, in general, if Hads is awoken 3 hours before her normal awake time and misses her afternoon nap as well from a flight delay, she’s going to lose her mind. Go figure.

Maybe the worst part is, especially when you’re on the plane, you can’t escape, and everyone is watching you. I can’t cover Hads’ head with a towel until she stops crying. I can’t yell at her when she launches herself backward, headfirst, onto some poor unsuspecting fellow passenger.

By the second flight, when both adults had been reduced to bare consciousness, we did what neither of us really wanted to do and pulled out the iPad while feeding her a continuous stream of delicious treats. I felt some judging eyes. Yes, we’re letting our 17 m.o. Near a screen and feeding her out of a wrapper. No, she’s not being read to in a foreign language while being fed quinoa-cakes. We get it, we’re the worst parents ever. But we survived, and they know where they can stick it.

August 31st - GMSR Time Trial

Rule 2 of Travel Stage Racing - despite shipping most of your worldly possessions 1000s of miles, you will forget something seemingly insignificant but important.

As I warmed up for the time trial that day, I couldn’t help but realize how terribly naked I was. I don’t mean I was riding naked, I mean that my skin suit, designed to hug my skeleton like a condom, did not come with pockets. That meant I couldn’t bring a flat kit, and even if I could have, I don’t have a CO2 cartridge along with me because I forgot to ship them with my bike (since TSA deems them a bomb threat and relieves you of them if you’re naïve enough to try to smuggle them through security). It seems like a small oversight, but if I hit a nail on the side of the rural country roads by the race start and flatted, I would be entirely screwed, miss my start and DNF, or, if I started at all, be hopelessly behind in time.

I didn’t flat. I started on time, my lungs filled with sea-level air, and I got 9/52 starters, 46 seconds off the lead. It was a performance good enough to let hope creep in on one side and bad enough for anxiety to enter the other—perfect!

When do the goats...uh,...get KILLED!

Me, dialing it up to 11 at the petting farm with Hads.

September 1, 2018 - GMSR - Mad River Road Race

We finally return from the race, and I settle in, drooling over my file on TrainingPeaks and stuffing myself with gourmet cheeses and fine deli meats. Eventually, we put Hads down, and I verbally promise to interact with Betsy rather than stare at this screen. I tell her, ‘5 minutes,’ and she lies down on the couch and promptly passes out.

My ploy has worked! Except there’s a heavy quiet in the house. Breathing sounds like a foghorn in here. My needs grow. I want to eat more! I need more flavored seltzer waters! My bladder is bursting! Can I fulfill my needs without disturbing this tranquil scene?

I try to make a move, and immediately my canoe feet hook a wooden chair underneath the table, and I move it screeching across the floor. Betsy immediately wakes up. Undaunted, I attempt to creep around further, but only manage to step on that one creaky floorboard outside where Hads is sleeping, risking ruin to us all.

I retreat to the kitchen despite the extraordinary pressures on my internal organs and decide I can overcome that urge with food. I try to open the refrigerator quietly, but can’t, because it hasn’t been serviced since Bill Clinton was in office, and groans incrementally as I open the door wider to view the potential cornucopia within. I’m confronted with yogurts, meats, fruit, leftover pizza, cheeses, and even the remains of a pasta dish!

I reject it all and make minute oatmeal, but as I close the refrigerator door, I realize that I’ve made several major noise errors. First, I have to use the microwave, which, if possible, is louder than the refrigerator. Second, I have to open the refrigerator again because I failed to remember two essential ingredients to a successful oatmeal—almond milk and peanut butter!

Sweat beads on my forehead. Another noise-filled step confronts me - I have to open the dishwasher to get a spoon. I have other spoon choices, but they’re all too large. I need the small ones, and they’re all hidden in the dishwasher, which, as you can imagine

As I open the dishwasher, the microwave loudly ends, and the sounds join together gleefully, resonating through the kitchen. Through a few walls, I hear the first sounds of Hads waking up from her nap.

My oatmeal was undercooked, Betsy was up, Hads was up, no one likes me, even me.

This is a two-handed drive.

Betsy, right as I was about to pull out onto Vermont country roads with a full cup of coffee in my hand.

I normally have trouble falling asleep, so I’ve gotten in the habit of putting in earbuds and falling asleep to a podcast. It works like a charm, as long as you don’t mind it draining the battery life of my phone and rendering me completely deaf to intruders. But hey, tradeoffs.

So last night, wrestling in my usual bout of insomnia (probably my fault, probably 200 mg of coffee at 1 pm, but hell, that Vermont coffee is flippin’ delicious!) I threw in the buds and carefully crept into our bedroom, currently inhabited by a slumbering Hads, trying not to run into her bed, my bed, the fan, or a few scattered objects, all of which would lead to a noise that would wake her up and create a crisis lockdown.

I avoided that, put the buds in, and started drifting. Unfortunately, I couldn’t fall asleep on one side, so I switched to the other like a petulant tween and pressed my right ear deeply into the pillow. My auditory senses seemed too dull, but sleep swiftly took me, and I was out before I knew it.

1:45 am came. I awoke for no particular reason beyond a burning desire to visit the bathroom, but then I noticed something odd. The hearing in my right ear was off, muffled, dulled. I realized with a start that somehow the plastic cup on the right audio bud had ripped off from the receiver and was now lodged deeply in my ear. Instinctively, I tried fishing it out with my finger, but my fat index finger could barely press past the opening to my ear without encountering resistance, and I couldn’t even feel the beginnings of the plastic cover.

I started to sweat. 1:45 a.m. isn’t a kind hour. I had a race in 6 hours, and there was no way in hell I could start the race like this. If my finger didn’t work, maybe the receiver of my earbuds would.

I found it in the dark, careful not to wake Hads, and began probing. I could feel the end of it encounter the plastic! Success! I just needed to scoop it out somehow. I began trying to jam the plastic against one wall of my ear canal or the other and pulling upward.

I tried for 5 minutes and got nowhere. Worse, I was having trouble just finding it any longer - I was forcing it down. The right side of my head began to feel pressure. I think the damn thing was getting close to my eardrum!

I sat there in the dark, agape with the receiver in my hand, knowing I wasn’t doing anybody any good, and that Betsy could help me in the morning. I put the earpod down, turned over, and tried to sleep.

Five minutes later I knew sleeping was not in the cards. I could not stop thinking about the plastic in my ear. I could feel its pressure. I couldn’t hear anything, and fear gripped me. I had to do something, even if it meant getting up, maybe running into something, and waking Hads, maybe waking the entire condo.

I wandered out awkwardly and somehow made it to the bathroom. I knew Betsy’s toiletry kit was within, and with it, redemption, because she was a woman, and she had tweezers.

I flipped the light on and found that my sexist intuition was completely wrong. She did not have tweezers. In fact, I may have had more grooming products than she. I was desperate. I looked in the mirror and could not angle my head enough to even see inside my ear hole, much less see the plastic.

At last, salvation - I saw a bobby pin. I could get it with that, surely. I took it out of Betsy’s toiletry kit gleefully and began probing around in my ear again. But once again, I could feel it getting forced farther down with every swipe. This wasn’t working either.

I knew the hours were growing late, I could feel my adrenals starting to kick in, and I had to do something. I rifled through Jill’s bathroom drawers looking for a pair of tweezers and found none, but I did find scissors, which I used to cut off the nob-nose end of the bobby pin into a dagger. I held my weapon aloft in the painful bathroom light and drove the tip into my earhole.

I moved it back and forth until I found the plastic, which I could feel pressing against my eardrum, wincing, but I held on and pushed the plastic to one side until I could feel it stop moving, and wrenched it up.

POP! The dagger bobby pin had found purchase and had dragged the plastic up enough that a gap had appeared in my earhole. I was close. I dared another attempt and speared the plastic again. I yanked, and it came up, completely out!

Instant relief flooded my head! I could hear again! I looked down at the plastic earbud, which was caked like a donut in ear wax. I wiped it off, decided that my other ear must be in a similar state, and cleaned it out before slinking back into bed for a final few hours before my fate.
