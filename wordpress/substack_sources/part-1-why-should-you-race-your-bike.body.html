"Wow. That seems like some bullshit."

I know. But I can prove it.

The Modern Problem

Modern Life:

  1. Epistemological uncertainty.

  2. Few short, reality-based feedback loops.

Epistemological Uncertainty.

Epistemology is how you know what you know ( I promise I will never use that word again after the last sentence).

Because of the erosion of education, technology (the Internet and smartphones), and the nature of most jobs, not only does the average person not know how to determine what's most likely to be true, but they can create their own bubble to confirm their existing beliefs (Don't worry—this won't get political), all while spending most of their time engaged in abstract, 'knowledge' work in front of a screen.

The net result? Nothing feels real (Is sitting in front of a screen, emailing people, and attending meetings really 'work?'). Worse, the normal feedback mechanisms we rely on to check our own sanity are unreliable (Shit on the Internet), and there are few ways (and even fewer motivations) to test your feelings against reality.

When this is widespread, two things happen:

  1. Mass delusion.

  2. Life feels 'off.'

How We Test Reality

You might think you're about to be dragged into some half-baked philosophical exploration, but I will spare you that. Most people test reality like this:

You're out with your friends on a rooftop bar, and they turn to you and say, "I can fly."

Provided your night didn't start with LSD, your response will be somewhere along the lines of "The fuck?"

Why? You've never seen a person fly.

Your friend insists they can, and before you can stop them, they jump (I know this is a little dark, but stay with me). The result?

Yep - they died. Sadly, they were wrong - they can't fly.

K - while the scientific process wasn't on your mind that night, your friend experimented with reality and obtained results, albeit pretty gruesome results.

What's my point here? Unlike in most people's lives, their theory, their mental model of what's true or not, was testable and falsifiable.

But for most of the rest of their lives, there's no gravity; there's no ground. Anything they think in their heads can leap off a building, and they freefall, not forever, but until they're caught by information (thanks, Internet) that confirms their prior that they can fly.

Flying sounds kind of fun, doesn't it? No matter what you believe, you can find something to confirm it, and while holding that belief can and will harm you eventually, the future feedback loop is too long to feel relevant.

With that in mind, let's test another theory.  

"I Can Win the Tour De France"

Imagine you're sure you can win the Tour. The last few winners seem like washed-up scrubs that couldn't hold your wheel on the local group ride for a second.

That's nuts! Who knew you were so talented? That's like saying you could fly!

I wonder if we can test this or if it's just another mental jump off a building with no ground beneath it.

Good news! We can test the belief against reality!

To win the Tour de France, you need to be able to push roughly 6.6 w/kg for 30-40 minutes three weeks deep in a grand tour after burning about 5,000 calories 4-5 hours deep in the stage.

Given that most people lack the attention span or time, let's skip simulating racing for three weeks and riding for five hours and just jump straight into holding 6.6 w/kg for 40 minutes.

Execution is simple:

  1. Get on your bike with a power meter.

  2. Warm-up.

  3. Assuming you weigh roughly 155 lbs, hold 465 watts for 40 minutes.

  4. Finish the test and upload your file for analysis.

Ah, I see your file came in! Let me analyze it quick. The result:

It looks like you held 465 watts for two minutes.

Hmmmm. Something doesn't seem right here. It must have been a fluke. Maybe your power meter was wrong.

I mean, you were 100% certain you could dust those no-talent has-beens, but unless there was some gross recording error, the math makes it look like you couldn't even hold their wheel for two minutes fresh after they had been racing for three weeks and already burned 5000 calories.

Wild.

☝🏻 That's the Disease

The problem is that the average person is walking around thinking they can win the Tour, and there is nothing to check that delusion.

Sure, they don't think they could win the Tour, but they think they could be famous, rich, etc., or minimally 'exceptional' if they just had the chance or 'really cared.'

I'm not making this shit up - after the Olympics, someone surveyed the public, and 27% of people think they could get good enough to compete after only six months of training.

That's right - couch to medal contender in six months.

What's the Harm in that?

It makes you a terrible person.

If few things you believe are checked by reality and often nurtured by the Internet, it makes you:

  • Grandiose - "I'm amazing."

  • Entitled - "Life owes me."

  • Arrogant - "That's easy"

  • Impatient - "It doesn't take long"

  • Complacent - "I can start later."

  • Guttless - "I don't need to put myself out there."

Doing Anything is Hard

Testing the shit in your head always involves doing something, and doing things, ANYTHING, is hard and often humiliating.

Try this: the next time you have a strong emotion, write about it. Grab some paper and a pencil and get to the bottom of it. Write 250-750 words, no interruptions, and describe what you're feeling and why you think you're feeling it. Give yourself 10 minutes, tops.

How did it go? I bet that was hard. In fact, unless you're regularly in the habit of writing, I bet you tripped over yourself trying to express your emotions.

"I'm bad at writing, though. That's not a good example."

Writing is thinking. You're telling me you struggled to produce an articulate summary of your own emotions when you are indisputably not only the best person in the world qualified to do so (they're YOUR emotions), but before trying to write them down, you were so sure you understood how you felt perfectly?

If that's not a cold shower for you, I'm not sure you're paying attention. The same sort of person who thinks they can win the Tour and compete at the Olympics can't even easily describe what they should be an authority on - themselves.

Maybe you want me to concede that writing is a specialized skill, and that example is a cheap shot. Fine - granted.

Pick something else you're sure you're good at then—maybe cooking isn't that hard, or you could launch a business or build a fence.

Fine! Test it - do it then.

I guarantee that when your theory against reality:

  1. The chicken will burn.

  2. The business will struggle.

  3. The fence will be crooked.
