Several days ago, before you were awake, a Ram 1500 Hemi carrying my entire life roared toward Colorado in the early Wisconsin chill.

My wife and I were the only ones awake. Instead of sitting in silence like you do when you're more than five years deep into marriage during a road trip, she suspiciously started asking me questions, which she usually only does when she wants something from me, but a request never came.

Instead, she kept asking me personal questions, even asking if I had any rants I'd like to share. Why she would ever ask me that is beyond me, and about 20 seconds into my first diatribe, I could tell she regretted it.

I won't get into the best ones because they're hurtful, unfounded, and should be dismissed as a consequence of spending 12 days in America's Dairyland overdosing on cheese and mispronouncing vowels. Amid my ranting, a theme did develop, though:

I have had it up to fucking HERE with people claiming something is great with zero evidence, just feels.

For example, an athlete said a not-quite-true (but scarily close) thing: "I started spending time in the Sauna lately, and my FTP has increased by 40 watts."

How the fuck do you know that? How long have you been using the Sauna? For how long and how hot is each session? What is your pre and post-hydration protocol? Also, are you (and how are you) measuring your FTP before and after?

If pressed on any of these questions, 9/10 athletes will not have a good answer and will settle on something like, "I know what I know," and also get pissed at me for doubting them.

Empirical-free claims aren't just found in training interventions. They're also very common in the working world, especially in the sector of the economy that employs millions of laptop warriors with dubious titles who spend their working hours attending meetings, sending a few emails, and BSing with coworkers while draining the lunch room kegger.

During their performance reviews, their boss will say something positive but ultimately baseless and harmful, like, "You did a great job, and you're getting a raise."

How? How did they do a great job? Because the boss said so? What criterion were they using to access the employee? None?! Big surprise there!

Even if we pretend they had criteria to assess their employees, what are the chances that the criteria can be evaluated objectively and aren't merely serving as cover for the boss to compensate the employee however they see fit? Worse, when the boss made the role, were they even able to clearly demonstrate how that employee contributes to the company's revenue with black-and-white metrics so both could be satisfied by their effort?

The chances are nope. The metrics aren't clear. The working world runs mostly on vibes and politics, especially in the laptop class.

If you observed me in both situations, listening to the boss and the athlete, I'd appear calm, but if you looked me in the eyes, you'd immediately see that I was using every drop of willpower I had not to scream 'HOW DO YOU KNOW??????' while landing as many haymakers as possible before I got dragged away.

The common issue with athletes and bosses is that they don't 'know' what they think they know, mainly because they're not measuring anything. Even if they were, they did not measure what matters; even if they were, it would still be a stretch to claim causality confidently.

Whatever your claim is, my challenge is this: what can you point to outside your subjectivity that reasonably demonstrates that when you do X, you're getting Y, or, if the casualty is too high a standard, that when you do X, you generally see Y vary in a predictable way that's unlikely to be a random variation?

If you're not pointing at anything, you're not alone. Hardly anyone bothers.

Whether the claim is about training or work or anything else, I normally hear people making emotionally charged, zeitgeist-humping claims about some device, protocol, or habit that they're 100% sure is producing X outcome without a shred of evidence that relies entirely on vibes, which brings me to a question I've been avoiding because it's hard to answer: So what???

So what if people don't actually know what they know if their claim falls apart under scrutiny's sidelong glance?

If this is how you feel, you have a point. A nameless, not very well-liked internet provocateur is famous for saying, 'Facts don't care about your feelings,' which is clever and seemingly unassailable until, a few years later, someone pointed out 'Facts might not care about feelings, but feelings also don't care about facts.'

On balance, the comeback to the troll's initial banger reveals more about human nature than just about any sentence ever written. Feelings really don't care about facts - that's humans for you. I won't insult you by pointing out historical examples demonstrating this.

I raise that point because it explains why most people don't bother to gather evidence to support their knowledge - because that's what people do! They develop feelings about things and, depending on what they are, will ignore any contrary evidence, especially when it threatens their membership in a social group. For example, though I consider snowshoeing to be invented by the devil and partially responsible for the holocaust, I will snowshoe with my relatives on Christmas break while they're alive to keep them happy.

If you're like me, that's a sobering reality to accept, but necessary nonetheless if you want to stay sane. You gotta let people vibe, unfortunately, and for most, nothing you say is going to change how they feel. All you can do is smile and nod.

HOWEVER!!!!!!!!!!

Instead of letting other people's fact-free bullshit get in your head or get you down, realize that it's an opportunity for you to do what almost no one else does - have evidence to support what you believe!!

As stupid as this sounds, if you vow to quit being the mayor of vibe town and instead assess the success of your efforts with objective data, well, you might find yourself becoming wildly more effective than your peers.

Why?

To quote a mostly wrong libertarian, "You can deny reality, but you can't escape the consequences of denying reality."

And THAT is why you should resist letting your feelings override your facts. The efficiency of your actions does not, in most cases, depend on how you feel about them subjectively. I mean, when I'm six Miller Lites deep as I drive my kids to Sunday School, I'm CERTAIN I'm going to win Unbound 200 this year—my vibes are very clear on this - but then tomorrow comes, and the numbers I see when I pedal don't seem to line up with my pilsner inspired confidence.

My point is this: Most people are idiots most of the time, and they are more invested in feeling OK than figuring out what's true. If you want to kick ass in 2025, it's probably as simple as answering, even badly, a simple question -How do you know? - with ANY data besides woo-woo vibey BS.
