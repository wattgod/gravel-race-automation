Linings of Silver

They don't tell you this, but, in the end, the final result of personal striving is despair.

Why?

Because, even in the best-case scenario, like when you achieve your goal, the feelings you get don't last. No matter what you accomplish, eventually, no one gives a shit - not even you.

When you realize that everything you've tried to win for yourself ultimately fades away to irrelevance, even the least self-aware among us feels a chill that even a golden 50th Anniversary Miller Lite can't save you from.

Solution? Way-uhl:

  1. Pursue new goals and ignore that no matter what you achieve for yourself, it will never heal the deadness inside your self-addicted heart.

  2. Give to other people.

What am I trying to get at? Am I insinuating that I'm some kind of gravel preacher who had the spiritual wherewithal to lightly let go of months and many 401k withdrawals all to achieve a head-turning result at Unbound 200 that would win me middle-aged female groupies and a 10% lifetime discount with Silica in favor of showing up for my athletes for once at the aid stations, genuinely enjoy cleaning their jock straps and have the emotional bandwidth for sloppy bear hugs when they finished?

No. Are you kidding me? I wanted to have a ripper, bruh.

But, I did a U-turn away from the course and made it back to our Airbnb before the Unbound support crew left and rolled out with them to the aid stations, and I have to say, I...enjoyed myself. It was neat being there for my athletes (and even randos) in varying degrees of hell, diagnosing their ills like an expert mechanic, plying them with pickle juice, deli meats, chain lube, or even unsolicited questions of whether they'd ever heard of a man named Jesus, and then shoved them (with maybe a too eager hand) back into the raw, boiling prairie.

And, shockingly, they not only finished, but they finished well, though never, of course, to their satisfaction, which is a good thing because if they were, I'd quickly be unemployed.

It was something, though, those last moments sitting in a camp chair with my last athlete to finish amidst the throb of the Emporia downtown, barely saying a goddamn thing, just listening to their triumph, which drowned out the dull ache of my ribs and the inner voice that whispers, 'Me, me, me.'
