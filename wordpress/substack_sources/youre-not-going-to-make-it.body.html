While it might be hard for the average suburban American dweller who has spent their entire life surrounded by slight variations of the same uninspired, cheaply sourced housing slop their entire lives, architecture like this 100% exists - believe me, I’ve asked Chat GPT on at least five separate occasions and it comes up with the same answer every time so it must be true. This unlikely building is a church, called the Cologne Cathedral, and is waiting for you to see in Germany if you have the means.

Now, what’s really stunning about the Cologne Cathedral, counterintuitively, isn’t its architectural style. That’s obvious. I’d wager that even if you grew up around it with a view of it through your kitchen window, you would struggle for years to believe what you were seeing, much like how when I ride in Colorado and look up and see the mountains I struggle to rid myself of a somewhat dumbfounded expression, having been born in bred in a place (Wisconsin) where a a modest 600 feet of gain qualifies as a ‘mountain’.

Anyway, what’s the deal with that thing?

Construction on the Cologne Cathedral began in 1248. The builders aspired to build the grandest Gothic Cathedral in Europe - taller, ornate, more architectually unlikely than anything in the world. Why? Because God.

Saying things is easy, doing things is hard, and it quickly became apparent that the resources, labor, and technicality of implementing the original vision for the Cathedral was greater than the available funds (and will) to do so, so the building stopped in 1473, after more than 200 years of constructions.

I’m going to pause us here. Let’s reflect that a contruction project that lasts 200 years has no modern parallel. Even the Saudis with their vast, endless oil wealth, don’t seriously fund projects that take more than a decade to build (no, that weird ‘Line’ project in the desert is never going to happen I’ve got a line in Vegas going go ahead bet against me). But the Cologne cathedral? Before constructions slowed, it had carried on for TWO HUNDRED YEARS. What that means is that the original architects weren’t just long dead, but their grand children were most likely long dead in the grave. The people carrying on this vision were the great, great, great, great, great, great, great (6-8 ‘greats’) grandchildren of the Cathedral.

This is remarkable. Consider that you won’t listen to one of your grandparents stories and think they’re hopelessly out of touch and barely worth looking at but the people working on the Cologne Cathedral spent a better part of their resources and free time for at least six generations working on a building. And remember, mind you, that the people working on this Cathedral weren’t even remotely materially secure in the way we are now - this was the Middle Ages - where famines, societal unrest, and other distruptive forces were a frequent and expected part of life. I mean, if you were super stressed about getting food on the table or not getting murdered would you spend your free time lending a hand to building the fever dream of some addled priest? No one, at least in the Western world, believes in anything that much anymore (no, not even the Mormons).

While that’s noteworthy, what’s even more stunning is that even after constructions stopped (with only one tower completed and the other tower left half finished with a crane on top of it), geopolitical forces and German cultural winds shifted and construction renewed in 1842, following the original 13th century plans, which somehow survived for 500 years. The Cologne Cathedral was completed in 1880, 632 years after it began.

The Seven Year Itch

After meeting, courting, marrying, and having children with the love of your life, seven years into your marriage you’re going to feel an inexplicable urge to burn it all down and run off with the Zoomer secretary with a nose ring and an attitude. The sudden, ruinous urge to ruin your life after novelty wears off in your long term relationship isn’t special, it’s utterly banal and has a name - The Seven Year Itch (after a meh film featuring Marilyn Monroe in 1955).

I’m bringing this up because you’ll remember this concept, but not what I’m about to say.

Seven years is also roughly the time frame most athletes require to maximize their physiology, acquire and automate key habits, inculcate key technical and tactical skills, while having (and processing) enough experiences that they’re mentally sharp, all of which is required to perform at or near your athletic potential. How many athletes get to that point? Roughly the same number of people that stay married for 50 years (~10%).

This should encourage you, because it means that 90% of athletes don’t sacrifice their family, career, and friendships just so to get good at cardio.

It should, however, at least make you curious as to why the attrition rate in the athletic process is so high. What exactly is going on that causes so many people to drop out long before they can drop everyone?

Well, despite lacking any data in the slightest to back up my claims, I have the answer. Let’s break it down:

The Process is All There Is

Those that make it fall in love with the process. That’s it - it’s not that deep.

Now, the problem with me saying that is that you think you know what I mean when I say ‘fall in love with the process’. You don’t. You have no idea what it looks like.

Remember my last post about my teammate Cole Tamburri, and how the secret was ‘taking care of yourself’ for a long period of time? Well, one small, but crucial detail I neglected to mention about Cole taking care of himself isn’t just that he takes care of himself, but that he enjoys taking care of himself.

Confusing? K:

When Cole takes care of himself - makes his chicken and rice, puts on his compression socks, keeps Z2 easy, ignores his girlfriend (who he should propose to don’t be an idiot you’ll never find a girl like her again) to get more TSS, etc - he doesn’t do so like a robot, he does so with pure, childlike glee.

Why is this the important?

Because every one of those things, eating chicken and rice, putting on compression socks, keeping Z2 easy, ignoring his girlfriend aren’t inherently enjoyable. In fact, most people hate doing these things, and only do so because they see them as necessary evils to reach their potential. Whether you hate it or love it depend entirely on your perspective; two people could engage in the exact same actions and yet those actions elicit opposite reactions.

And, obviously, who is more likely to remain in the sport long enough to reach (or at least get close) to their potential - the person who loves the process, or the person who hates the process.

Correct.

Look, I’m sure I’m not alone in getting tired of imagining Cole Tamburri as some sort of role model, but, as much as this might cut him deeply, I think there are better examples of athletes out there that take excruciating pleasure out of the process in ways that the average person would find genuinely confusing. The person I’m thinking is more of a legend than a person - his name is Freshly Dialed.

Freshly Dialed was my teammate and roommate back in 2013-2014. Like me, he was an aspiring cyclist, but unlike me, he was a successful one because while I wanted to go out and dance up on my future wife nine Miller Lite’s deep on a Tuesday, he was at home, watching cycling highlights, foam rolling on the living room floor while he ate a perfectly portioned ‘Big Fucking Salad” while performing diaphragmatic breathing, and all of this after carefully washing his entire bike and relubbing the drive train after a hard workout where a rode through one puddle.

While I envied Freshly Dialed, no part of me had any chance of even badly replicating his meticulous, unerring process genius simply because everything I tolerated doing, he loved. Like, he got the nickname Freshly Dialed because he liked making his bike ‘Fresh’, and he was ‘Dialed’ about every detail of the bike racing lifestyle. The Process wasn’t a lifestyle framework he chafed against, it was something he lived for; for him, it was harder not to do all the little (and bike) process details perfectly, with pleasure, than it was to do it.

I’ll never forget during the 2013 offseason my roommate and I dragged him out to go out on the town, hoping to get properly ‘pissed up’ a la Geraint Thomas, only for his attitude to absolutely torpedo the vibes because he was so terrifically depressed that he had to take a sip of alcohol which would slightly compromise his super important 90 minute Endurance ride the next day in the middle of October. The picture I somehow remember to take is a perfect visual encapsulation of Freshly Dialed that I should frame and put about my hearth.

Saturday Breakfast

Years after we lived together, my phone would vibrate obnoxiously on Saturday Mornings as Freshly Dialed sent me Russian Novel level details of his breakfast that morning, including what he had chose to make, the exact recipe, how it was going currently, how much of it he was going to make, whether he should invite some girl over to taste test it, and then finish the entire unreciprocated avalanche of text messages with grainy, faintly pornographic photographs of syrup dripping over pancakes at non-traditional angles.

Why would he do this?

Because Saturday, if you’re an aspiring professional cyclist with a full time job, is your long ride day - training for four to six hours, and maybe more if time allows. On those days, you fuel big before, during, and after, and Freshly Dialed took singular joy in maximizing the possible pleasure one could derive from making breakfast. It wasn’t just breakfast to him, it was a celebration of his lifestyle, what he lived for during the week - the chance to make (and burn) enormous amounts of calories in pursuit of his potential.

Years later, long after Freshly’s cycling trajectory easily blew past my own and I spent long times in the mirror wondering whether I was worthy of the chamois I put on, it hit me that the reason I wasn’t better wasn’t because I didn’t train hard, it was because, in a sense, I just didn’t enjoy Saturday Breakfast that much.

If you want to make it, if you want to see what you can do, you better be dreaming about hot cakes early on Saturday morning, not what you hope it feels like to stand on the top step of the podium.
